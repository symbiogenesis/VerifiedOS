# Proof reuse inventory: operating systems, storage, composition and scheduling

This is a source inventory, not a claim that the external theorems prove VerifiedOS. The research snapshot is 2026-09-10, except for SYS-PROSA, SYS-REFINEDPROSA, SYS-LAWYER, SYS-WAITFREE, SYS-LILO, SYS-PARCAS and every record under *Space, allocation and reclamation* and *Resource-sharing and ghost-state logics*, whose sources and licences are read on 2026-09-13. The local comparison baseline is `40c48830e11d2af3807d2431f7fc42ec9ce83bf3`. The [requirements register](../../requirements-register.md), [crown jewels](../crown-jewels.md), [implementation checklist](../../implementation/implementation-checklist.md) and [third-party record](../../../THIRD-PARTY.md) determine the local obligations and incorporation policy.

**Evidence labels:** *established reference* means a published mechanized development with identifiable source; *qualified reference* retains a material completeness, availability or metatheory limitation; *paper reference* supplies an argument rather than a checked term. None of the external developments below is replayed in this research lane. A published or author-reported successful check is distinct from a local replay, and a permissive license is distinct from semantic compatibility. Branch links explicitly identified as discovery pointers are not immutable editions.

The landed kernel, schedule, journal and composition artifacts remain useful comparison targets. [EndpointIPC.v](../../../proofs/EndpointIPC.v), [PartitionContext.v](../../../proofs/PartitionContext.v), [CyclicExecutive.v](../../../proofs/CyclicExecutive.v), [JournalIndex.v](../../../proofs/JournalIndex.v), [ApexTheorem.v](../../../proofs/ApexTheorem.v), [SeamWitnesses.v](../../../proofs/SeamWitnesses.v), [RingContract.v](../../../proofs/RingContract.v) and [CopyRingService.v](../../../proofs/CopyRingService.v) contain local statements, executable definitions or bounded witnesses. Their existence does not turn an external model's implementation theorem into a local refinement proof.

## Kernel and information-flow foundations

### SYS-SEL4: seL4 functional correctness and information-flow enforcement

**Established reference; Isabelle/HOL; conceptual reuse only for kernel-specific material.** Gerwin Klein and colleagues, *seL4: Formal Verification of an OS Kernel*, SOSP 2009; Toby Murray and colleagues, *seL4: From General Purpose to a Proof of Information Flow Enforcement*, IEEE S&P 2013. The [official proof overview](https://sel4.systems/Verification/proofs.html) distinguishes architecture and configuration coverage, and the [assumption account](https://sel4.systems/Verification/assumptions.html) states the external boundary. Source edition: [`l4v` c18eef3c556573e0bfedfeb88bdb7525cda051a1](https://github.com/seL4/l4v/tree/c18eef3c556573e0bfedfeb88bdb7525cda051a1); inspect `proof/refine`, `proof/crefine`, and [`proof/infoflow/Noninterference.thy`](https://github.com/seL4/l4v/blob/c18eef3c556573e0bfedfeb88bdb7525cda051a1/proof/infoflow/Noninterference.thy). Release-specific manifests, not a generic seL4 version, select the Isabelle environment and supported kernel configuration.

The refinement and unwinding architecture directly informs R-07-001, R-08-022 and `CJ-KERNEL`/`CJ-NI`, including the already landed M4.2a/M4.2b definitions and the deferred C refinement. It does not establish the project's purecap semantics, multikernel composition, robust release or physical timing isolation. The [source header](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/seL4/l4v/c18eef3c556573e0bfedfeb88bdb7525cda051a1/proof/infoflow/Noninterference.thy) names Data61/CSIRO and `GPL-2.0-only`; the [license map](https://github.com/seL4/l4v/blob/c18eef3c556573e0bfedfeb88bdb7525cda051a1/LICENSE.md) explicitly distinguishes kernel proofs from BSD libraries. Preserve the existing M4.1a/M4.1b decision: no translation or copying of the GPL kernel proof into this project's Apache proof tree. General l4v libraries require their own file-level reading.

### SYS-CERTIKOS: mCertiKOS security and concurrent abstraction layers

**Established reference; legacy Coq; licensing blocks direct reuse.** David Costanzo, Zhong Shao and Ronghui Gu, *End-to-End Verification of Information-Flow Security for C and Assembly Programs*, PLDI 2016, supplies the [security artifact](https://flint.cs.yale.edu/flint/publications/pldi16-security/). Its documented entry point is `mcertikos/security/Security.v`; the artifact requires Coq 8.4pl4. Ronghui Gu and colleagues' [OSDI 2016 CertiKOS](https://flint.cs.yale.edu/certikos/publications/certikos.html) and the [CCAL development](https://flint.cs.yale.edu/certikos/publications/ccal.html) extend the layered method to concurrent kernels and C/assembly linking.

Use the contextual-refinement architecture for `CJ-KERNEL`, R-07-001, R-08-022 and the apex's semantic joins. Do not infer that a security result for the sequential restricted configuration is a confidentiality result for every concurrent CertiKOS configuration. Local CHERI capabilities, no allocator/page tables, endpoint policy and declassification remain different obligations. The [published proof-file notice](https://www.cs.yale.edu/flint/certikos/publications/device/mcertikos/mcertikos.mm.PTOpGenSpec.html) names the FLINT Group, Yale University, and the Yale University Non-Commercial License Agreement. The archive also contains CompCert components with their own terms. No permissive grant for the security or concurrent-kernel artifact is established here; these are paper/design references for this integration, not vendorable proof sources.

### SYS-SIFUM: compositional noninterference with rely/guarantee assumptions

**Established reference; Isabelle/HOL; permissive but requires a new Rocq formalization.** Sylvia Grewe, Heiko Mantel and Daniel Schoepe, [*A Formalization of Assumptions and Guarantees for Compositional Noninterference*](https://isa-afp.org/entries/SIFUM_Type_Systems.html), AFP 2014, mechanizes the underlying concurrent-security method. The entry supplies an Isabelle2025-2 archive dated 2026-02-06 as an identifiable replay edition. Read `Security.thy`, [`Compositionality.thy`](https://isa-afp.org/thys/SIFUM_Type_Systems/Compositionality.html) and `TypeSystem.thy` together: the central result composes individually secure threads only under the specified mode-use and environment assumptions.

It is a precise reference for R-08-022's multikernel composition and the abstract interfaces in `ApexTheorem.v`, including review of already authored seams. Its shared-variable language and observations do not implement CHERI-C, dynamic user grants or the Sail timing model. The entry marks its source BSD; the actual [AFP license](https://isa-afp.org/LICENSE) is the three-clause BSD form, retaining author notices and forbidding implied endorsement. Ported proof scripts cannot be loaded by Rocq; a compatible original Rocq theorem over the local transition systems must be proved.

### SYS-DECLASS: robust declassification and checked release-policy soundness

**Two different evidence levels; no drop-in theorem.** Steve Zdancewic and Andrew C. It is relevant to R-08-025's quantification over compromised-component strategies. This search does not establish a licensed mechanization of that paper's precise theorem.

Toby Murray, Mukesh Tiwari, Gidon Ernst and David Naumann's [*Assume but Verify: Deductive Verification of Leaked Information in Concurrent Applications* artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/10037769), CCS 2023, version 1, supplies Isabelle/HOL soundness proofs and concurrent case studies for expressive declassification policies. It is a qualified mechanized reference for authoring `CJ-NI` and reviewing the consent/declassification seam, not evidence that its policy notion equals R-08-025. The public record identifies `CCS2023-Artifact.zip`; this lane does not establish the archive's exact license, prover edition or a theorem-to-file correspondence. Do not redistribute its source until these are read. Neither source decides the project's consent-record freshness, recipient authority or release bound.

## Crash consistency, indexing and filesystem confidentiality

### SYS-PERENNIAL: crash weakest preconditions and recovery reasoning

**Established reference; Rocq/Iris; best framework-reuse candidate.** Tej Chajed, Joseph Tassarotti, M. The [maintained repository](https://github.com/mit-pdos/perennial) separates the original `sosp2019` artifact from later frameworks. The edition examined here is [`3fe5d0a957a95c27cff204e729a5e96a3e3217ad`](https://github.com/mit-pdos/perennial/tree/3fe5d0a957a95c27cff204e729a5e96a3e3217ad). [`src/program_logic/crash_weakestpre.v`](https://github.com/mit-pdos/perennial/blob/3fe5d0a957a95c27cff204e729a5e96a3e3217ad/src/program_logic/crash_weakestpre.v) contains `wpc_frame`, `wpc_bind`, `wpc_crash_mono` and crash-frame rules. These are concrete reusable proof infrastructure for R-10-002, R-16-003 and R-18-026, including the framework owed behind landed `JournalIndex.v`. They require an instantiated crash language, state interpretation and recovery specification; they do not select the currently unsettled local recovery policy. The [MIT license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mit-pdos/perennial/3fe5d0a957a95c27cff204e729a5e96a3e3217ad/LICENSE) carries a 2021 copyright line without a named holder; preserve that notice verbatim and attribute the paper authors separately. Compatible infrastructure is available, but introducing it without a local crash-language consumer would not discharge a proof.

### SYS-REFFS: dynamically layered definite releases and MoLi

**Qualified reference; Coq object proofs with paper-only framework soundness.** Mo Zou, Dong Du, Mingkai Dong and Haibo Chen, [*Using Dynamically Layered Definite Releases for Verifying the RefFS File System*](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi24-zou.pdf), OSDI 2024. The [author project page](https://ipads.se.sjtu.edu.cn/pub/projects/reffs) links the paper and a metatheory draft. It supplies a strong specification method for R-10-006's deadlock/livelock obligation and the local L2/liveness work.

**The paper explicitly excludes crash safety.** Its contribution statement says MoLi's soundness is proved on paper and a Coq metatheory is future work. Its limitations describe RefFS as an in-memory filesystem, with fair scheduling and sequentially consistent hardware assumptions, and no crash, interrupt or exception reasoning. RefFS therefore supplies no combined concurrency, crash-safety and liveness theorem; the checklist keeps crash reasoning and its composition as separate work. Neither termination alone nor fairness gives a finite WCET bound. This survey finds no identifiable public Coq source edition with a usable license, prover version and theorem-file index; this is an availability gap, not evidence that none exists. The unrelated `sbu-fsl/RefFS` is a C++ reference filesystem for FAST 2024 Metis and is not this artifact. No source incorporation is justified by the wiki's own CC-BY-SA license.

### SYS-REFINEDPROSA: a response-time bound joined to the C scheduler that earns it

**Qualified recent reference; Rocq, Prosa over RefinedC; the nearest published statement-to-implementation join.** Kimaya Bedarkar, Laila Elbeheiry, Michael Sammler, Lennard Gäher, Björn Brandenburg, Derek Dreyer and Deepak Garg, [*RefinedProsa: Connecting Response-Time Analysis with C Verification for Interrupt-Free Schedulers*](https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2025-pldi-refinedprosa.pdf), Proc. ACM Program. Lang. 9, PLDI, Article 150, June 2025, [doi 10.1145/3729249](https://doi.org/10.1145/3729249). The [archived artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/15252399) is the only source form located: the project landing page links no repository beyond the deposit, and two deposit identities circulate, `10.5281/zenodo.15185870` cited by the paper and `10.5281/zenodo.15252399` linked by the project, so an edition must be named rather than assumed.

For Roessl, a fixed-priority non-preemptive interrupt-free scheduler in C, RefinedC proves the code emits only traces of basic actions satisfying a scheduler-protocol state-transition invariant; that trace, with assumed per-action worst-case times and an arrival sequence, becomes a Prosa-compatible schedule proved to satisfy the validity constraints; the abstract response-time framework then yields a machine-checked bound for every job, accounting for scheduling overheads. This is the concrete shape of what the landed M4.2a [CyclicExecutive.v](../../../proofs/CyclicExecutive.v) statement still owes an implementation under R-11-006/R-11-009 and M4.4, and it is a better comparison for that join than SYS-RTCERTIKOS, whose Coq archive this lane cannot identify. The theorem is conditional on parameters rather than results: every basic action's and client callback's worst-case time is assumed, the arrival sequence is assumed to respect its curve, the surrounding system is assumed to implement the system calls as specified, and `read()` is axiomatized in the underlying C semantics. Nothing is proved about machine code, the compiler, the architecture or caches, and timing is deliberately not reasoned about at the C source level. Interrupts, device interference and instruction timing are outside the theorem, and R-11-006 selects an interval-arithmetic check over a table-only executive rather than a response-time analysis, so the transferable part is the trace-to-schedule join and not the analysis it feeds.

## Reuse disposition and outstanding searches

No system-level source in this lane is a drop-in proof for an existing local obligation. The nearest compatible materials are Perennial's generic crash-logic lemmas, GoTxn's refinement architecture, the complete static-policy DiskSec edition once selected, Verdi's reusable distributed semantics, Lawyer's liveness infrastructure, and RefinedProsa's trace-to-schedule join. Each needs a real local semantic consumer and an assumption comparison before import. Replacing an authored local witness with an external theorem over a different state space would reduce clarity rather than add assurance.

SYS-REFINEDPROSA supplies the nearest published mechanized join from a schedule theorem to the C scheduler that earns it, for one interrupt-free fixed-priority scheduler whose worst-case action times are assumed parameters; it does not cover the table-only executive, interrupts or device interference. SYS-MEMMGR supplies the nearest published specification under which allocation success is a consequence of declared resources, for a sequential runtime allocator under reciprocal terms. What none of the space records reaches is the local subject: every located development specifies an allocator or a collector running inside the program, where [MemoryPlan.v](../../../proofs/MemoryPlan.v) and [StaticMemoryLaminar.v](../../../proofs/StaticMemoryLaminar.v) state a whole-program placement whose free points are fixed at compile time. So the placement side is unmatched, and the reclamation side is matched only by algorithms resting on compare-and-swap and retry that R-15-026, R-15-030 and R-15-242 exclude from this machine.

The optimization question that arrives with those sources is already decided, and in two directions. An untrusted offline search proposing a placement that the existing exact checks re-validate is admissible, is free to be as aggressive as it likes, and is what [the placement search](../../implementation/placement-search.md) and the scaling experiments already are: R-05-066 states that pattern, and it needs no certificate-checking import to stand. Adopting a certified constraint-programming or pseudo-Boolean optimization system as a shipped checker is the different act, and it is refused: it mints a checker, several of the candidates check in Lean or HOL4 rather than Rocq and fail R-05-020's first condition outright, none of them retires an interim, and where the yield is only a smaller span over a plan that is already sound, R-05-105's standing no-tightening rule refuses it on its own terms, R-05-104 having deleted the path-enumeration machinery rather than retargeted it and R-05-106 generalizing that deletion from estimates to tools. One argument is not already refused, and it is recorded here as an open question and not as a recommendation: a checked infeasibility certificate would turn a search that found no placement into a proof that no legal placement exists, which is a yield other than tightness. It still has to clear R-05-020's three conditions, and the [foundation disposition](foundations.md#certified-search-and-the-registers-standing-refusals) distinguishes the extracted Rocq checkers from SMTCoq's reflective route. The portable [LRAT experiment](../../implementation/static-memory/certificates.md) uses a separately licensed Isabelle-LLVM artifact only as evidence-producing machinery under R-05-011b; no external verdict grounds an admitted claim or completes Q27a.

The unresolved search areas are precise: the correct public RefFS/MoLi Coq release and its license; a complete historical SFSCQ static-permission edition; the license/toolchain and exact theorem in the CCS 2023 release-policy artifact; RT-CertiKOS source and licensing; the redistribution terms of the Iron development, which no reachable host would serve to this lane, and of the Lilo and Mixtris mechanizations, which exist only inside archives this lane did not open; a rights grant for the Nextgen mechanization, whose repository states none; an already mechanized composition of robust declassification, CHERI-C and multikernel timing; and a local-compatible TDM-NoC noninterference theorem. Finding a paper mentioning these mechanisms does not close `CJ-NI`, `CJ-ISOL`, `CJ-WITNESS` or the apex. These gaps remain recorded instead of being promoted to proof availability claims.
