# Compiler, extraction, checker and ownership proof precedents

This inventory records upstream proof artifacts examined between 2026-09-10 and 2026-09-13, with the bounded CIC literature follow-up dated separately below. A mature result can be a strong reference while failing this project's admission conditions. The entries distinguish a theorem in its original semantics, a reusable proof component, and a promising project whose required theorem is absent. No entry discharges a VerifiedOS requirement merely by association.

The integration baseline is the [implementation checklist](../../implementation/implementation-checklist.md): M1.1/M1.1a/M1.1b and M1.2a are completed compiler investigations, M1.6 is the completed lowering investigation, M6.1a supplies the supervision-tree reference, and M6.2a supplies the admission-path statement artifact. The corresponding target refinements and hardening proofs remain separate work. [AdmissionPath.v](../../../proofs/AdmissionPath.v) expressly distinguishes its closed statement artifacts from an implemented, verified checker; [VerifiedExit.v](../../../tools/bedrock2-lowering/VerifiedExit.v) expressly distinguishes its RV32I measurement from the descriptor derivation over 64-bit words. Those distinctions apply to completed work throughout this inventory.

Source inspection, upstream publications and license inspection support the assessments below. These upstream developments are not rebuilt in this survey, and their complete transitive assumption sets are not certified here. Before importing any result into the standalone Rocq 9.2.0 gate, instantiate its actual premises, establish its relation to the frozen Sail semantics, and run the project's assumption audit under R-05-164. A theorem with a parameterized premise is not automatically an axiom-free proof of that premise. The [third-party ledger](../../../THIRD-PARTY.md) owns the repository's existing containment and incorporation decisions.

## Compiler correctness and resource planning

### CompCert: semantic preservation and allocation validation

**Authority and artifact.** Xavier Leroy and the CompCert contributors; [the official project](https://compcert.org/) and [source release v3.18](https://github.com/AbsInt/CompCert/tree/14d616046360a0b2611ebdfc2f98368af402e1f7). The Rocq declarations `transf_c_program_correct` and `separate_transf_c_program_correct` are in [driver/Compiler.v](https://github.com/AbsInt/CompCert/blob/v3.18/driver/Compiler.v). Register allocation has its own `transf_program_correct` in [backend/Allocproof.v](https://github.com/AbsInt/CompCert/blob/v3.18/backend/Allocproof.v), over the transformation specified in `backend/Allocation.v`.

**Use and limits.** This is a mature reference for CJ-COMPCERT, R-05-023, R-18-014, M1.2 and the hardening program. Allocation validation is useful prior art for R-08-014 and [MemoryPlan.v](../../../proofs/MemoryPlan.v): an untrusted producer proposes a resource assignment and a proved validator checks the semantic obligations. Its register and spill-slot simulation does not establish whole-program weighted offline placement, peak-liveness optimality, capability representability, initialization, erasure or reuse-barrier safety. It also does not give CHERI robust preservation or binary constant time. The project already installs CompCert 3.18 in its separate oracle switch, not its standalone proof gate.

**License and disposition.** The actual [v3.18 LICENSE](https://github.com/AbsInt/CompCert/blob/v3.18/LICENSE) places these backend and driver proofs under the INRIA Non-Commercial License Agreement; they are outside the listed LGPL dual-licensed subset. The grant limits use to education, research and evaluation and carries publication credit and same-terms redistribution conditions. Preserve M1.1a's contained route. No source copied.

**Related academic lead.** Sandrine Blazy, Benoît Robillard and Andrew W. Appel, *Formal Verification of Coalescing Graph-Coloring Register Allocation*, ESOP 2010, [author's publication record](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~appel/papers/). This is a mechanized total-correctness precedent for iterated register coalescing, not a weighted-storage optimality theorem. Its historical standalone source edition and license are not established by the current CompCert license reading, so it is not an import candidate yet.

### SECOMP: robust preservation components with an unfinished join

**Authority and artifact.** Jérémy Thibault, Roberto Blanco, Dongjae Lee, Sven Argo, Arthur Azevedo de Amorim, Aïna Linn Georges, Cătălin Hrițcu and Andrew Tolmach, *SECOMP: Formally Secure Compilation of Compartmentalized C Programs*, CCS 2024; [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16277), [archived artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/14537282). The existing project pin is `5c20b839`; [security/RSC.v](https://github.com/secure-compilation/SECOMP/blob/5c20b839/security/RSC.v) contains `RSC`, and [security/Blame.v](https://github.com/secure-compilation/SECOMP/blob/5c20b839/security/Blame.v) contains the blame development. The [upstream README](https://github.com/secure-compilation/SECOMP) identifies `does_prefix_star`, `blame_program`, recomposition and back-translation evidence, with branch-specific build instructions.

**Use and limits.** The closest language-family reference for CJ-SECOMP, R-05-024, R-18-014 and the compiler hardening work. `RSC.v` at the pin assumes back-translation, recomposition, forward/backward correctness and blame through explicit axioms and leaves auxiliary lemmas admitted. The upstream README also reports incomplete integration and remaining compiler-pass admits. A paper-level secure-compilation result and component proofs therefore do not amount to the closed integrated theorem this project needs. Dynamic compromise, its bounded-trace formulation, compartment semantics and the frozen purecap calling convention must be reconciled explicitly.

**License and disposition.** [LICENSE at the pin](https://github.com/secure-compilation/SECOMP/blob/5c20b839/LICENSE) has the CompCert non-commercial grant and enumerated LGPL subset. The capability backend is outside that subset. This is reference evidence under the existing containment decision, not directly vendorable proof code. The current README names Rocq 9.1 but also retains older Coq commands; use the archived artifact's own toolchain for reproducing its claims.

### RustCompCert: a Rust front end over CompCertO, on CompCert's terms

**Authority and artifact.** Jinhua Wu, Yuting Wang, Liukun Yu and Linglong Meng, *RustCompCert: A Verified and Verifying Compiler for a Sequential Subset of Rust*, [arXiv:2602.07455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07455) of 7 February 2026, whose comments field reads "Submitted to Rust Verify 2026" and for which no acceptance is established. The development is a branch of a CompCert fork, [rust-verified-compiler](https://github.com/SJTU-PLV/CompCert/tree/rust-verified-compiler), carrying `rustfrontend/`, `rustparser/` and `rustexamples/`.

**Use and limits.** The shape is a Rust front end (Rustsurface, Rustlight, RustIR) layered on CompCertO that lowers into Clight and reuses the CompCert backend; it does not compile Rust to CompCert, and that distinction is what makes it a comparator for R-18-015's front-end-over-MIR shape rather than for a target refinement. Two claims are aimed at and neither is finished: semantics preservation from the subset down to assembly, and memory safety delivered by a Polonius-style borrow-checking pass treated as verifying compilation. The paper is self-described ongoing work with no end-to-end theorem claimed complete, no artifact evaluation and no peer review. Scope is a sequential subset: no concurrency, no `unsafe`, no trait or generic machinery beyond the subset, and nothing in CompCert's own trusted base is displaced.

**License and disposition.** The fork inherits CompCert's own [LICENSE](https://github.com/SJTU-PLV/CompCert/blob/rust-verified-compiler/LICENSE): the INRIA Non-Commercial License Agreement, with the same enumerated dual-licensed subset, LGPL flocq and MenhirLib trees and BSD runtime that the CompCert record above states, so the consequences recorded there apply to this branch unchanged and are not lifted by its being a research fork. Unqualified lead: a preprint whose theorem is unfinished and whose terms are non-commercial.

### Cogent and Dargent: certified refinement through a declared data layout

**Authority and artifact.** Zilin Chen, Ambroise Lafont, Liam O'Connor, Gabriele Keller, Craig McLaughlin, Vincent Jackson and Christine Rizkallah, *Dargent: A Silver Bullet for Verified Data Layout Refinement*, PACMPL 7(POPL), Article 47, January 2023, DOI 10.1145/3571240; source at [au-ts/cogent](https://github.com/au-ts/cogent). Cogent's certifying compiler co-generates, per program, C code and Isabelle/HOL theorems establishing that the C refines a shallow Isabelle/HOL embedding of the source, combining language-level proofs with per-program translation validation into one top-level theorem. Dargent adds a declarative bit-level layout description language: the compiler emits the getters and setters realising a declared layout together with generated proofs that they respect it.

**Use and limits.** This is the closest published layout-refinement result to R-05-083's obligation, that field layouts are declared once in a register-description language and the shift/mask accessors are generated correct-by-construction and checked against that declaration; it is the same move made for record layouts instead of MMIO registers, and it is equally relevant to R-05-082's descriptor primitives and to [DescriptorCheck.v](../../../tools/bedrock2-lowering/DescriptorCheck.v). The Cogent compiler is not itself verified: each compilation carries its own certificate, so a compiler defect shows as a failed proof for that program rather than being ruled out. Isabelle/HOL, the C-to-Isabelle parser, AutoCorres and the C compiler are trusted, and C bit-field semantics sit outside AutoCorres and are simulated rather than modelled natively. The theorem is refinement only: functional correctness, security and termination of the source must be proved separately over the shallow embedding, hand-written foreign C such as allocators and drivers is outside the certificate, and there is no concurrency. The prover is Isabelle/HOL, so the method transfers and the proof terms do not.

**License and disposition.** There is no single repository licence, which an incorporation reading must not paper over: the tree ships [LICENSE_BSD2.txt](https://github.com/au-ts/cogent/blob/master/LICENSE_BSD2.txt) and [LICENSE_GPLv2.txt](https://github.com/au-ts/cogent/blob/master/LICENSE_GPLv2.txt) and decides per file by a tag header, the compiler sources carry the GPLv2 tag, and the POPL'23 artifact deposit is GPL-2.0-only. A tree that vendored the compiler would carry a copyleft obligation the BSD-2 file alone does not lift. Reference for the layout-refinement method; no source is an import candidate on these terms.

### Lean-MLIR: peephole rewriting proved once, per rewrite

**Authority and artifact.** Siddharth Bhat, Alex Keizer, Chris Hughes, Andrés Goens and Tobias Grosser, *Verifying Peephole Rewriting in SSA Compiler IRs*, ITP 2024, LIPIcs volume 309, Article 9, pages 9:1-9:20, [DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2024.9](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2024.9); this is a peer-reviewed publication and not an arXiv entry, and the ITP24 tag of [opencompl/lean-mlir](https://github.com/opencompl/lean-mlir) is its artifact. A core calculus for SSA-based intermediate representations, generic over the IR and covering the nested-region scoping MLIR uses, is mechanised in Lean, and `denote_rewritePeepholeAt` proves that applying an already-correct peephole rewrite at a program point preserves the program's denotational semantics.

**Use and limits.** A reference for the structure a rewriting argument takes in the lowering work of M1.6 and R-18-014: the framework proves the application discipline once, and each individual rewrite still needs its own proof, which is the division a local rewriting pass would inherit. Neither MLIR nor LLVM is verified. The semantics of the modelled dialects are the authors' own Lean formalization, nothing connects it to MLIR's C++ implementation or to LLVM's language reference, and an unmodelled dialect gets no guarantee. The upstream's `sorry`-free guarantee is scoped to core framework theorems and not to all code in the repository. The prover is Lean, so no proof term reaches the Rocq gate.

**License and disposition.** The actual [LICENSE](https://github.com/opencompl/lean-mlir/blob/main/LICENSE) is Apache-2.0 with an appended "License for Other Assets" section carving out the `/images` directory, and it names no copyright holder; the paper itself is CC BY 4.0 under LIPIcs. A blanket "Apache-2.0" description is not what the file says. Reference only.

### Rupicola: proof-producing relational compilation

**Authority and artifact.** Clément Pit-Claudel, Jade Philipoom, Dustin Jamner, Andres Erbsen and Adam Chlipala, *Relational Compilation for Performance-Critical Applications*, PLDI 2022; [paper and artifact links](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/RupicolaPLDI22/). At `5d37f8567667a3c699d2730574c35dfbe772dd67`, [IPChecksum.v](https://github.com/mit-plv/rupicola/blob/5d37f8567667a3c699d2730574c35dfbe772dd67/src/Rupicola/Examples/Net/IPChecksum/IPChecksum.v) derives `ip_checksum_br2fn_ok`; [the tutorial](https://github.com/mit-plv/rupicola/blob/5d37f8567667a3c699d2730574c35dfbe772dd67/etc/relational-compilation-tutorial.v) explains the relational compilation contract.

**Use and limits.** Strong reusable architecture for M1.6, Q2 and the wire-parser lowering route. [IpChecksumBaseline.v](../../../tools/bedrock2-lowering/IpChecksumBaseline.v) and [DescriptorCheck.v](../../../tools/bedrock2-lowering/DescriptorCheck.v) are already local consumers of this family. The generated Bedrock2 function has a correctness proof against its Gallina specification. Printing that function as C is a different boundary; this theorem does not justify the printer, CHERI pointer lowering, the target compiler or the final bytes. The local released stack already runs Rupicola 0.0.11 and Bedrock2 0.0.9 under Rocq 9.2.0 in a separate switch.

**License and disposition.** [LICENSE](https://github.com/mit-plv/rupicola/blob/5d37f8567667a3c699d2730574c35dfbe772dd67/LICENSE) is MIT, copyright the Rupicola authors named in `AUTHORS`; retain copyright and permission notice when copying substantial code. Suitable for further component-specific derivations, but no unfilled local proof matches this example as a drop-in theorem.

### Bedrock2: a proved compiler pipeline to a specified RISC-V machine

**Authority and artifact.** The Bedrock2 authors, identified in [AUTHORS](https://github.com/mit-plv/bedrock2/blob/06655844959f089c90d91d41041dd475cb2489f9/AUTHORS). At `06655844959f089c90d91d41041dd475cb2489f9`, [compiler/Pipeline.v](https://github.com/mit-plv/bedrock2/blob/06655844959f089c90d91d41041dd475cb2489f9/compiler/src/compiler/Pipeline.v) provides `compose_phases_correct`, `composed_compiler_correct`, `compiler_correct` and `compiler_correct_wp`. The theorem interfaces quantify over language, word, memory, external-call and machine parameters.

**Use and limits.** A substantial mechanized precedent for CJ-COMPCERT, R-18-014, M1.6 and Q2's source-to-target correspondence experiment. Its abstraction boundaries are especially useful for writing the missing purecap bridge. A successful invocation still owes all instance laws, external-call contracts, code installation, stack and memory conditions. The project's recorded `Words32Naive`/RV32I experiment does not instantiate the frozen RV64 CHERI machine, and changing a width parameter alone cannot add capability tags, bounds and authority.

**License and disposition.** The actual [LICENSE](https://github.com/mit-plv/bedrock2/blob/06655844959f089c90d91d41041dd475cb2489f9/LICENSE) is MIT, copyright 2017-2021 the Bedrock2 authors; preserve its notice and read each imported dependency's own license. Existing local opam packages already establish useful Rocq 9.2.0 compatibility for the released route. The newly surveyed source revision is not substituted for that measured release.

### Bedrock2 leakage logic: nondeterministic constant-time proof components

**Authority and artifact.** Owen Conoly, Andres Erbsen and Adam Chlipala, *Smooth, Integrated Proofs of Cryptographic Constant Time for Nondeterministic Programs and Compilers*, PLDI 2025; [author's paper page](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/TimingPLDI25/). **Use and limits.** Source-qualified proof components for R-05-063 and the constant-time hardening work: nondeterminism is part of the security contract, and ordinary functional refinement does not supply it. The paper reports an end-to-end Bedrock2-to-RISC-V result; this survey locates the logic theorems, not a reproduced end-to-end build of its artifact. Adapt the observation alphabet to the actual CHERI instructions, SRAM banks, spills, memory classes and external calls. R-05-063 still requires on-artifact checking; a compiler theorem cannot substitute for that requirement.

**License and disposition.** The named source files fall under the MIT [Bedrock2 LICENSE](https://github.com/mit-plv/bedrock2/blob/06655844959f089c90d91d41041dd475cb2489f9/LICENSE). Preserve attribution to the paper authors and the software notice. Useful logic components; not a drop-in proof of this project's binary constant time.

### MetaRocq: type and proof erasure correctness

#### Conversion certification without normalization

**Authority and artifact.** Meven Lennon-Bertrand, *What Does It Take to Certify a Conversion Checker?*, FSCD 2025, DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.FSCD.2025.27](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol337-fscd2025/html/LIPIcs.FSCD.2025.27/LIPIcs.FSCD.2025.27.html). The paper links the [`fscd25` formalization](https://github.com/CoqHott/logrel-coq/tree/fscd25) and [versioned artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/15359259), published 2025-05-07, version v1.

**Use and limits.** Separates positive soundness, negative soundness and termination of conversion checking. In its MLTT setting, type-constructor injectivity suffices for positive soundness; normalization is needed for termination of the unbounded procedure. This is the principal method reference for M6.2b-0's bounded partial route. A PCUIC application still needs its own proved structural properties, guard semantics, universe and environment treatment. The paper's parameterized metatheory cannot become undeclared local axioms.

**License and disposition.** The paper states CC BY 4.0. The archived source's own license and complete dependency terms are unread; the branch is a locator, not the frozen local input. Reference for the proof decomposition; unqualified source lead pending that reading and native qualification.

#### Martin-Löf à la Coq and executable partial checking

**Authority and artifact.** Arthur Adjedj, Meven Lennon-Bertrand, Kenji Maillard, Pierre-Marie Pédrot and Loïc Pujet, *Martin-Löf à la Coq*, CPP 2024, DOI 10.1145/3636501.3636951; [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06376) and [`logrel-coq` development](https://github.com/CoqHott/logrel-coq).

**License and disposition.** The current repository advertises MIT and Coq 8.20 compatibility; its own selected-edition license and dependency closure remain unread and unbuilt here. Method reference and unqualified source lead. A current branch is not evidence about the CPP artifact's exact edition or compatibility with the local Rocq tuple.

#### Rocq guard specification and reimplementation

**Authority and artifact.** Yee-Jian Tan and Yannick Forster, [*Towards Formalising the Guard Checker of Rocq*](https://msp.cis.strath.ac.uk/types2025/abstracts/TYPES2025_paper15.pdf), TYPES 2025 extended abstract; the earlier [Coq Workshop 2024 abstract](https://www.yeejian.dev/files/240914-coqws-extended-abstract.pdf) points to [`inria-cambium/m1-tan` at `v1.0.0`](https://github.com/inria-cambium/m1-tan/tree/v1.0.0). **Use and limits.** The 2026 work supplies a specification and proof-of-concept MetaRocq reimplementation, decomposing the guard into an eliminator-oriented core, restricted beta-iota cuts, reduction that checks erasable subterms, and extended subterm analysis through fixpoints and matches. ### Peregrine: a middle-end whose new legs are the unverified ones

**Authority and artifact.** Eske Hoy Nielsen, Simon Dima, Lucas Escot, Orestis Melkonian, Hugo Segoufin-Cholet, James Chapman, Yannick Forster, Matthieu Sozeau and Bas Spitters, *Peregrine: a middle-end for code generation from proof assistants*, a four-page contributed abstract at TYPES 2026. TYPES abstracts are contributed talks whose post-proceedings publication is a separate later process, and none is established here, so this is a conference-branded preprint and not a paper. The tool is [peregrine-tool](https://github.com/peregrine-project/peregrine-tool), with the CakeML backend and the typed-extraction components in sibling repositories of the same organization; the Agda frontend is [agda2lambox](https://github.com/agda/agda2lambox), which lives in the Agda organization and not in that one.

**Use and limits.** Relevant to M1.6's extraction alternatives and to M6.2b's checker-and-extraction correspondence as an engineering shape rather than a theorem: a standalone middle-end over Rocq's extraction intermediate language that adapts the verified MetaRocq pipeline and adds verified de-serialization of that language from an S-expression representation. ### RefinedRust: sound refinement typing over Radium

### VerusBelt: a semantic foundation for a subset of Verus, not for Verus

**Authority and artifact.** Travis Hance, Laila Elbeheiry, Yusuke Matsushita and Derek Dreyer, *VerusBelt: A Semantic Foundation for Verus's Proof-Oriented Extensions to the Rust Type System*, PACMPL 10(PLDI), Article 247, June 2026, DOI 10.1145/3808325, with the [archived artifact](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19613067). **Use and limits.** R-18-018's fourth sub-deliverable names VerusBelt as the foundational grounding of the manual-proof path for HAL-adjacent `unsafe`, and R-05-081's machine-checked memory-safety and hardware-contract obligation is the local consumer; this record states what that grounding is and is not. ### RustHornBelt: prophecy specifications for safe APIs over unsafe code

### Diaframe: proof automation whose output is still a kernel-checked term

### Affect: an affine type and effect system over one-shot continuations

### Blaze: a relational separation logic for effect handlers

### Program Logics à la Carte: adequacy proved once, effects added as fragments

**Authority and artifact.** Max Vistrup, Michael Sammler and Ralf Jung, *Program Logics à la Carte*, PACMPL 9(POPL), Article 11, January 2025, DOI 10.1145/3704847, with the [archived artifact](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14180355). A general-purpose Iris program logic over interaction trees is mechanised in Rocq and its soundness and adequacy proved once; on top of it sits a library of effect fragments, each with reasoning principles proved sound independently and composable into a logic for a given language, covering shared and global state, concurrency, demonic and angelic non-determinism, safe and unsafe termination, and partial correctness.

**Use and limits.** The composition discipline speaks directly to R-05-020's non-duplication condition: a logic assembled from fragments whose soundness is proved once is the alternative to re-minting a program logic per consumer, and that is the argument a local adoption would have to make. No real language, compiler or program is verified by this work, the demonstrations being small example languages. It applies only once an interaction-tree semantics for a language exists, and nothing here establishes that such a semantics agrees with the language's own specification, which is where any downstream adequacy claim actually rests. **License and disposition.** The artifact record and the host's own file-derived report both state BSD-3-Clause, but the repository's licence file itself is unread, the upstream host having refused connection during this qualification, so no copyright holder is established. ### Interaction Trees: infrastructure whose axioms travel with it

**Authority and artifact.** The DeepSpec [InteractionTrees](https://github.com/DeepSpec/InteractionTrees) library, maintained by Li-yao Xia per `coq-itree.opam`, and the associated paper by Li-yao Xia, Yannick Zakowski, Paul He, Chung-Kil Hur, Gregory Malecha, Benjamin C. Pierce and Steve Zdancewic, *Interaction Trees: Representing Recursive and Impure Programs in Coq*, PACMPL 4(POPL), Article 51, January 2020, DOI 10.1145/3371119. The maintained library and the paper are different kinds of evidence and are cited as such. The library supplies a coinductive type for recursive and effectful computations together with a mechanised equational theory: strong and weak bisimulation, monad laws, iteration and mutual-recursion combinators with their fixpoint equations, category laws, and interpreters proved to commute with the structure so a program can be refined in stages.

**Use and limits.** Infrastructure, not a verification of any program or language: giving a language an interaction-tree semantics, and showing that semantics faithful to the language's own specification, is the user's obligation and is not established here. The fact that matters most locally is the assumption set. The development states a dependency on uniqueness of identity proofs, functional extensionality and excluded middle, so anything built over it inherits those three. R-05-163 enumerates every shipped theorem's axiom and assumption set mechanically from its proof term and fails the build wherever it is not exactly the declared set, and R-05-164 fixes that declared set as R-06-011's admission axioms, R-06-014's bootstrap root and R-18-031's *Ax* boundary ledger. None of the three assumptions is in any of them, so a development over this library needs an amendment to R-06-011 or to the *Ax* ledger, decided at the review gate; declaring them inside the development to make the gate pass is itself a review-gate finding against R-05-150. Equivalence up to tau deliberately ignores finite runs of silent steps, so it distinguishes neither step counts nor timing, and the base type does not model non-determinism.

**License and disposition.** The actual [LICENSE](https://github.com/DeepSpec/InteractionTrees/blob/master/LICENSE) is MIT, copyright 2018 DeepSpec, corroborated by the `license: MIT` field in `coq-itree.opam`. Reference, and a dependency entered deliberately rather than inherited through a downstream library, given the axioms above.

### CakeML: a verified compiler and resource-sensitive correctness statement

**Authority and artifact.** Ramana Kumar, Magnus Myreen, Michael Norrish, Scott Owens and the CakeML contributors; [official project and publications](https://cakeml.org/). At `e6bb97e7b11423ddb00b92e00b7dc972de361e89`, [backendProofScript.sml](https://github.com/CakeML/cakeml/blob/e6bb97e7b11423ddb00b92e00b7dc972de361e89/compiler/backend/proofs/backendProofScript.sml) proves `compile_correct` and `compile_correct_is_safe_for_space` in HOL4. The former relates installed machine behavior to source behavior extended with resource-limit outcomes, subject to compiler/machine configuration and initialization conditions.

**Use and limits.** A mature independently developed reference for CJ-COMPCERT, R-18-014 and M6.2b's executable checker assurance. Its treatment of code installation, foreign functions and resource failure is relevant to the seams local closed Gallina statements do not yet cover. It targets another source language and HOL4, with no automatic Rocq proof reuse. Its runtime/GC and source resource-limit extension also differ from R-08-010's static memory design. A capability backend and a Gallina/CIC bridge are substantial new work.

**License and disposition.** [LICENSE](https://github.com/CakeML/cakeml/blob/e6bb97e7b11423ddb00b92e00b7dc972de361e89/LICENSE) is BSD-3-Clause and names Anthony Fox, Google LLC, Ramana Kumar, Magnus Myreen, Michael Norrish, Scott Owens, Yong Kiam Tan and other contributors. Preserve notices and avoid endorsement claims. Survey records HOL4 as the prover; it does not establish a reproducible HOL dependency revision. Proof-design reference, not a drop-in dependency.

### Candle: sound proof production connected to execution

**Authority and artifact.** Oskar Abrahamsson, Magnus O. Myreen, Ramana Kumar and Thomas Sewell, *Candle: A Verified Implementation of HOL Light*, ITP 2022, with an extended journal version; [official project](https://cakeml.org/candle/). The CakeML revision above includes [holSoundnessScript.sml](https://github.com/CakeML/cakeml/blob/e6bb97e7b11423ddb00b92e00b7dc972de361e89/candle/standard/semantics/holSoundnessScript.sml), whose `proves_sound` connects derivability and model-theoretic validity, and [computeProofScript.sml](https://github.com/CakeML/cakeml/blob/e6bb97e7b11423ddb00b92e00b7dc972de361e89/candle/prover/compute/computeProofScript.sml), whose `compute_thm` validates the in-logic computation primitive. The official project states an end-to-end result about facts exported through a kernel-controlled FFI channel.

**Use and limits.** Gold-standard methodological reference for M6.2b and the R-05-164 checker trust boundary: an inference-rule proof is connected to the implementation and its observable output. The located HOL4 theorems are useful inspection points, not a rebuilt end-to-end witness here. HOL Light is a different logic from CIC, its runtime differs from the on-device static-memory machine, and the project's proof serializer and target compiler still need their own correctness statements.

**License and disposition.** The selected proof files are under CakeML's BSD-3-Clause [LICENSE](https://github.com/CakeML/cakeml/blob/e6bb97e7b11423ddb00b92e00b7dc972de361e89/LICENSE). The separate executable [CakeML/candle repository](https://github.com/CakeML/candle) is a HOL Light fork and needs its own source-specific review before any incorporation. No source or generated binary is imported.

## Leads that do not supply the requested closed proof

**SECOMP2CHERI.** [The PriSC 2023 extended abstract](https://catalin-hritcu.github.io/publications/SECOMP2CHERI-PriSC23.pdf) is relevant to R-18-014, but the [SECOMP README's capability-backend section](https://github.com/secure-compilation/SECOMP#compiling-compartmentalized-programs-with-the-capabilities-backend) calls the implementation a prototype and identifies unsupported output instructions. M1.1b/M1.2a already reject using it as a working translation. It supplies neither the missing full backend theorem nor the frozen-profile bridge; the non-commercial source terms are those recorded for SECOMP above.

**MCQC.** Eleftherios Ioannidis and coauthors, [*Extracting and optimizing formally verified code for systems programming*](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/mcqc%3Anfm19.pdf), NFM 2019, and [mit-pdos/mcqc](https://github.com/mit-pdos/mcqc). Its own paper identifies the Haskell compiler, C++17 native library and ordinary C++ compiler as trusted components; the primitive library is tested rather than formally verified. This supports M1.6's engineering precedent for extraction without a tracing runtime, not a verified GC-free lowering theorem. No exact compiled-correctness theorem is identified and the standalone source license is not audited here, so no import is proposed.

**Corten.** František Farka, Carmine Abate, Sven Linker and Sebastian Ertel, [*Corten: Foundational Verification of Rust Programs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04372), September 2026: a deep embedding of Rust's typed high-level intermediate representation into Rocq, a weakest-precondition calculus for its dynamic semantics, a program logic proved sound against an interaction-trees denotation, and a buddy-allocator case study establishing memory safety of allocation and deallocation but not the allocator's functional correctness. Two things keep it out of the inventory proper. It is a plain preprint, its PDF set in the ACM template with an unfilled reference line and a placeholder DOI, so no acceptance may be asserted. And it names no repository, deposit or code URL anywhere in its forty-one pages, so there is nothing to pin, nothing to build and no licence to read: the licence is recorded as unread because no artifact exists to read one from, and the posting covers the text alone. Its stated trusted base includes rustc's parser, name resolution and type checker, and the hax and hax2rocq transpiler, which is explicitly not verified against rustc's own representation; the borrow checker is used only as an untrusted oracle, so no borrow-checking soundness result follows. Unqualified lead, and unreleased.

**Alive2.** [AliveToolkit/alive2](https://github.com/AliveToolkit/alive2), with Nuno P. Lopes, Juneyoung Lee, Chung-Kil Hur, Zhengyang Liu and John Regehr, *Alive2: Bounded Translation Validation for LLVM*, PLDI 2021, DOI 10.1145/3453483.3454030; the repository [LICENSE](https://github.com/AliveToolkit/alive2/blob/master/LICENSE) is MIT. For a source and target LLVM IR function pair it encodes both against its own formalization of LLVM semantics, including undef, poison and undefined behaviour, and asks an SMT solver whether the target refines the source. It must not be described as unbounded, exhaustive or verified: its own title says bounded, loops are unrolled to a bound so bugs can be missed, the verdict is relative to a semantics encoding that is not itself verified and has knowingly diverged from the language reference, the solver is trusted, and interprocedural transformations are unsupported. Decisively for this inventory it emits no re-checkable proof object, so it cannot be the existing checker that decides a producer's output under R-05-066. It belongs to R-18-019's class: a bug-finding oracle riding alongside the certifier, never a second checker, and never evidence about an admitted binary.

**Secret erasure.** The inspected compiler, erasure and ownership results do not prove R-05-126d for registers, spills and all copies, and none prove physical erasure. A new result must connect the must-erase grade, allowed consuming operations, compilation-introduced storage and target observations. Type/proof erasure and logical ownership consumption are not that theorem.

The incorporation decision for this lane is therefore to record these source-qualified precedents and their gaps. The strongest immediate continuations are component derivations through the already installed Rupicola/Bedrock2 route, a separately resolved RefinedRust proof environment, and careful reuse of RustBelt initialization/lifetime arguments while authoring the CHERI-TAL interpretation. Each continuation preserves the requirement that the actual local theorem, its premises and its emitted artifact pass the project gate. Several records above carry a licence recorded as unread, each naming what reading it takes; until that reading happens none of them is permissive, and a repository badge, a sibling project and an archive deposit's content licence are none of them the file.
