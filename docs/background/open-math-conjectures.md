# Open mathematical problems and recent advances relevant to VerifiedOS

> Non-normative research survey. General source review through 6 October 2026; eleven-square packing, 3SUM/APSP and integer-multiplication artifacts checked on 10 October 2026. The [requirements register](../requirements-register.md) remains authoritative. This survey admits no new axiom or implementation decision and supplies no evidence that a platform guarantee has been established. Existing declared cryptographic premises retain their current status.

The strongest connections are to memory placement, scheduling, proof production, computational security, and arithmetic kernels. Some resolutions could improve an algorithm; others would establish limits or invalidate a security assumption. A proof of existence need not provide a usable construction, and a faster asymptotic algorithm need not be faster at this machine's sizes.

There is no exhaustive, stable list of **all** open mathematics that could affect a general-purpose computer. This survey covers named conjectures and concrete unresolved questions for which a causal connection to the present design can be stated. It includes open complexity classifications and cryptographic hypotheses, explicitly labeled because they are not all conjectures in the same sense. It also covers [recent solutions and advances](#recent-solutions-and-advances-worth-evaluating), including results that offer something useful before a broader conjecture is settled. Project-specific research questions appear separately. Famous problems without an identified consumer are excluded rather than assigned an invented benefit.

The mathematical statements and status come from the linked sources. **The proposed VerifiedOS applications are this survey's inferences**, unless an existing project contract already states the connection. Recent preprints are identified as such; finding a claimed proof is not the same as establishing its acceptance or checking it in the project's prover.

The status review includes recent manuscripts, their revisions, and public GitHub proof and implementation repositories. Repository age, popularity, author affiliation and AI assistance do not decide mathematical validity. A new repository can supply a decisive counterexample or a complete proof before journal review. Its evidence must still match the original statement: inspect definitions, quantifiers, assumptions, imported dependencies and the computational model, then distinguish source inspection, supplied build or kernel records, independent reproduction and external mathematical review. A declaration named after a conjecture, an empty placeholder type, or a clean search for `sorry` does not establish that correspondence. The artifact notes below identify what was actually inspected; this review did not rerun external Lean proof packages.

The [OpenAI mathematical collection at revision `adc7f124`](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a), inspected on 6 October, supplies several directly overlapping proof and algorithm claims. Its README distinguishes verification stages and warns that unformalized results may contain issues. The entries below record manuscript statements, available Lean developments and their stated scope; neither this review nor a catalogue label establishes independent replay or mathematical acceptance. The collection's [Comparator instructions](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/ComparatorChallenges/README.md) point to [a checker](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator) that compares trusted statements and their dependencies with candidate proofs and checks permitted axioms. The [Hadamard entry](#hadamard-matrix-conjecture) includes independent exact verification of twelve finite matrices, and the [pinwheel certificate entry](#pinwheel-scheduling-certificates-and-complexity) an independent exact feasibility check of one counterexample's finite instances. ## Allocation, fragmentation and scheduling

### Dynamic Storage Allocation below a factor of two

**Open approximation problem; direct connection.** Given known object sizes and lifetime intervals, assign each object one fixed contiguous address interval. Objects whose lifetimes overlap must occupy disjoint addresses. Minimize the resulting address span. Can a polynomial-time algorithm achieve a worst-case approximation factor strictly below two for the unrestricted problem? Khan's [open-problem collection](https://www.csa.iisc.ac.in/~arindamkhan/FGA_OpenProblems.pdf) lists this frontier, with a known `2 + epsilon` guarantee; [Buchsbaum et al.](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539703423941) establish the classical approximation and load analysis. The sub-two ratios of [Mömke and Wiese, ICALP 2020](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2020.86), including polynomial-time `63/32 + epsilon` for uniform capacities, concern the Storage Allocation Problem: select a maximum-profit subset of tasks drawable as non-overlapping rectangles under a capacity profile. They do not approximate DSA's minimum span.

A constructive improvement could reduce the backing needed by the fixed-tier [memory plan](../spec.md#r-08-012). A hardness result would sharpen the reason to exploit structured lifetimes. Neither outcome says that optimal span equals peak live bytes. Alignment, CHERI representability, pinned locations, distinct memory classes and revocation may invalidate a direct transfer of the classical guarantee.

A related [August 2026 tree-scan preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14471v1) bounds space by maximum live memory plus the largest buffer for constant-bounded programs, using defragmentation, and obtains optimality when in-place swapping is allowed. Moving objects and transforming execution distinguish that result from the fixed-address approximation question here.

### Modified Integer Round-Up Property for bin packing

**Claimed refutation with a supplied Lean development; allocation lower-bound connection.** Let `OPT` be the minimum number of unit-capacity bins and `LP` the optimum of the Gilmore-Gomory configuration relaxation for the same item multiset. The Modified Integer Round-Up Property, MIRUP, asserts `OPT <= ceil(LP) + 1`. The stricter assertion `OPT = ceil(LP)` is false. [Eisenbrand, Pálvölgyi and Rothvoss](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.2170v2) state the conjecture, due to Scheithauer and Terno; the [June 2026 exact-algorithm preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05152v2) predates the refutation claim below.

Related targets are a universal constant additive LP gap and an efficient algorithm returning at most `OPT + O(1)` bins. These are different assertions: an existence bound does not itself supply the algorithm. [Hoberg and Rothvoss](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08796v1) give a logarithmic additive guarantee. OpenAI's [family 118 manuscript and formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/118.md) claim that for every integer `c >= 0` there are `5B` rational items, each greater than `1/6`, with both the individual-copy and size-type LP values equal to `B` but `OPT > B+c`. It also claims NP-hardness of every fixed additive approximation allowance. If established, this refutes MIRUP and every universal constant additive LP gap, and rules out polynomial-time `OPT+O(1)` packing unless `P=NP`; the Komlós results would not undo that barrier. This review read the [challenge definitions and theorem](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/ComparatorChallenges/BinPackingGap.lean), paired configuration and [solution theorem](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Computability/BinPacking/Main.lean), without replaying the transitive development.

Such results could tighten packing of equal-capacity banks or storage containers and estimates of unavoidable slack. Ordinary bin packing has no lifetimes or cross-bin contiguity requirement. It therefore does not directly solve [size-class waste](../spec.md#r-08-018b), elastic-heap fragmentation, or the full physical placement problem.

A [4 June 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05152v2) gives polynomial algorithms for the Augmented IRUP benchmark class and pseudopolynomial algorithms for Augmented Non-IRUP instances. Their scoped optimality guarantees make them preprocessing leads, without resolving MIRUP or the unrestricted constant-additive-gap target.

### Asymptotic Rank Conjecture and exact bin packing

**Named conjecture; conditional exact-search connection.** The tight-tensor formulation of Strassen's Asymptotic Rank Conjecture over the rationals asserts that a tight `d` by `d` by `d` tensor has asymptotic rank at most `d`, with equality for concise tensors. Asymptotic rank measures the exponential growth of rank under repeated tensor products. [Björklund et al., SODA 2025, section 1.1 and footnote 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04987v2#S1.SS1), distinguish this formulation from stronger unrestricted versions; the tensors used for their partitioning algorithms are tight, and their conditional algorithm works over characteristic-zero fields.

[Dhar et al., Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12224v1#S1), published in the [ESA 2026 proceedings on 25 August](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.53), uses that partitioning result to obtain a randomized exact bin-packing algorithm in `O((2-epsilon)^n)` time for some constant `epsilon > 0`, with high-probability correctness, assuming ARC. The unconditional general sub-`2^n` target remains open. This is a possible route to smaller offline packing searches, separate from [MIRUP's additive approximation gap](#modified-integer-round-up-property-for-bin-packing). The runtime remains exponential, and ordinary bins still omit lifetimes, alignment and CHERI bounds. A finite candidate needs independent checking under the [placement contract](../implementation/placement-search.md); the conjecture supplies no platform proof premise.

Few distinct sizes give a separate exact frontier. For high-multiplicity instances with `d` distinct item sizes and encoding length `|I|`, [Jansen, Ohnesorge and Pirotton, ICALP 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.116/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.116.html), show that Goemans and Rothvoss's `|I|^(2^O(d))` algorithm cannot be improved to `|I|^(2^o(d))` unless ETH fails. They leave open whether the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in `d`. [Koana and Kumabe's 15 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16923v1) answers that question with a deterministic `O*(2^(d^O(d)))` algorithm, where `O*` suppresses factors polynomial in `|I|`, with sizes and multiplicities encoded in binary. [Jansen and Ohnesorge's 29 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31328v3), first posted on 25 September, extends that approach to a cone-and-polytope intersection problem; its Corollary 1.3 decides whether the items fit into a given number of bins in deterministic `2^(2^O(d)) * |I|^O(1)` time, removing the `log d` factor from the `2^(2^O(d log d))` dependence of Koana and Kumabe's bound. Because `2^(2^o(d)) * |I|^O(1)` is at most `|I|^(2^o(d))`, the ICALP bound makes this doubly exponential dependence optimal under ETH, as the authors state. The algorithm also constructs a packing from at most `2^(2d+1)` distinct bin configurations and their multiplicities. The authors disclose that GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 3.8 Flash were used in formulating theorems and proofs, language editing, notation review and literature search. This review inspected Jansen and Ohnesorge's theorem statements without verifying either proof. Even the optimal dependence on `d` is doubly exponential. A composition-fixed set of size classes may motivate this parameter, but it becomes this bin-packing instance only through the reduction required [above](#modified-integer-round-up-property-for-bin-packing).

### Optimal copying overhead for memory reallocation

**Open tight-bound and implementation problems, with a major 2026 advance; direct fragmentation connection.** In `M` cells, maintain contiguous objects under insertions and deletions, with live volume at most `(1-epsilon)M` and relocation allowed. For an update of size `s`, charge `1 + moved_existing_volume/s`. [Jin's STOC 2026 result, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15417v1), also in the [proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3798129.3800822), gives `O(log^4(1/epsilon) * (log log(1/epsilon))^2)` worst-case expected per-update overhead against an oblivious adversary. The general lower bound is `Omega(log(1/epsilon))`; closing that gap and obtaining a time-efficient implementation remain open. Its Theorem 1.4 bounds worst-case expected squared overhead below by `Omega(epsilon^(-1/7))`, so subpolynomial overhead cannot hold with high probability. Moved volume is not computation time.

A movement budget below allocated volume leaves a constant-factor space cost. In the partial-compaction model, total moved volume never exceeds `1/c` of the volume allocated so far. [Cohen and Petrank's full PLDI 2013 version](https://csaws.cs.technion.ac.il/~erez/Papers/compaction-full.pdf) proves worst-case heap lower bounds for every manual or automatic memory manager: with 256 MB of live data and objects of at most 1 MB, twice the live space at a 10% budget and 3.5 times at 1%. Jin's allocator instead may move, in expectation, up to a polylogarithmic multiple of each update's size.

This sharpens the fragmentation-versus-copying tradeoff for explicitly movable application storage. It does not alter the fixed tier's static backing. Native CHERI capabilities and guest Wasm offsets do not update themselves when bytes move: an application needs a proved relocation or indirection discipline, authorized reference updates, revocation accounting, intermediate space and bounded yield behavior. Expected costs do not establish a deadline, and an oblivious-adversary analysis does not cover arbitrary adaptive requests. Any experiment belongs within the [elastic envelope](../implementation/contracts/elastic-domain.md) and its existing authority rules.

### Strong, or prefix, Komlós conjecture

**Constant-bound conjecture refuted in a September 2026 manuscript; optimal growth remains open.** The conjecture asked whether every fixed ordered sequence of vectors of Euclidean norm at most one admits signs making every prefix's maximum-coordinate magnitude universally bounded, even when the signs can use the entire sequence. [Kintali's manuscript dated 15 September](https://shivakintali.github.io/papers/StrongKomlos.pdf), for which no arXiv posting was located, gives explicit sequences in dimensions `d = 2^(2^k - 1)` requiring prefix discrepancy at least `(1/2)*sqrt(1 + log2 d)`, and square `n` by `n` examples requiring `Omega(sqrt(log log n))`. The sequences are extremely long. [Karingula and Lovett's 22 September revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20979v2) incorporates this refutation and contrasts it with the dimension-independent `O(sqrt(log n))` upper bound, which an [online linear-time algorithm](#ordinary-komlós-and-beck-fiala-proof-announcements) now attains with high probability. The remaining question is the optimal growth between these bounds, not whether a universal constant exists. The manuscript discloses that ChatGPT worked out details of the author's outline, with the author verifying the proofs. No independent mechanization of the lower bound was located in this review.

The same manuscript's Corollary 3.2 gives `{0,1}` matrices with at most `s` nonzero entries per column whose fixed-order prefix discrepancy exceeds `s/8`, ruling out an `O(sqrt(s))` prefix analogue of Beck-Fiala. Their length `T` is at least `2^(s-1)`, so `s` lies below the sparsity threshold of the [online Beck-Fiala algorithm](#ordinary-komlós-and-beck-fiala-proof-announcements); this review's comparison finds no conflict between the two results.

If a composition's legal binary assignments admit this vector model, constructive bounds could constrain accumulated resource imbalance at every phase and inform [buffer reservations](../spec.md#r-08-046). The refutation rules out a universal constant for unrestricted instances; structured workloads may still allow one. A larger choice set also restores a constant: [Niles-Weed, Sadovsky and Shkrob's 1 October preprint, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02127v1#S1), chooses a sign and a coordinate permutation for each vector online, so that every prefix of vectors of Euclidean norm at most one in `R^n` has maximum-coordinate magnitude at most `(sqrt(n-1)+1)/sqrt(n)`, which is asymptotically optimal; the authors disclose LLM assistance. That transfer requires each item's demand to be freely reassignable among interchangeable resources. For `s`-sparse binary choices, a fixed-order bound of order `sqrt(s)` needs additional structure, such as the online result's horizon condition `s >= C log(T) (log log(T))^(2+eta)`. It is not an online dispatch theorem: signs, nonnegative inventory, precedence and per-coordinate capacities all need a faithful interpretation.

The local [resource-credit proof contract](../implementation/static-memory/resource-contracts.md#prefix-and-sequential-composition-proof-contract) applies the distinction between final balance and intermediate demand to the existing finite take/return semantics. Its extension characterizes success at every prefix and composes sequential requirements exactly, with balanced traces of equal totals but different peaks as witnesses. This does not formalize the Komlós result or infer permission to reorder operations.

### Efficient constant-discrepancy Komlós construction

**Polynomial-time constructions announced, one with a polynomial bit-complexity claim; a checked partial solver exists, while an implementation of a proved construction remains open.** Ordinary Komlós controls the final signed sum. Following the September existence proofs, [Guo, Fang and Lu's 20 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23540v1) gives a deterministic constant-discrepancy signing algorithm using `O((m*n^9 + n^10) log(2+m+n))` exact arithmetic operations and comparisons for `n` vectors in `R^m`. This is a unit-cost real-RAM result, also yielding an `O(sqrt(t))` Beck-Fiala construction. The authors credit Odin AI assistance and describe their revision and verification of the proofs. [Bansal's 27 September exposition](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33215v1#S1) re-derives its spectral-potential argument for the Beck-Fiala case as a continuous process, treats the polynomial-time discretization as standard and sketches the Komlós extension; it discloses Astra 6.0 assistance in understanding the paper and with calculations. It is a simplified account, not a new bound or a complete discretized proof. The elementary proof's own finite rational construction is not the frontier for algorithmic existence.

**Rational bit-complexity claim, 24 September preprint.** [Xiaoyu Li, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30044v1), gives a deterministic signing with `||A*epsilon||_inf < 99` using `O(mn + n^(omega+2) log^3 n)` arithmetic operations and comparisons, for any fixed attainable matrix-multiplication exponent `omega`. Current exponent bounds give `O-tilde(mn + n^4.372)`; classical multiplication gives `O(mn + n^5 log^3 n)`. The algorithm uses matrix products, positive-definite inversion, field operations and comparisons, with no square roots or eigendecompositions. Its Proposition 11.4 asserts `poly(m,n,L_in)` bit complexity for rational input of encoding length `L_in`; the proof argues polynomial length for the persistent state and per-update data without an explicit bit bound. Corollary 1.2 gives Beck-Fiala discrepancy below `99*sqrt(t)`. The author discloses GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 use in developing and checking the analysis. The preprint has no implementation or experiments, and this review did not verify its proof.

**Faster construction and a checked partial solver, 29 September revision.** [Akbas and Sra, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27172v2#S3.SS1), building on Li's row screening, give a deterministic signing with discrepancy below `1877/50 = 37.54` using `O(n log^4(2+n))` walk moves and `O(mn) + O-tilde(n^4)` field operations and comparisons on real numbers; logarithms, exponentials, square roots and eigenvectors are approximated by finite procedures rather than taken as primitives. They bound no bit length for the states, which are rational for rational input, mark the algorithm's section as not yet carefully scrutinized and rewritten, and disclose extensive GPT-6 use. The proved step in the first phase at `m = n = 10^4` is `2^-114`, far too small for practice. Their [Algorithm 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27172v2#S5.SS1), implemented in the [companion repository at revision `88db0a69`](https://github.com/suvrit/komlos/tree/88db0a692951f6a12ad54717d610d7e3dab8abd6), is a separate checked routine: it tries eight seeded random signings, then a floating-point walk, and returns signs only when outward-rounded interval arithmetic certifies every row sum of the supplied binary64 matrix below `1877/50`, otherwise reporting failure. The [README](https://github.com/suvrit/komlos/blob/88db0a692951f6a12ad54717d610d7e3dab8abd6/README.md) states that the exact finite construction is not implemented, and no acceptance probability is proved. In the recorded [comparison](https://github.com/suvrit/komlos/blob/88db0a692951f6a12ad54717d610d7e3dab8abd6/results/comparison.md), all 48 matched cases, with `n` at most 1024, and 18 larger runs up to `n = 4096` accepted a random proposal. This review inspected the theorem statement, routine description, README and recorded comparison, without running the code or verifying the proofs.

This makes multidimensional balancing a stronger lead for the [offline composer](../implementation/placement-search.md). A reduction from actual legal assignments, finite constants, concrete bit and memory bounds, and an implementation of a proved construction still need to be established. A unit-cost real-RAM theorem alone does not supply polynomial bit complexity; a polynomial bit bound alone supplies neither a budget at this machine's instance sizes nor a feasible memory layout. A returned signing is checkable by computing `A*epsilon` directly; the algorithmic guarantees determine whether a producer succeeds within its budget, not whether its output is admitted. The checked routine follows that split: its interval check certifies the represented binary64 matrix, not an unrepresented real input, and without a proved acceptance probability or an implemented exact fallback it supplies no completion bound. The [formal existence artifact](#ordinary-komlós-and-beck-fiala-proof-announcements) also does not certify any follow-on algorithm's running time.

### Euclidean Steinitz conjecture

**Proof announcement with a supplied Lean development; conditional buffer connection.** Given vectors of Euclidean norm at most one whose total is zero in `R^d`, is there a permutation for which every partial sum has norm `O(sqrt(d))`, with a universal constant? Earlier [2026 work on Steinitz constants](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1112/mtk.70085) records `d - 1 + 1/d` as the bound for every symmetric norm and `sqrt(d+3)/2` as a Euclidean lower bound. As [Dutta, Jha and Jiang's April 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13355v1) records, Banaszczyk's nonconstructive `O(sqrt(d) + sqrt(log n))` bound for `n` vectors meets the conjecture when `d >= Omega(log n)`, and the previous algorithmic bound was `O(sqrt(d) log n)`. Their efficient construction attains `O(sqrt(d))` when `d >= Omega(log^7 n)`.

OpenAI's [family 097](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/097.md) claims the unrestricted `Theta(sqrt(d))` Euclidean bound, for both signed prefixes and zero-sum reordering. Its manuscript explicitly supplies neither an online rule nor an efficient algorithm. This review inspected the statement and supplied Lean theorem/configuration without replaying them. The existence claim deserves a status change here; a polynomial-time construction and legal dependency-preserving ordering remain separate needs. This dimension-dependent Euclidean bound does not contradict the reported refutation of a dimension-independent infinity-norm prefix bound.

Production and consumption operations can sometimes be modeled as signed resource vectors. A suitable ordering could reduce temporary inventories in [joint scheduling and memory planning](static-memory-research.md#certified-offline-placement-and-joint-scheduling). For a finite trace of `n` operations, existence is already within an additive `O(sqrt(log n))` of the conjectured bound; efficient construction and its constants matter more to a producer than the unrestricted conjecture. An arbitrary permutation is not necessarily executable: dependencies may prohibit it or it may consume unavailable data. The result would require a legal ordering, a baseline inventory, and translation of the norm bound into the exact capacities charged by admission.

### Three identical processors with unit jobs and precedence

**Claimed polynomial-time solution with a supplied Lean development; conditional static-scheduling connection.** For unit-duration jobs with arbitrary precedence constraints on exactly three identical processors, the classical `P3 | prec, p_j=1 | C_max` problem asks for minimum makespan. [Nederlof, Swennenhuis and Węgrzycki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03495), published in [ACM TALG in 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785365), make subexponential progress. OpenAI's [family 124](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/124.md) claims an exact deterministic polynomial-time algorithm. The inspected [formal statement](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/ComparatorChallenges/ThreeMachine.lean) specifies an explicit acyclic graph, unit slots with capacity three, precedence preservation, and a fixed finite machine with time bound `C*(input_length+2)^150020`. The [solution module](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Computability/Scheduling/Main.lean) supplies a proof of that statement; this review inspected its export and configuration without replay. The bound supports the claimed classification, not a practical runtime estimate.

Approximation is better understood. For any constant number `m` of identical machines, [Li's SODA 2021 algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01231) finds a `(1+epsilon)`-approximate makespan for unit jobs in `n^(O(m^4/epsilon^3 * log^3 log n))` time, which is not polynomial for fixed `m` and `epsilon`. When the machine count is part of the input, Graham's list scheduling achieves factor two and, as [Davies et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00111) summarize, Svensson shows that improving it is NP-hard under a Bansal-Khot variant of the Unique Games Conjecture. When each job's machine is fixed in advance, even constant-factor approximation is NP-hard; see [unique-machine precedence scheduling](#unique-machine-precedence-scheduling-and-communication-delays).

An efficient exact algorithm could improve [static schedules](../spec.md#r-07-013) for a matching restricted reaction graph; hardness would clarify scaling limits. At small fixed sizes, the approximation schemes' exponents give no evident advantage over checked exact search. Real workloads add varying WCET, communication, labels and recurring deadlines. Arbitrarily splitting a job into unit pieces would introduce preemption or migration that the original service may not permit.

OpenAI's [ordinary Unique Games claim, family 102](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/102.md), has a supplied Lean development whose theorem and configuration were inspected without replay. It does not by itself discharge the Bansal-Khot structural variant used in the scheduling bound above, or the separate Small Set Expansion conjecture.

### Unrelated-machine makespan approximation

**Open approximation problem; conditional heterogeneous-core connection.** Each job has a processing time `p_ij` on machine `i`; assign each job to one machine to minimize the greatest total assigned processing time. The general polynomial-time approximation frontier has a factor-two algorithm and hardness below `3/2`. Improving the general factor-two guarantee and identifying the optimal threshold remain unresolved; see [the 2025 scheduling study](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A0e0229d0-ca8a-473f-955d-a75a5ebaae66/files/r3b591b19x) and [2026 work](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13133v1). In the restricted-assignment special case, each job has one processing time on every compatible machine; [Jansen and Rohwedder](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07208v2) give a `1.8334`-approximation in quasi-polynomial time, and this review located no polynomial-time guarantee below two for that case.

This resembles assigning compatible work to [scalar, vector and matrix cores](../implementation/compute-compatibility.md), so progress could reduce the most heavily loaded core or required core count. Restricted assignment matches only when every compatible core runs a job at the same admitted cost. Fixed-machine-count special cases already have stronger algorithms. Neither the general ratio nor a prediction-assisted result covers the platform's full dependency, slot, label and fabric constraints.

[Bernstein, Gupta and Wang's 11 July 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10075) gives polylogarithmic-round CONGEST algorithms with `1+epsilon` fractional and `2+epsilon` integral guarantees for fully unrelated processing times. This advances distributed computation; it does not improve the general centralized factor-two guarantee.

### Pinwheel scheduling certificates and complexity

**Open complexity questions; conditional repetitive-service connection.** Task `i` needs one unit of service in every `a_i` consecutive slots, forever, on one server. Does every feasible instance have a polynomial-size certificate checkable in polynomial time, placing feasibility in NP? Is general feasibility PSPACE-complete? [ICALP 2026 research](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.122/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.122.html) identifies these unresolved questions. A periodic schedule can exist without an explicitly written period having polynomial length.

Compact certificates could help express maintenance or replenishment cadences without enormous schedule tables. This model omits WCET variation and the complete [schedule contract](../implementation/contracts/schedule-record.md). [Kobayashi, Lin and Swernofsky's 17 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20075v1) strengthens the hardness frontier: dense instances with `sum_i 1/a_i = 1` are NP-complete even for unary periods and explicitly listed repeated tasks. Hence general feasibility is strongly NP-hard. The authors state that GPT-5.5 initially found the proof, which they checked and revised. NP membership for the dense restriction does not establish NP membership for general feasibility; its certificate-size and PSPACE-completeness questions remain open.

**Kernel Conjecture counterexample, 1 October GitHub artifact.** [Gąsieniec, Smith and Wild's Conjecture 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01784v1) asserted that every schedulable instance `A` with sorted periods `a_1 <= ... <= a_k` admits a schedulable `A' <= A`, componentwise, whose `k`-th period is at most `2^(k-1)`; such an `A'` is stricter, so its schedules also serve `A`. Their Proposition 2.4 makes this equivalent to their conjecture that every `k`-task instance with a periodic schedule containing an idle slot has one with an idle slot at least every `2^k` slots. The [repository at revision `08f48090`](https://github.com/MathIsEvenEasier/pinwheel-kernel-counterexample/tree/08f48090c39bf665a0bce7a044e7fcab001f8e17) shows `(3,4,5,20,22,36)` schedulable by a 36-slot periodic word, while `(3,4,5,20,22,b)` is unschedulable for every `b <= 35`, so no such `A'` meets the bound `2^5 = 32`. Its [Lean statements](https://github.com/MathIsEvenEasier/pinwheel-kernel-counterexample/blob/08f48090c39bf665a0bce7a044e7fcab001f8e17/lean/Result.lean) quantify over every infinite schedule meeting every window, including the initial ones. The README reports an external build and a nanoda replay depending only on `propext` and `Quot.sound`, and attributes the discovery, certificates and formalization to GPT-6 Astra under human direction. This review inspected the [model definitions](https://github.com/MathIsEvenEasier/pinwheel-kernel-counterexample/blob/08f48090c39bf665a0bce7a044e7fcab001f8e17/lean/Pinwheel.lean) and theorem statements without building them. An independent [exact state-graph check](pinwheel-evidence/README.md) agrees: from the all-zero age state, the 56,284 reachable states for `b = 35` and the 50,881 for `b = 32`, matching the certificate sizes, admit no infinite legal path, while `b = 36` admits one. Capping periods at `2^(k-1)` therefore keeps produced schedules valid but can reject feasible instances. The counterexample removes a conjectured bound on the periods a decision procedure must consider; the NP-membership and PSPACE-completeness questions remain open.

### Small Set Expansion conjecture

**Hardness conjecture; direct connection to limits on memory optimization.** Roughly, for arbitrarily small fixed error there is a sufficiently small fixed set-size fraction for which it is NP-hard to distinguish a regular graph containing a set of that size with almost no escaping edges from one where every set of that size has almost all edges escaping. [Raghavendra, Steurer and Tulsiani](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.2586) state the formal hypothesis and its relation to Unique Games.

Assuming it, [Austrin, Pitassi and Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.4910) derive constant-factor inapproximability for several layout problems and one-shot pebbling, where intermediates must be retained until their consumers run and recomputation is forbidden. This is a concrete barrier relevant to [lifetime and execution-order optimization](static-memory-research.md). It motivates restricted graph families and checked instance-specific search. It says neither that all real workloads are hard nor that ordinary fixed-lifetime contiguous placement has exactly the same lower bound. Disproving the conjecture alone would not provide a better planner.

### Strongly polynomial linear programming

**Open algorithmic problem with a live solution claim, associated with Smale's ninth problem; direct offline-optimization connection.** Can every rational linear program with `m` constraints and `n` variables be solved using `poly(m,n)` arithmetic operations, independent of coefficient bit lengths, while all intermediate numbers have polynomially bounded encoding size? Ordinary polynomial-time solvability is already established. [Dadush et al.'s STACS 2025 account](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.2) states the general frontier and the solved case with at most two nonzero entries per row or column.

Such an algorithm could improve predictable search budgets for packing relaxations, resource assignments and lower-bound certificates used by the [planner](../implementation/portable-memory-planner.md). It would not make arbitrary integer placement strongly polynomial or eliminate rounding gaps. Bit operations, finite arithmetic and target memory still cost resources. [Natura's February 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06958v2) proves short monotone circuit walks through polyhedra; finding them within the required algorithmic bound remains open.

[Awoniyi's claimed general solution, revision 10 of 4 July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12041v10), claims at most `2(k+n)` complementary-pivot iterations for `k` constraints and `n` variables. This review inspected the primal-dual formulation and algorithm description, but did not independently establish correctness, termination or intermediate bit-size bounds. No counterexample is asserted; the live claim remains unresolved in this review.

### List Edge-Coloring Conjecture

**Named conjecture; conditional flexibility in static slot assignment.** Is the list chromatic index of every loopless multigraph equal to its ordinary chromatic index? If `k` colors suffice with a common palette, the conjecture says that giving each edge any list of `k` permitted colors still suffices. A [June 2026 survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31702) discusses the open general case and known cases such as bipartite graphs; [Jafari's 16 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22895v2) proves the stronger online form for `K_(p-1)` and `K_(2p)` for odd primes `p`, plus specified matching-deletion families. It does not settle arbitrary loopless multigraphs.

For unit transfers whose conflicts are exactly shared endpoints, edges can represent transfers and colors can represent time slots. Lists express allowed slots, so a constructive resolution could support more flexible [static schedule synthesis](../implementation/contracts/schedule-record.md). Routed fabric conflicts, unequal durations, precedence and additional shared resources do not automatically fit this graph. A bipartite endpoint model already has a theorem and need not await the general conjecture. The related ordinary edge-coloring bound is now a [published result](#goldberg-seymour-and-the-number-of-transfer-rounds).

### Unique-machine precedence scheduling and communication delays

**Open approximation problem with a July 2026 hardness advance; direct fixed-core scheduling connection.** In Unique-Machine Precedence Scheduling, UMPS, every job is assigned in advance to one machine; the scheduler chooses only each machine's nonpreemptive order, subject to an arbitrary precedence DAG, to minimize makespan. [Davies et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00111) introduced the problem, conjectured NP-hardness of approximation within `n^epsilon` even for unit jobs, and reported that job-shop and LP-hierarchy techniques give no nontrivial guarantee. [Guruswami, Ren and Tang's 29 July preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26590v1) proves that unit-length UMPS is NP-hard to approximate within any constant factor and, unless NP has quasi-polynomial-time algorithms, within `(log n)^gamma` for some constant `gamma > 0`. Through known reductions, the same bounds hold for unit jobs on identical machines with non-uniform job-to-job or job-to-machine communication delays. Whether unit UMPS admits a polylogarithmic approximation or is `n^epsilon`-hard remains open.

With one uniform delay `c` between dependent jobs on different machines, whether a constant-factor approximation exists is a top-ten open question in scheduling theory. [Davies, Kulkarni, Rothvoss, Tarnawski and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09682v1) give `O(log c * log m)` for `m` machines; [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02770v2) give `O(ln c / ln ln c)` in near-linear time when jobs may be duplicated. This review located no constant-factor algorithm.

A composition that fixes each partition's core, as [`ModeSchedule`](../implementation/contracts/schedule-record.md) visits do, leaves exactly this ordering problem for dependent work across cores, and fabric latency between dependent visits resembles a communication delay. Unless `P = NP`, no polynomial-time producer can promise a constant-factor makespan for arbitrary dependency graphs, even with unit jobs. This motivates restricted dependency families, exact search for small compositions and the existing independent admission checks. It shows neither that a particular composition is hard nor that a checked schedule is poor.

These are makespan results for one acyclic instance, not cyclic frames with recurring deadlines. Unit jobs, a uniform delay and interchangeable machines are premises that a positive algorithm's transfer must match. Duplication re-executes a job, changing its effects, authority and WCET accounting, and fabric grants are shared resources whose conflicts the delay models omit.

## Proofs, verification and compilation

### P versus NP

**Foundational conjecture, usually stated `P != NP`; direct connection.** Can every decision problem with polynomial-size, polynomial-time-checkable witnesses also be decided in deterministic polynomial time? [Clay's official problem page](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/) continues to list it as unsolved.

A constructive equality could transform exact finite packing, schedule synthesis, bounded superoptimization and proof search with an explicit polynomial witness bound. A separation would establish worst-case limits. Input encoding matters: a numerically enormous schedule is not a short certificate merely because its duration has few binary digits. Polynomial algorithms can also have unusable constants or exponents.

Neither answer decides unrestricted termination, arbitrary program equivalence or every theorem. `P != NP` is not a proof of cryptographic one-wayness; `P = NP` would defeat the usual asymptotic one-way-function definition. The [resident toolchain](../spec.md#r-13-027) would still produce artifacts checked by existing admission mechanisms.

**Concrete proof-claim audit.** A [June 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03194v1) claims a machine-verified equality, but its [Lean target at revision `3c9c90ed`](https://github.com/TiruArt/Pedigree-Polytopes-Lean4/blob/3c9c90ed2e38dd3a679891029c8e7622b5801988/MembershipProject/Core/N_PEqualsNP.lean) declares `P_equals_NP : Prop` without defining the standard classes, sets `An n := Unit`, and assumes bridges including `tardos_strongly_polynomial`, `membership_An_of_Pn` and `mi_objective_solves_stsp_ax`. Source inspection therefore does not establish the advertised theorem. These are statement and assumption gaps, independent of the author's identity or the repository's age; the proof package was not built here.

A [July PolaritySAT claim at revision `9564eba5`](https://github.com/yinhao2026/P-NP--Complete-Archive/blob/9564eba543080e3ad4f08a16663174f64e45d8e5/README.md) has a direct counterexample to its displayed skeleton lemma. It defines a globally unified variable by a unique polarity across clauses shared with higher-index variables. For `x < y`, the satisfiable formula `(x OR y) AND (NOT x OR NOT y)` has no unit clause or pure variable: `x` occurs with both polarities beside `y`, and `y` has no higher variable, so both polarities satisfy its empty condition rather than a unique one. The claim that the largest variable must be pure also fails. This checks the stated argument, without running the claimed solver; it supplies no resolution of P versus NP.

A [September Lean repository at revision `4e088344`](https://github.com/zhengbojin/mp/tree/4e088344d91cc9871bf8bdd416b13a8d0396269d) proves `P_sym0_neq_NP_sym0` for classes of `List F4` languages defined by its own CBTM/IVM machine model. Its [README](https://github.com/zhengbojin/mp/blob/4e088344d91cc9871bf8bdd416b13a8d0396269d/README.md) calls the separation framework-internal and states that it cannot be proved in the classical framework alone. Source inspection of that scope statement and the theorem declaration therefore finds no claim about standard P versus NP, whatever the build reports. All three audited revisions remained the latest public commits of their repositories on 3 October 2026.

### NP versus coNP and short propositional proofs

**Foundational conjecture, usually stated `NP != coNP`; direct certificate connection.** Is there a sound, complete, polynomial-time-checkable propositional proof system in which every tautology has a proof polynomial in its formula length? Existence of such a polynomially bounded system is equivalent to `NP = coNP`, by [Cook and Reckhow](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf). Strong proof-size lower bounds remain a subject of [current research](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/080/download/).

Equality could permit uniformly compact certificates for finite Boolean validity or unsatisfiability. It would not promise efficient discovery, a compact encoding of an entire physical system, or short proofs in this repository's particular logic. Inequality would mean that every such propositional system has hard families. This matters to proof artifact storage and checking budgets, while [the proof kernel](../assurance/cic-checker-qualification.md) remains responsible for each accepted claim.

[De Rezende et al., May 2026](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/078/), accepted at [FOCS 2026](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/), prove superpolynomial lower bounds for tree-like semantic Frege with bounded line size and related bounded-degree threshold systems. Tree structure and line restrictions are essential: this is proof-system progress, not a lower bound covering every Cook-Reckhow system.

[Hakoniemi, Limaye and Tzameret, May 2026](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/070/), also accepted at FOCS 2026, give the first CNF lower bounds for `roABP-IPS_LIN`, the Ideal Proof System fragment whose Nullstellensatz coefficient polynomials are read-once oblivious algebraic branching programs. Earlier IPS lower bounds concerned non-Boolean algebraic instances. Checking a general IPS refutation requires polynomial identity testing, so IPS is not known to be a Cook-Reckhow system. The fragment result says nothing about Frege, Extended Frege or CIC certificates.

[Li, Ren and Zhong's 20 September 2026 preprint](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/196/) starts from a demi-bits generator, for which a proof system cannot efficiently prove `y` outside the range for any noticeable fraction of strings `y`. A random subset of its output bits is then, with constant probability, a proof-complexity generator, for which no such statement has an efficient proof. This is a conversion of one hardness property into another; applications retain generator, LPN or random-SAT assumptions. It neither supplies an unconditional hard family for every proof system nor a lower bound on this project's exported CIC corpus.

**Resolution over parities, September 2026 preprints.** In Res(⊕), proof lines are disjunctions of affine equations over `F_2`. [Braun's 19 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23015v1) records superpolynomial lower bounds previously only for tree-like, regular or bounded-depth refutations; its Theorem 1.1 proves that every DAG-like Res(⊕) refutation of the bit pigeonhole principle with `n+1` pigeons and `n = 2^ell` holes, `ell >= 32`, has more than `exp(n/(32768*ell^2))` clauses. The author reports substantial assistance from Claude and GPT models. The paper's [Lean theorem at revision `8904bf09`](https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/blob/8904bf09a5376f48a00d1c25d079bf0c6441502a/formalization/claims/BitPHPExponential.lean) states this bound for refutation DAGs over the pigeon-collision clauses; its [rule definitions](https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/blob/8904bf09a5376f48a00d1c25d079bf0c6441502a/formalization/claims/AffineDAGRegistry.lean) also admit semantic weakening and any sound inference from two earlier lines, so the formal statement covers syntactic refutations a fortiori. Source inspection found no `sorry`, `axiom` or `native_decide` in those files; this review did not build the package or audit its imports. [Pang's 30 September preprint, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00837v1), adapts Braun's common-multiplier idea: for every constant-width CNF, linear polynomial-calculus degree over a prime field `F_p` forces exponential DAG-like size in resolution over constant-degree polynomials over `F_p`, hence in the mod-`p` analogue Res(⊕_p). Its stated families include random constant-width CNFs at constant clause density, with high probability, and the bit pigeonhole principle at `exp(Omega(N))` for `N` holes. Pang discloses that ChatGPT-6 models proposed essentially the main arguments, which the author reconstructed. The paper derives superpolynomial `AC^0[p]`-Frege lower bounds only from very strong, unproved polynomial-calculus degree bounds. These bounds concern systems just above resolution: a certificate format built from parity-clause inferences has explicit families with no short refutation, while CIC proofs and stronger Cook-Reckhow systems are not covered.

### A p-optimal propositional proof system

**Open existence question; conditional certificate-interface connection.** Does one Cook-Reckhow proof system polynomially simulate every other, with polynomial-time translations and bounds allowed to depend on the source system? [Egidy's 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02294v1) studies barriers around this unresolved question; [Krajíček](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04711) connects it to optimal proof search.

Egidy's oracle constructions rule out relativizing proofs of either answer even with specified polynomial-hierarchy behavior. Independence in those oracle settings is not independence from the axioms of the project's logic and supplies no universal certificate translator.

A constructive positive answer could inform a common representation for certificates from heterogeneous solvers. This is relative efficiency, not a promise that every theorem has a short proof. It is also not the weaker notion of an optimal system that only bounds translated proof length without requiring an efficient translation. The [portable proof workflow](../assurance/proof-assistance.md) would still need concrete reconstruction, assumption auditing and a qualified checker; the conjecture authorizes none of those changes by itself.

### P versus PSPACE

**Foundational conjecture, usually stated `P != PSPACE`; direct finite-state connection.** Can every problem decidable with polynomial working space also be decided in polynomial time? It remains unresolved. [Williams's 2025 time-to-space simulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17779v1) is a major partial result, not an equality or separation of these classes.

Some reachability and temporal-verification problems over succinctly represented finite machines are PSPACE-complete: the state graph can be exponentially larger than its description. Equality could change the cost of exhaustive assurance for those models; separation would establish limits while leaving restricted methods useful. A finite deployed machine does not make its entire graph affordable to enumerate. Conversely, infinite-state undecidability should not be attributed to a fixed finite configuration. No general Turing-machine simulation supplies the target memory traffic, slowdown or WCET needed for this project's [assurance pipeline](../assurance/coverage-matrix.md).

### P versus uniform NC

**Foundational conjecture, usually stated `P != NC`; conditional parallelism connection.** Does every polynomial-time decision problem admit uniform polynomial-size circuits of polylogarithmic depth, equivalently a suitable highly parallel algorithm with polynomially many processors? See the [University of Illinois complexity lecture](https://www.cs.uic.edu/~block/courses/cs505-spring2025/lecture-18.html). The processor count and uniformity condition are essential.

A constructive equality could expand parallel compilation and analysis. A separation would establish that some efficient sequential computations resist this strong parallelization target. Neither predicts speedup on the finite number of cores and fixed communication grants of [this ensemble design](../spec.md#r-15-171a). Many useful restricted tasks are already parallelizable: [Ganardi and Lohrey](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19060v1) distinguish efficient parallel register planning for explicit expression trees from harder succinct representations. Ordinary register planning need not wait for a resolution.

**June-July 2026 preprint advance.** [Chatterjee et al., revision 2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/100/revision/2/download/), give deterministic NC algorithms for bipartite matching, including finding a maximum-weight perfect matching for polynomially bounded edge weights, extend weighted/search results to linear matroid intersection, and compute the noncommutative rank of a symbolic matrix in NC. [Kopparty and Saraf's 15 July note](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/119/) gives a simpler-to-state variant of its perfect-matching detection criterion with improved parameters. This is a concrete lead for parallel offline assignments that reduce to these problems. Processor count, memory, field/weight representation and checking the resulting assignment still matter; the general `P = NC` question is unchanged. The same algorithm yields a uniform [modular-counting containment for directed reachability](#l-versus-nl-directed-reachability-in-logarithmic-workspace).

**August-September followups.** [Xiao's 30 August preprint](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/162/) announces a finite-field extension placing perfect-matching existence and maximum-weight threshold decisions in `Mod_p L`, with polynomially bounded nonnegative integer weights. This is a modular-counting class, not a deterministic-logspace witness algorithm. [Lakhani and Mukhopadhyay's 17 September preprint](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/185/) gives a deterministic `NC^3` algorithm that, for fixed `0 < epsilon < 1`, selects a matrix from a rational matrix space with rank at least `(1-epsilon)` times its maximum rank. Approximate commutative rank must not be substituted for exact rank or general PIT. The [resident-pass comparison](../performance/toolchain-residency.md#research-leads-for-bounded-resident-passes) owns possible implementations; no target speedup is measured here.

### L versus NL: directed reachability in logarithmic workspace

**Open complexity question; direct verification-memory connection.** Can reachability between two vertices of an explicitly represented directed graph be decided deterministically with `O(log n)` writable workspace? This is equivalent to `L = NL`; read-only input storage is excluded from the space bound. [Jeffery and Pass, ICALP 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.117/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.117.html), record the unresolved classical question. Undirected reachability already has a logarithmic-space algorithm.

A constructive answer could reduce scratch reservations for dependency analysis and graph-based checking in the [resident toolchain](../spec.md#r-13-027). It would not shrink an implicitly exponential state graph, remove input storage, or guarantee near-linear time. The cited paper's new quantum result is not an algorithm for this classical machine. The relevant target is a complete classical implementation with explicit time and memory bounds.

**Intermediate time-space frontier.** Even a deterministic algorithm combining polynomial time with ordinary workspace `O(n^(1-epsilon))`, for some fixed `epsilon > 0`, remains open for general directed reachability. [Pyne and Tell's 2026 account, section 3.3.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/045/download), contrasts the known polynomial-time, `O(n / 2^sqrt(log n))`-space algorithm with Savitch's `O(log^2 n)`-space algorithm, whose runtime is superpolynomial. A polynomial-factor scratch reduction would already be useful for resident graph passes. Input backing still counts toward the machine's capacity, and a catalytic clean-space bound does not establish this ordinary-workspace target.

**Uniform modular-counting containment, July 2026 preprint.** [Applebaum's note, revised 10 August](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/131/), uses the [NC bipartite-matching algorithm](#p-versus-uniform-nc) to transform every nondeterministic branching program, in deterministic logspace, into an equivalent polynomial-size parity branching program. This proves that `NL` is contained in parity logspace `ParityL`, and in `Mod_p L` for every fixed prime `p`; Wigderson had proved only the nonuniform version. Directed reachability therefore reduces in logspace to deciding whether a constructed graph has a nonzero number of source-to-sink paths modulo `p`. Whether `ParityL` is contained in `L` is also open, so this supplies no logarithmic-space reachability algorithm and no workspace reduction for a resident pass.

### The power of catalytic memory

**Open computational-model questions; direct allocation-flexibility connection.** A catalytic computation has clean scratch space plus writable storage initially holding an arbitrary string, which it must restore exactly on termination. `CL` allows `O(log n)` clean workspace and polynomially many catalytic bits. Does `CL = L`? Does every polynomial-time problem belong to `CL`? [Koucky's account](https://bulletin.eatcs.org/index.php/beatcs/article/download/400/380) and [Henzinger, Pyne and Ragavan's February 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14320v2) discuss the unresolved power of this model.

This explores whether already occupied memory can help compute without losing its original information. It could reduce additional scratch reservations inside one owner's authorized, quiescent region. It does not reduce physical capacity charged to composition, authorize cross-label access, preserve concurrent reads, or guarantee restoration after faults or forced termination. Restoring raw bits also needs a separate proof for capability tags, initialization and typed-object invariants. Compress-or-random algorithms branch on whether the catalyst is compressible, so their running time and access pattern can depend on the borrowed contents wherever timing is observable. [Recent explicit algorithms](#catalytic-graph-and-sequence-algorithms) make this more concrete than an existence question, while leaving those implementation obligations intact.

[Koucký, Mertz and Sami's May 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09648v1) characterizes models whose own restoration may be imperfect: randomized machines that may destroy the catalyst with some probability or leave a bounded expected number of wrong bits, and deterministic machines whose expected errors are bounded over initial catalyst contents. These are errors the algorithm is permitted to make, not recovery from an external fault or forced termination; an owner requiring exact payload bytes cannot accept them.

### Minimum Circuit Size Problem

**Open complexity classification; conditional synthesis connection.** Given the complete `2^n`-bit truth table of a Boolean function and a threshold `s`, is there a circuit of at most `s` gates computing it? MCSP is in NP, but membership in P and NP-hardness under standard polynomial-time reductions remain unresolved. [Hirahara and Ilango, FOCS 2025](https://www.rahulilango.com/papers/MCSP-Proceedings-2025.pdf), and [Goldberg, Juvekar and Kabanets, June 2026](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/091/), establish conditional results rather than unconditional NP-completeness of ordinary MCSP.

The first result uses deterministic quasipolynomial-time nonadaptive reductions under cryptographic and circuit-hardness assumptions. The June result concerns a gap version of **implicit** MCSP, with randomized half-Levin reductions under subexponentially secure obfuscation and an assumption excluding infinitely-often subexponentially optimal proof systems. Input representation, reduction type and assumptions are part of these claims, not removable implementation details.

Constructive progress could aid minimization of small finite Boolean kernels or candidate hardware circuits. The input is the entire truth table, so polynomial time here can still be exponential in the number of Boolean inputs. The partial-function variant, whose table may leave some inputs unspecified, is already NP-hard under randomized polynomial-time reductions ([Hirahara, FOCS 2022](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/119/)). Exact minimization with don't-care inputs therefore has no polynomial-time algorithm unless `NP` is contained in `BPP`, whatever ordinary MCSP's status. This is not a universal efficient optimizer for source programs or succinct RTL. Gate count also differs from delay, energy and proof cost; the [certifying compute path](../implementation/compute-compatibility.md) still needs semantic equivalence and target measurements.

### Derandomization: P equals BPP

**Named complexity conjecture; conditional toolchain connection.** Every bounded-error randomized polynomial-time decision procedure would have a deterministic polynomial-time counterpart. [Doron, Moshkovitz, Oh and Zuckerman's 2026 work](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/082/) continues to study the hardness assumptions and overheads of such derandomization.

Useful constructions could remove random choices and probabilistic error from some offline search and analysis, helping reproducibility and deterministic resource accounting. They need not preserve the original runtime exponent. Untrusted randomized search can already be safe when its output is independently checked. Derandomization does not generate secret entropy, replace a physical entropy root, or establish constant-time handling of secrets. Its benefit belongs to the [composer and proof producer](../spec.md#r-13-001c), not to a relaxation of admission.

[Chattopadhyay et al.'s 24 September 2026 preprint](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/208/) gives exponential correlation bounds against low-degree `F_2` polynomials, with polylogarithmic-seed pseudorandom generators for those polynomials and alternating circuits with parity gates. This advances restricted derandomization; it is not a generator for every polynomial-time verifier or a proof that `P = BPP`.

**Space-bounded analogue with a solution claim.** Whether every bounded-error randomized logspace decision procedure has a deterministic logspace counterpart, `BPL = L`, is addressed by OpenAI's [family 103 manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Exact-Derandomization-of-Logarithmic-Space-L-equals-RL-equals-BPL-September-23-2026/build/sections/introduction.tex). It claims `L = RL = BPL` and an effective deterministic logspace, polynomial-time simulation given valid bounds for a randomized polynomial-time logspace machine. This review inspected the model and statement; the release catalogue supplies no Lean link. The prior general simulation of randomized space `S` uses `O(S^(3/2)/sqrt(log S))` space ([Hoza, RANDOM 2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2021.28)). [Hoza and Shalunov's 17 September 2026 preprint](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/184/) gives a barrier to a particular spectral-expansion analysis of the INW generator, not a barrier to every derandomization. A usable implementation still needs its source bounds, constants, input storage and target timing charged. This claim settles neither `P = BPP` nor `L = NL`, and supplies no secret entropy. [Pass composition](#time-space-cost-of-composing-low-space-passes) gives a separate conditional route to average-case `BPL` derandomization.

### Deterministic polynomial-time polynomial identity testing

**Open algorithmic problem; conditional rewrite-verification connection.** Given an arithmetic circuit over an appropriately represented field, decide whether the formal polynomial it computes is identically zero in deterministic polynomial time, with circuit, degree and field-operation costs specified. General derandomization remains open in [2026 PIT research](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/076/download/).

This could improve exact algebraic simplification and validation of candidate [vector and matrix kernels](../implementation/compute-compatibility.md), reducing dependence on randomized fingerprints. It is narrower than all of `P = BPP`. Formal polynomial equality is not floating-point equivalence, equal trap order, equal memory effects, or equality of machine arithmetic with overflow. Any use in the certifying toolchain needs the actual semantics and the existing theorem-checking path.

The cited [Kaplan-Shpilka result](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/076/), published at [CCC 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.25), gives deterministic polynomial-time white-box and quasipolynomial-time black-box tests for non-multilinear read-4 formulas over characteristic zero or at least five. [Garg, Oliveira, Sengupta, Shalmon and Shpilka, CCC 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.17), bound the rank of minimal `Sigma^k Pi Sigma Pi^2` identities by a function of the top fan-in `k` over characteristic zero; this yields the first deterministic polynomial-time black-box test for these depth-4 circuits with constant `k` and bottom-level polynomials of degree at most two. The formula, depth, fan-in and field restrictions identify usable special cases without resolving arbitrary-circuit PIT.

**AI-assisted September preprint advance.** [Guo's 6 September note](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/168/), crediting an LLM-suggested use of Mason-Stothers, removes squarefreeness and homogeneity restrictions for depth-4 `Sigma^[3] Pi Sigma Pi^[delta]` circuits in `n` variables: at most three product summands, bottom-factor degree bounded by constant `delta`, and each product's degree bounded by `d`. Its [Corollary 2.7](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/168/download) gives a deterministic black-box hitting set of size `(nd)^O_delta(1)` over arbitrary characteristic. Evaluation points may lie in an extension field, and the running-time claim uses the prior algebraic cost model. The statement and these conventions were inspected, without independently verifying the complete proof. The [arithmetic handoff](../implementation/contracts/compute-semantic.md#arithmetic-research-handoff) needs a matching exact representation and separately charged field/bit costs; no machine-arithmetic equivalence follows from the formal identity alone.

**Characteristic-zero preprint advance, 28 September.** [Bhardwaj and Saptharishi, Theorem 1.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/217/), give an explicit univariate substitution of degree `O(n*r^2)` that keeps nonzero every `n`-variate polynomial whose space of partial derivatives of all orders has dimension at most `r`, over any characteristic-zero field. For degree `d`, this yields a black-box hitting set of size `poly(n,d,r)`, and hence of size `poly(n,d,s)` for size-`s` depth-3 powering circuits, sums of powers of linear forms, whose best previous hitting sets the paper cites at `n^O(log log n)`. The authors state that the proof was obtained in conversations with OpenAI GPT-6 Astra and rewritten in their words. This review inspected the statement and its cited prior bounds, not the proof. Characteristic zero excludes the finite fields of modular machine and cryptographic arithmetic, and bounded partial-derivative dimension is a strong restriction on the circuit class.

**Noncommutative-formula claims in the OpenAI collection.** Family 116 supplies matrix hitting constructions across characteristics and for rational formulas. Its [formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/116.md) distinguishes the characteristic-zero universal hitting theorem, whose selected formal statement omits the construction-time and dimension bounds, from the rational-formula theorem carrying polynomial output, dimension and bit-time bounds. The positive-characteristic manuscript is not listed among that page's formalized papers. This review inspected the catalogue and scope, without replay. These are possible exact-algebraic validation leads after a representation match; they do not settle general commutative arithmetic-circuit PIT or floating-point equivalence.

### Deterministic polynomial factorization over finite fields

**General algorithmic question with a prime-field solution claim; conditional algebraic-toolchain connection.** Given a dense degree-`d` polynomial over `F_q`, can its irreducible factors and multiplicities be computed deterministically in `poly(d,log q)` bit operations? Supply the field explicitly, for example as `F_p[t]/(h)` with binary-encoded prime `p` and an irreducible polynomial `h`; constructing that representation is a separate task. Efficient randomized algorithms exist. [Chatterjee, Harsha and Kumar's March 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05176v2) distinguishes the then-open general problem, even quadratics over varying prime fields, from the structured instances it solves for Reed-Solomon decoding. [Altman's September 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12705v1) gives an unconditional deterministic algorithm factoring the reductions of an irreducible integer polynomial modulo every prime up to a bound `N`, with average time per prime polynomial in the input size and the degree of the polynomial's splitting field; for Galois polynomials this is amortized polynomial time. Amortization over all small primes does not factor one polynomial over one large, independently chosen field.

OpenAI's [4 October prime-field manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Deterministic-Polynomial-Factorization-over-Prime-Fields-October-4-2026/build/main.tex), family 142, claims complete deterministic factorization, including multiplicities, in `poly((d+1)*log p)` bit operations for dense input over `F_p`. It uses a companion uniform Hecke zero-free theorem and claims no GRH, primitive-root oracle or integer-factorization oracle. The inspected catalogue and manuscript abstract supply no Lean formalization link. This directly addresses the prime-field frontier; transfer to the explicit extension-field representation above needs its own reduction and cost account. Polynomial factorization is not integer factoring or a break of lattice cryptography.

Progress could make future field/code construction and exact algebraic preprocessing reproducible with stronger runtime bounds. This is a prospective connection to [arithmetic generation](../implementation/fiat-crypto-emission.md), not an identified bottleneck in the pinned primitives. GRH alone is not a known solution of the unrestricted factorization problem; [Ivanyos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5653) explain the remaining GRH-conditional frontier. Factorization of polynomials over finite fields is also distinct from factoring integers and from proving cryptographic hardness.

### Tree Evaluation in logarithmic space

**Open complexity question; direct recomputation connection.** A Tree Evaluation instance is a complete binary tree of height `h` whose leaves hold `ell`-bit strings and whose internal nodes hold functions from two `ell`-bit child values to one `ell`-bit value; the task is to compute the root value, with input length `n = 2^Theta(h+ell)`. Can it be solved in deterministic `O(log n)` workspace? [Cook and Mertz, SICOMP 2025](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1690126), use `O(ell + h log ell)` space, which is `O(log n * log log n)`; [Goldreich's refinement](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/124/revision/1/download/) reaches `o(log n * log log n)`. [Henzinger, Pyne and Ragavan's February 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14320v2) note the Cook-Mertz running time `n^O(log log n)` and give a polynomial-time catalytic algorithm with `O(log n)` clean workspace and `2^(log^epsilon n)` catalytic bits for each fixed `epsilon > 0`. No logarithmic-space algorithm or impossibility proof was located; the [withdrawn April catalytic-pebbling claim](#catalytic-graph-and-sequence-algorithms) supplies neither. Because bottom-up evaluation takes polynomial time, an impossibility proof would separate `L` from `P`.

[Williams](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17779v1) reduces time-`t` multitape computation to Tree Evaluation instances and observes that a logarithmic-space algorithm would place `TIME[t]` in `SPACE[sqrt(t)]`, removing the logarithmic factor from the [square-root simulation](#square-root-space-simulation-and-circuit-evaluation); the catalytic algorithm already improves his simulation of time by catalytic space. Expression, dataflow and certificate evaluation in a resident pass have the same bottom-up shape. For DAGs, [Sibgatullin and Williams, Theorem 2.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/223/), evaluate any gate of an `n`-gate circuit whose single-bit gates have fan-in at most `b` and, with their wiring, are computable in `O(b + log n)` space, using `O(D + b + log n)` space; the normalized depth `D` sums `ceil(log2(in-degree + 1))` along the worst path to that gate, and Tree Evaluation instances recover the Cook-Mertz bound. Depth-first evaluation retains about `h*ell` bits of pending child values, whereas these algorithms recompute subtrees through an algebraic encoding.

The deterministic algorithms trade that storage for superpolynomial time, and the catalytic algorithm inherits every [restoration obligation](#the-power-of-catalytic-memory). A transfer needs node operations evaluable within the workspace bound, an output-equivalence proof for the recomputing evaluator and explicit time. The complexity question supplies no bounded activation or target timing for the [resident-pass comparison](../performance/toolchain-residency.md#research-leads-for-bounded-resident-passes).

## Computational performance and Wasm workloads

These entries concern algorithmic work performed by applications, native services, or offline producers. They are not conjectures that every Wasm interpreter can match native execution. The [selected execution contract](../implementation/contracts/wasm-execution.md) already provides the place to study validated internal representations, fused handlers, bounded whole-loop operations and native-service calls, with effects, traps, authority and polling preserved.

### Exponential Time Hypothesis and Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis

**Related hardness hypotheses; direct limits on exact search.** ETH says that 3-SAT has no `2^o(n)`-time algorithm in its number of variables. SETH makes the stronger quantitative assertion that for every fixed improvement below base two, some fixed clause width prevents that improved exponential runtime for `k`-SAT. See the [original exponential-complexity research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000000917276) and [SETH formulations and consequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2275). The deterministic or randomized algorithm model must be stated when using a consequence.

These hypotheses constrain some exact scheduling, packing and finite verification methods more sharply than `P != NP`; [2026 high-multiplicity bin-packing research](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.116/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.116.html) illustrates such a connection. A refutation could improve exponential search without proving `P = NP`. A transfer to the [memory planner](../implementation/portable-memory-planner.md) needs a reduction preserving the relevant size parameter, not just an NP-hardness label.

A [20 September 2026 revision by Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01671v2) studies logarithmic-width SAT, local-query indistinguishability and lower bounds for specified proof systems. These restricted-model results do not establish a general time lower bound or settle ETH or SETH.

For exact-search budgets, randomized PPSZ still has the best proven worst-case bound for general 3-SAT; [Jiang and Cai's July reanalysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10697v1) certifies `O*(1.307031578^n)` without changing the algorithm, an improvement in the eighth decimal place of the base. [Kulikov and Mihajlin's SOSA 2024 note, posted in July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21276v1), shows that a reduction proving an `alpha^(n^2)` lower bound for exact edge coloring on `n` vertices, for their explicit constant `alpha > 1`, assuming SETH or optimality of the known 3SUM or APSP algorithms, would refute that assumption. Such a reduction therefore cannot explain the missing `2^o(n^2)` algorithm for exact edge coloring, the model behind [transfer-round coloring](#goldberg-seymour-and-the-number-of-transfer-rounds).

### Orthogonal Vectors conjecture

**Fine-grained hardness conjecture; conditional guest-workload connection.** For every `epsilon > 0`, there is a constant `c` for which finding an orthogonal pair between two sets of `n` Boolean vectors of dimension `c log n` has no `O(n^(2-epsilon))` algorithm in the stipulated computation model. [July 2026 research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23799v1) proves the monotone-circuit form, a tight `Omega(n^2 d)` formula bound and branching-program bounds, and [Lin's ECCC report, revised 4 September](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/139/), proves `n^Omega(k)` fixed-depth `AC^0` lower bounds for `k`-OV. These restricted-model results do not prove the conjecture.

It underlies barriers for certain exact similarity, string and attention problems. These can matter to inference and applications hosted in Wasm. Connections to attention depend on dimension, approximation error, magnitudes and other parameters. For attention computed within additive error `epsilon` from entries bounded by `B`, [Gupta et al., ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/8a01099096c85890b1d1aff3c6b4ea56-Abstract-Conference.html), give `O-tilde(n^(2-1/d) polylog(B))` time for constant head dimension `d`, and [Theorem 1.6 of their preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14840v1) proves `n^(2-o(1))` SETH hardness once `d = 2^Omega(log* n)`, with `B = poly(n)` and inverse-polynomial error. The 128-wide heads in the [inference demand estimate](../performance/inference-demand.md) would leave that algorithm an exponent saving of `1/128`, and per-token decoding is already linear in context length. A refutation would invalidate particular conditional lower bounds, not make every attention variant linear or remove the [resident model's memory traffic](../performance/inference-demand.md).

[The SODA 2026 Online Orthogonal Vectors result](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611978971.201), also [posted to arXiv in May](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04798), gives deterministic data structures refuting a particular OnlineOV data-structure conjecture. Its preprocessing/query model is distinct from the standard offline conjecture stated here; that refutation must not be transferred without accounting for preprocessing and parameters.

### Weighted All-Pairs Shortest Paths hypothesis

**Fine-grained hardness hypothesis with a claimed integer-model refutation and public Lean proofs.** The hypothesis excludes `O(n^(3-epsilon))` algorithms for every fixed positive `epsilon`, commonly for polynomially bounded integer weights with distances well-defined. [Fischer's STOC 2026 work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27736) studies its conditional equivalences. The [3SUM/APSP formalization below](#integer-3sum-and-apsp-formalization) supplies an `O(n^2.99942)` integer word-RAM claim. The matching integer formulation therefore needs that refutation status, rather than an unqualified open label; the external development was not replayed in this review.

Progress could improve graph analyses or min-plus computations if profiling identifies those kernels in the composer or guest applications. This is not a demonstrated current bottleneck. Fast ordinary matrix multiplication does not automatically accelerate min-plus multiplication, and graph-analysis savings do not directly reduce interpreter dispatch overhead.

[Polak and Schmidt's July 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00857v1) gives prediction-assisted APSP in `O(n^2.83 + eta*n)` time, where `eta` measures prediction error. Error can be quadratic, leaving cubic worst-case work. A prospective graph-pass comparison must include prediction production and the error-dependent execution bound; the result does not refute the unrestricted hypothesis.

### Modern 3SUM conjecture

**Fine-grained hardness conjecture with a claimed integer-model refutation and public Lean proofs.** The conjecture excludes a fixed polynomial improvement below quadratic for deciding whether three distinct input positions sum to zero; logarithmic improvements alone do not refute it. See [Dallant and Iacono's 2025 research](https://dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/398079/3/j47.pdf). The [3SUM/APSP formalization below](#integer-3sum-and-apsp-formalization) supplies an `O(n^1.9992)` claim for polynomially bounded integers. Its machine theorems do not cover real inputs, so integer and real formulations must retain separate status and evidence.

Consequences can constrain exact computational geometry and dynamic data structures used by desktop applications. The integer result motivates revisiting any matching conditional lower bound before using it to rule out an algorithm. A specific reduction is required before using it to bound [placement](../implementation/placement-search.md), graphics or Wasm performance; numerical representation and the machine model cannot be dropped from the claim.

[Kirkpatrick et al., February 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11363v1), obtain simultaneous subquadratic query and storage bounds for the preprocessed-universe variant: about `n^(1.5+epsilon)` randomized query time and `n^(2-2*epsilon/3)` space, suppressing polylogarithms, after roughly quadratic preprocessing. That initial cost prevents an immediate refutation of ordinary 3SUM; reuse across many queries is the prospective application.

[Carmel, Kosoburd and Krauthgamer, 23 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22355v1), improve the space to `O-tilde(n^max(2-epsilon,11/6-epsilon/3))`, with worst-case query time `O-tilde(n^(3/2+epsilon))` for `0 <= epsilon <= 1/2` and randomized `O-tilde(n^2)` preprocessing. They preprocess two size-`n` integer sets; queries select subsets and supply an unknown target set of size `O(n)`. At `epsilon = 1/4`, space and query time are both `O-tilde(n^1.75)`. With high probability the preprocessing succeeds simultaneously for every query, including adaptive queries. This improves the earlier tradeoff at interior parameter values while retaining the initial quadratic cost and the distinction from ordinary 3SUM.

The [FOCS 2026 accepted list](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/) includes Fischer, Polak and Schmidt's *3SUM Is Really Hard: A Real-to-Integer Reduction*. No public text was located, so its reduction, model and consequences for the integer and real formulations are not recorded here.

### Online Boolean matrix-vector multiplication conjecture

**Fine-grained hardness hypothesis; dynamic-workload connection.** Given an `n` by `n` Boolean matrix, receive `n` Boolean vectors sequentially and output each Boolean-semiring product before the next vector arrives. The OMv conjecture excludes total time `O(n^(3-epsilon))` for any constant positive `epsilon` in its stipulated randomized model. [Henzinger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06773) give the formulation; [current dynamic graph research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02582) continues to use it for conditional bounds.

Its reductions constrain some incremental reachability and dynamic indexes relevant to applications and analysis tools. It highlights why a generation known at composition can admit optimizations unavailable to an application receiving unknown future updates. This concerns Boolean products and online response order, not arbitrary numerical matrix-vector inference. Preprocessing, total cost and per-operation latency must be kept distinct when applying it to [Wasm workloads](../implementation/contracts/wasm-execution.md).

A [February 2026 revision of the NeurIPS 2025 result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21240v3) gives about `n^(2-1/d)` per-vector query time after roughly quadratic preprocessing for Boolean matrices of bounded VC-dimension `d`, suppressing polylogarithms. This is a structural opportunity for suitable dynamic graphs, not a general OMv refutation.

The [October 3SUM/APSP paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06783v1#S5.SS4) claims refutations of three rectangular hinted matrix-vector conjectures. Their hints and rectangular parameters differ from the ordinary online square-matrix problem here; that claim does not settle this OMv conjecture.

### Matrix multiplication exponent equals two

**Named conjecture; direct arithmetic-kernel connection.** The conjecture `omega = 2` means that, for every fixed `epsilon > 0`, square matrix multiplication in the applicable algebraic model has an `O(n^(2+epsilon))` arithmetic-operation algorithm. It does not assert a literal `O(n^2)` implementation. An [August 2026 preprint by Dupont et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16884v1) reports `omega < 2.371177`, using optimization assisted by AlphaEvolve. [Alman et al.'s 19 August revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349v3) adopts that value as its analysis applied to the eighth power of the Coppersmith-Winograd tensor; its own fourth-power parameters give `omega < 2.371339`. Dupont et al. describe exact-rational certification with directed logarithm bounds, but say verification code and candidate data are being prepared for release. This review did not locate that promised artifact; the preprint bound is not independently reproduced evidence and does not resolve `omega = 2`.

**Formal artifacts, August-September 2026.** [Zuiddam's Lean development at revision `f8478f7b`](https://github.com/jzuiddam/matrix-mult-exponent/tree/f8478f7b8706db8f176e3cc1954003af9c4113f0) states and proves [`omegaMM F < 2.371339` for every field `F`](https://github.com/jzuiddam/matrix-mult-exponent/blob/f8478f7b8706db8f176e3cc1954003af9c4113f0/OmegaBound/FinalBound.lean). [`omegaMM`](https://github.com/jzuiddam/matrix-mult-exponent/blob/f8478f7b8706db8f176e3cc1954003af9c4113f0/OmegaBound/Omega.lean) is the infimum of exponents `tau` with tensor rank of `n` by `n` multiplication at most `C*n^tau`, where rank at most `r` means restriction from the `r`-dimensional identity tensor. Its [metadata](https://github.com/jzuiddam/matrix-mult-exponent/blob/f8478f7b8706db8f176e3cc1954003af9c4113f0/formalization.yaml) reports no `sorry`; the released-certificate instance uses `native_decide` for 191 finite checks, adding `Lean.ofReduceBool` and `Lean.trustCompiler` to the trusted base, and discloses Claude Code and Codex assistance with author review of the statement's definitions. Mathlib assigns `0` to the infimum of an empty or unbounded-below set, so the statement's meaning also relies on the exponent set being nonempty and bounded below, standard facts outside the final theorem. This review inspected those statement files and metadata without building the 2,003-module package; it formalizes the fourth-power bound, not `2.371177`. A separate [repository at revision `3794e68b`](https://github.com/bsd-developer/matrix-math-publish/tree/3794e68b9dfeeae2b7d6d7d3c29bf512530a5b09) describes `omega <= 2.371281...` as fully machine-checked, but its omega theorem depends on the project axiom [`AX1_combination_loss`](https://github.com/bsd-developer/matrix-math-publish/blob/3794e68b9dfeeae2b7d6d7d3c29bf512530a5b09/lean/MatrixMath/Axioms.lean), which asserts that feasible combination-loss parameters bound `omegaExponent`. That Lean content checks a certificate against an assumed laser-method analysis rather than proving the bound.

Practical constructions could improve dense inference, graphics and certified native kernels. Constants, scratch space, communication, rectangular shapes and numerical semantics still determine target value. Matrix-vector autoregressive decoding does not inherit the same benefit as large matrix-matrix multiplication. No operation-count theorem removes the cost of reading weights under the [fixed memory grants](../performance/inference-demand.md), or permits numerically different reassociation without an accepted semantic contract.

OpenAI's [family 107](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/107.md) claims `omega <= 9/4` over the complex numbers, or `O_epsilon(n^(9/4+epsilon))` arithmetic operations. A separate result gives `omega < 2.371054886006746` over every fixed field. The complex result must not replace an all-field bound, and neither proves `omega = 2`. This review inspected the manuscript statement and supplied Lean theorem exports/configurations without replaying their dependencies. Representation, numerical stability and finite-size traffic still need the existing arithmetic implementation bridge.

Finite algorithms can advance independently of this asymptotic conjecture. The [48-multiplication constructions](#finite-matrix-multiplication-with-48-products) below supply concrete candidates for small matrix blocks and recursive kernels.

### Optimal bit complexity of integer multiplication

**Claimed refutation of the lower-bound conjecture; arithmetic and proof-production connection.** Is multiplying two `n`-bit integers intrinsically an `Omega(n log n)`-time task in the multitape Turing-machine bit model? [Harvey and van der Hoeven](https://www.texmacs.org/joris/nlogn/nlogn.pdf) achieve the matching upper bound and explicitly distinguish it from the unproved lower bound. The upper bound was announced in 2019 and [published in 2021](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2021/193-2/p04); it is not a newly solved 2026 problem.

OpenAI's [family 109 manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Integer-multiplication-below-n-log-n-September-23-2026/paper.pdf) claims exact deterministic worst-case `O(n*(log n)^(1-kappa))` time with `kappa=2^-182`, on one fixed finite-alphabet machine with finitely many ordinary one-dimensional tapes. If correct, this refutes the stated conjecture in its own bit model. The manuscript describes extremely large constants and thresholds; it supplies no usable target crossover. This review inspected its statement and model, without checking the complete proof; the release catalogue supplies no Lean link for this family.

**Conditional community refinement, October 2026.** Douglas Colkitt's [CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds at revision `3b6b6689`](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/tree/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f) builds on family 109. Its [selected-result record](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f/certificates/selected-result.json) reports the same asymptotic form with `kappa = 330942774629799/500000000000000000`, approximately `0.000661885549259598`. Dugongue's #186 construction combines Chafik Boukhalfa's shared-edge complex supplier with eumemic's physical bit word, coordinating operation frames and completing entrance banks. The [construction argument](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f/research/coordinated-frames-and-entrance-banks/PROOF.md) retains the all-size compilation, routing, precision/recovery, uniform setup and analytic fixed-tape interfaces as assumptions. This is a stronger conditional exponent saving, not an independent proof of those interfaces, a runtime speedup or a usable crossover.

The [maintainer review](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f/docs/research/community-round8-review.md) and [integration receipt](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f/docs/research/community-round8-integration.json) report exact finite word replay, dirty-register restoration, rational inequality checks, corruption controls and passing Linux jobs. The [bank-scheduling supplement](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f/research/community-round8-audit/BANK-SCHEDULE.md) supplies a separate conflict-free scheduling argument with additional finite prime exclusions; its small local-ring diagnostic does not instantiate the production construction or prove a tape machine. The [repository's formalization scope](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3b6b66891c0ac888521cf591fe306c6286601d4f/README.md#citation-and-license) limits its Lean profile instantiations to historical checkpoints, not this selected witness or the full multiplication theorem. This review read the selected record, certificate, construction, supplement and supplied validation records, and matched the certificate and supplement hashes to the selected record; it did not rerun the finite checks, Lean packages or multiplication proof.

A faster construction could improve sufficiently large exact arithmetic in proof producers, symbolic tools and guest libraries. The [proof-producer handoff](../assurance/proof-assistance.md#research-handoff-for-future-proof-producers) separates certificate replay from the missing all-size theorem, and the [Wasm handoff](../implementation/contracts/wasm-execution.md#mathematical-research-handoff) requires an identified workload and finite costs. Neither determines the best fixed-width cryptographic multiplier or the practical crossover against simpler algorithms. Bit complexity differs from counting word multiplications, and secret-dependent arithmetic still needs the [cryptographic implementation's](../implementation/fiat-crypto-emission.md) timing and correctness evidence.

[Harvey and van der Hoeven, FOCS 2025, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22848v1), reduce binary matrix transposition to multiplication: the conjectured transposition lower bound implies the multiplication lower bound. Consequently, if the claimed sub-`n log n` multiplication bound holds in this model, the transposition lower-bound conjecture also fails. The reduction itself supplies neither lower bound and is consistent with that conditional consequence.

### Dynamic optimality of binary search trees

**Named conjecture; conditional application data-structure connection.** Does splaying serve every access sequence within a universal constant factor of the best offline binary-search-tree execution in the same model, with the usual initial-state accounting? The broader existence question asks for any online BST with this guarantee. A [July 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18498v1) gives `O(log log n * (log log log n)^2)` competitiveness for splay trees, with an additive `O(n)` term for the initial tree, leaving the constant-factor target open.

For the other leading candidate, [Guo, Pettie, Skora and Wan's 5 August preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04410v1) proves `2^{O(sqrt(log log n))}` competitiveness for Greedy BST, its first `o(log n)` competitive bound. Greedy's conjectured constant competitiveness also remains open; this is a separate algorithm and guarantee from the splay result. Both theorems concern access sequences only; insertions and deletions, which an application map also performs, are outside them.

A proof would strengthen sequence-level efficiency guarantees for adaptive indexes or application maps. A counterexample would delimit the algorithm's universal claim. It would not establish bounded latency for each access: amortized and competitive guarantees can hide an expensive operation. Nor would it justify replacing the project's [storage index](../implementation/comparisons/storage-index.md), whose block traffic, persistence, recovery and endurance have different costs. The most plausible initial consumer is a bounded, in-label application data structure.

### Optimal randomized list labeling

**Open tight-bound problem; conditional ordered-index connection.** Keep at most `n` elements in sorted order in an array of `m = (1+Theta(1))n` slots, completing each insertion or deletion before the next request, and charge every element placement or move. For deterministic algorithms and `m = Cn` with `C > 1`, [Bulánek, Koucký and Saks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5636v1) prove the classical `O(log^2 n)` amortized cost optimal up to constants. [Bender et al., FOCS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00807v1), give a randomized algorithm with `O((log n)(log log n)^3)` amortized expected cost against an oblivious adversary; the best randomized lower bound is `Omega(log n)`, and removing the remaining `poly(log log n)` factor is open. Their [FOCS 2022 predecessor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02763v3) proves `Theta(epsilon^-1 log^(3/2) n)` expected cost optimal for history-independent structures with `m = (1+epsilon)n`, for `epsilon` between `n^(-1/3)` and a small constant.

A sorted array with bounded slack keeps an ordered index contiguous for rescans and predecessor search. It is a candidate representation for the [future namespace and query indexes](../implementation/contracts/service-authoring.md#research-handoff-for-the-future-index-implementation) or a bounded in-label ordered map. Unlike [memory reallocation](#optimal-copying-overhead-for-memory-reallocation), elements have unit size and must keep their order.

The randomized bounds are amortized expectations against an oblivious adversary; they supply neither a per-operation bound nor a guarantee against requests that adapt to the layout. [Layered list labeling, PODS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16623v1), combines separate worst-case, adaptive and expected solutions in one structure, so a phase bound would rest on its worst-case component. A moved element that holds a capability or is referenced by position needs its references updated, as in any relocation. History independence is relevant only where the layout is observable outside its owner, and it raises the optimal expected cost.

### Strong Descartes' Rule over finite fields

**Specialized proposed conjecture; conditional optimizer connection.** There are constants `0 < epsilon, delta < 1` such that, for sufficiently large primes `p,q` with `q` dividing `p-1`, a nonzero polynomial over `F_p` with at most `q^delta` nonzero terms and distinct exponents modulo `q` has at most `epsilon*q` roots in the order-`q` subgroup of the multiplicative group of `F_p`. [Li and Wu, ITCS 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.95/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.95.html), pose this as Conjecture 9.

The conjecture would improve soundness bounds for randomized identity tests on restricted circuits with exponentiation, motivated by neural-network optimization. Such tests could filter candidate [compute rewrites](../implementation/compute-compatibility.md). They would not replace an exact admission theorem or establish equivalence of floating-point, quantized or effectful execution. This is a narrower research lead than general PIT, not an established platform optimization.

[Malavolta and Rosen's June 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08662v1), Theorem 4.2, proves a nonuniform partial result: for each fixed prime `q`, all but finitely many eligible `p` give at most `k-1` subgroup roots for a nonzero `k`-sparse polynomial with distinct exponents modulo `q`. The exceptional range depends on `q`, leaving the uniform conjecture open. Their Theorem 4.3 gives a modified randomized-prime identity test for condensed `k`-sparse exponential polynomials, under its degree, coefficient-height and admissible-prime conditions, including `q > 2kw`. This supplies a restricted soundness theorem without assuming the full conjecture; it is neither general PIT nor a floating-point equivalence theorem.

## Security, entropy and reliable communication

For these entries, a negative resolution may be more consequential than a performance gain. A verified implementation can correctly execute a cryptographically weak construction. The [cryptographic premise inventory](../assurance/proof-reuse/crypto.md) distinguishes mathematical reductions, concrete hardness assumptions, ideal-oracle models and attack-cost estimates; those categories remain separate here.

### Existence of one-way functions

**Foundational conjecture; direct assurance connection.** Does there exist a polynomial-time computable function family for which every probabilistic polynomial-time adversary has negligible probability of finding a preimage of the image of a uniformly sampled input? Classical and quantum-adversary formulations differ. See [Barak's public-key complexity survey](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2017/065/download/) and [research on deriving one-wayness from NP problems](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/513.pdf).

Existence would establish a foundation for general computational cryptography; nonexistence would undermine broad classes of encryption, signatures and pseudorandom generation. Neither outcome should be casually equated to a finite-cost attack on a deployed key size. Existence also would not prove this project's AES, hash or lattice choices secure. Worst-case `P != NP` is not known to imply the average-case hardness required here.

A [February 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17651) derives one-way functions from nontrivial non-interactive zero-knowledge arguments, or constant-round public-coin zero-knowledge arguments, for NP under `NP` not contained in `ioP/poly`. The nontriviality condition allows an inverse-polynomial gap between the sum of completeness, soundness and zero-knowledge errors and one. This extends the permitted error range without establishing unconditional one-wayness.

[Nandakumar et al., RANDOM 2026, published 9 September](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2026.44), relate one-way functions to a separation between polynomial-time dimension and polynomial-time Kolmogorov-complexity rate over efficiently samplable infinite sequences. One-way functions imply an almost-sure uniform separation; the converse yields infinitely-often one-way functions. This connects another mathematical frontier without proving unconditional existence.

[Liu and Pass, ITCS 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.97), characterize one-way functions by boundary hardness of randomized time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity, under the derandomization assumption that `E` is not contained in `ioSIZE[2^o(n)]`, and give a worst-case to average-case reduction for exact `MINK^poly` under the same assumption. Neither side of the characterization is proved unconditionally.

### Module Learning With Errors hardness

**Current computational assumption; direct, critical connection.** Over the specified polynomial quotient ring, distinguish `(A, A*s + e)` from `(A, u)`, where `A` is uniform, `s,e` follow the selected small-noise distributions and `u` is uniform. Search formulations instead seek the secret. Parameters, distribution and classical or quantum adversary access are part of the statement. [FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) connects ML-KEM security to Module Learning With Errors.

This bears directly on the selected key establishment and its [qualification contract](../assurance/pq-reference-contract.md). Better attacks could require migration or larger parameters, with memory, bandwidth and crypto-slot costs. Stronger reductions could improve the justification of a margin. A reduction to worst-case lattice problems does not prove those problems hard.

**Status sensitivity:** a [2026 quantum-algorithm announcement](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1591) acknowledges correction and dispute; a [September-updated refutation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1693) challenges its required advantage. This is not recorded as an accepted break, and rejecting an attack does not prove hardness. The existing [attack review](../assurance/proof-reuse/crypto.md#what-the-attack-corrections-establish) owns the project's disposition. The announcement's latest revision, of 17 August, still reports that the refutation is under evaluation; no later author response was located. Separately, [Guo and Yang's 17 August analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16598v1) completes proofs of three sketched lemmas but finds that they assume a partition fixed independently of the measured string, which the algorithm's partition rule does not supply, so the lemmas do not establish correctness.

The refutation has an [AI-assisted Lean artifact at revision `6de8c1db`](https://github.com/sragavan99/lean-ePrint-2026-1591-refutation/tree/6de8c1dbffec10b4b44b2cae3aeadeb1cf0cfc83), still the repository's only commit, with an exact `Q*sqrt(K/N)` distinguishing-advantage bound for its specified dihedral-coset algorithm model. This review inspected the documented theorem scope, including register and rounding conventions, but did not build or audit the complete dependency closure. It is concrete evidence to examine for that attack, not a proof of Module LWE hardness. Separately, [Wen and Zheng, CRYPTO 2026](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/155), relate constant-rank search MLWE over power-of-two cyclotomic rings to structured extrapolated dihedral cosets; a reduction is not an efficient attack. [Boucher, Fouque and Shen's 28 September preprint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2249) defines a cyclotomic coset problem, a generalization of DCP that keeps an exact hidden-subgroup structure, and solves it with a quantum sieve in time and samples `2^(O_p(log n log q))` for each fixed prime `p` and modulus `q = p^t`; the sieve also covers uniform EDCP and Gaussian `S|LWE>`, whose samples carry the error in quantum superposition, in quasi-polynomial time when `q = poly(n)`. The authors state that this does not by itself give a quasi-polynomial algorithm for standard LWE, because the known reduction yields only a limited number of approximate coset states. Only the abstract was inspected here.

**Concrete attack-model research, 2026.** [Hou, Jiang and Ogilvie, CRYPTO 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-35377-1_15), exploit power-of-two cyclotomic ring structure in primal and dual hybrid attacks: up to 14.2 bits below the corresponding LWE estimate for sparse-secret FHE parameters, but about 1 bit for standardized Kyber/ML-KEM parameters. [De Perthuis and Trenkić's PQCrypto 2026 estimators](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2195) give the module primal attack a subexponential asymptotic gain when the field discriminant satisfies `|Delta_K| < d^d`; power-of-two cyclotomic fields, including ML-KEM's, have `|Delta_K| = d^d`. [Li, Wang and Wang's 23 September preprints](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2166) give provably correct FFT dual attacks costing 410 bits and, over the [general dual lattice](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2186), 426 bits for ML-KEM-1024. They follow earlier provable analyses' cost conventions and report reductions of 68 and 52 bits against those provable baselines, so these figures are not comparable with heuristic core-SVP estimates without a common cost model. Each is attack-model evidence for the [PQ premise record](../assurance/pq-reference-contract.md#security-premise-qualification), not a hardness proof.

**Worst-case lattice complexity, AI-produced formal artifact, August 2026.** [OpenAI's `GapCVP.lean` at revision `94bc0feb`](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs/blob/94bc0feb6a9ff12c7d31d6de640a725c9d43d2b6/GapCVP.lean) proves `gapCVP400IsNPHard`: for integer lattices of dimension `n`, distinguishing a target within distance `r` from one farther than `n^(1/400)*r` from every lattice vector is NP-hard under polynomial-time promise reductions. A companion theorem makes binary nearest codeword NP-hard within factor `blockLength^(1/200)`. The [challenge statement](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs/blob/94bc0feb6a9ff12c7d31d6de640a725c9d43d2b6/ComparatorChallenges/H_GapCVP.lean) defines both promise problems and NP-hardness directly, and the [metadata](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs/blob/94bc0feb6a9ff12c7d31d6de640a725c9d43d2b6/formalization.yaml) reports no `sorry`, only standard Lean axioms, production by OpenAI's Astra model and agent review. This review inspected those statements and metadata without building the package. The factor is far below the approximation factors of at least `sqrt(n)` in the worst-case reductions behind lattice cryptography; at factor `sqrt(n)`, [Aharonov and Regev](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/papers/cvpconp.pdf) place GapCVP in `NP intersect coNP`, so NP-hardness there under such reductions would imply `NP = coNP`. The theorem concerns worst-case CVP, not average-case MLWE or the selected ML-KEM-1024 margin.

### Module SIS and SelfTargetMSIS hardness

**Current assumption family; direct signature connection.** Module Short Integer Solution asks for a nonzero short vector `z` with `A*z = 0 mod q` for a sampled module matrix. SelfTargetMSIS additionally couples the short-vector relation to a hash-derived challenge containing part of the sought solution. Norms, parameters and oracle access must match the security theorem. The [Dilithium specification](https://pq-crystals.org/dilithium/data/dilithium-specification-round3.pdf) distinguishes the assumptions; [FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final) specifies ML-DSA.

These assumptions affect generation signatures, attestation and replaceable boot stages. A break can defeat authenticity despite correct implementation. Tighter concrete reductions can improve justified parameter choices, but not automatically reduce costs. A [quantum reduction involving SelfTargetMSIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16619) already exists under stated conditions; the unresolved matter is hardness and the selected finite-parameter security claim, not the blanket existence of any reduction. See the [lattice premise ledger](../assurance/proof-reuse/crypto.md#lattice-conjectures-and-core-svp-estimates).

A candidate [ML-DSA Lean development at revision `e3d83efa`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/e3d83efabfd1cedaf11546fa61fd164a41f11f3e/LatticeCrypto/MLDSA/Security.lean), dated 25 September, still ends its `euf_cma_security` proof with `sorry`. Its named MLWE and SelfTargetMSIS reductions are also `sorry` placeholders, and the theorem's docstring states that the statement has no security content until the problems are specialized to ML-DSA and its unconstrained real loss parameters are fixed. The sibling [`SecurityNMA.lean`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/e3d83efabfd1cedaf11546fa61fd164a41f11f3e/LatticeCrypto/MLDSA/SecurityNMA.lean) has no `sorry` in source inspection: it bounds no-message-attack advantage by an MLWE key-swap hop and a SelfTargetMSIS extractor. That file leaves reduction of its tailored short-model relation to the standard SelfTargetMSIS form as follow-up work and treats SHAKE-based matrix expansion as an idealization assumption; its build and imported closure were not checked here. Both files' inspected claims are unchanged at [revision `3e4d6ecc`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/tree/3e4d6eccc245a3760d181fc3c2d7ca8e99bfb840) of 3 October: `Security.lean` is byte-identical, and `SecurityNMA.lean` changes only names and proof steps. This is partial reduction evidence, not a completed EUF-CMA theorem. The completed mechanized comparison remains [Barbosa et al.'s CRYPTO 2023 EasyCrypt proof](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/246), which repairs a gap in earlier CMA-to-NMA reductions and mechanizes Dilithium's CMA security in the classical ROM; it supplies neither FIPS 204 correspondence nor QROM coverage by itself. Even a completed reduction would retain its hardness assumptions, parameters and oracle model.

### Classical elliptic-curve discrete logarithm and Diffie-Hellman hardness

**Current assumption family; direct classical-hedge connection.** For the selected group, discrete logarithm asks for `a` from `G,aG`; computational Diffie-Hellman asks for `abG` from `G,aG,bG`. These related problems are not interchangeable. Their unresolved hardness here is classical: [Shor's polynomial-time quantum algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9508027) already remove the corresponding asymptotic quantum-hardness claim.

A fast classical attack would remove the classical component's contribution to hybrid key establishment. It need not defeat a correctly proved combiner whose other component remains secure. Improvements in security analysis or arithmetic could alter the cost and confidence of that hedge. The [project's assumption inventory](../assurance/proof-reuse/crypto.md) requires the exact group, game and combiner theorem. A named combiner can consume a stronger interactive premise: [X-Wing's classical IND-CCA theorem](https://doi.org/10.62056/a3qj89n4e) assumes strong Diffie-Hellman in the X25519 nominal group, in the ROM. This is not an RSA dependency or a claim that a single generic group lower bound proves the selected curve secure.

A [July 2026 classical guess-and-determine proposal, revised 6 August](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09814v2), now states that, once success probability is accounted for, the algorithm is no better than exhaustive search and poses no present risk to elliptic-curve cryptography. [September quantum resource estimates for secp256k1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05625) reach about 1,450 logical qubits and 40 million Toffoli gates under their model. [Luo et al.'s 5 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13816v3) instead minimizes space, with `3n + 6*floor(log2 n) + O(1)` logical qubits and `1056*n^3/log2 n + O(n^2)` Toffoli gates, or 835 logical qubits for a 256-bit prime-field curve. [Jo and Lee, 14 September](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2014), announce an asymptotically smaller-space quantum algorithm over an `n`-bit prime field using `5n/2 + o(n)` logical qubits and `O(n^2 polylog(n))` Toffoli gates. Its measurement-based uncomputation and hidden terms require their own finite resource assessment. These results sharpen the quantum threat assessment but are neither classical algorithms nor demonstrated attacks on deployed keys.

### Concrete AES pseudorandom-permutation security

**Concrete security assumption family; direct storage and session connection.** Can adversaries with specified resources and oracle access distinguish secret-key AES from a uniformly selected permutation with advantage above the claimed bound? This is a game-indexed quantitative question, not the assertion that AES is impossible to attack. [FIPS 197](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/197/final) defines AES; the [GCM security analysis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/193.pdf) explains its role in composition.

The [AES-GCM premise dossier](../assurance/aes-gcm-premise-dossier.md) already identifies the unresolved AES-256 forward-PRP premise. Stronger concrete evidence could improve defensible rekeying and data-volume budgets; a suitable attack could invalidate confidentiality or authenticity claims. Correct AES code does not prove pseudorandomness, and stronger AES security does not remove nonce requirements or GCM's finite-message bounds.

**AI-assisted reduced-round advance, July 2026.** [Nasr and Carlini's cryptanalysis](https://anthropic.com/document/aes_mobius_bridge.pdf), [announced on 28 July](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses), improves single-key seven-round AES-128 attack work from about `2^99` to `2^89.3` through `2^91.4`, retaining `2^105` chosen plaintexts. An LLM discovered the central method. This is substantive cryptanalytic progress against seven of AES-128's ten rounds, not a full-round AES-128 or AES-256 break, and it does not discharge or invalidate the selected forward-PRP premise.

[Bao, Guo, List and Wang's independent 29 July preprint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1549) also exploits the S-box's multiplicative inverse, through a cross-ratio property. It improves the lower-data Derbez-Fouque-Jean variant of the single-key seven-round AES-128 attack to `2^97` chosen plaintexts, about `2^96.6` offline and `2^92.4` online seven-round encryption equivalents, `2^92` states of memory and success probability at least about 0.23. This trades more time for less data and memory than the Nasr-Carlini attack. For the selected key size, [Baksi et al.'s January 2026 related-key differential attacks](https://doi.org/10.62056/a0qjmpgxq) reach 13 of AES-256's 14 rounds for weak-key classes of proportion `2^-11` to `2^-2`, with `2^89` to `2^112` data and `2^189.9` to `2^233.9` time. Related keys lie outside the dossier's single-key forward-PRP game.

### Concrete SHA and Keccak security properties

**Concrete assumption family; direct and partly immutable connection.** Do the selected hashes provide the collision, preimage and second-preimage resistance each consumer needs? Do keyed constructions satisfy their particular PRF, MAC or DRBG games? These are distinct claims with explicit query and resource bounds. [FIPS 202](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/202/final) defines SHA-3/SHAKE; [FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) specifies the hash-based signature standard used by the boot design.

Consumers include content addressing, Merkle binding, measured boot, derivation and the ROM's signature verifier. New attacks can reach guarantees that software updates cannot repair in immutable hardware. Stronger constructions or reductions may improve justified signature, verification or randomness budgets. A proof in an ideal-permutation model does not prove concrete Keccak ideal. Fixed keyless hashing also needs careful adversary quantification, as [Rogaway explains](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~rogaway/papers/ignorance.pdf); the [project ledger](../assurance/proof-reuse/crypto.md#idealized-models-and-keyless-hashing) records that boundary.

[Li et al.'s 9 September revision](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1120) announces collisions for 38-step SHA-256 at `2^104.3` work, 38-step SHA-512 at `2^125.4`, and 39-step SHA-512 at `2^178`, plus an explicit 36-step SHA-256 collision. These reduced-step results do not cover the standardized full functions. The [Keccak designers' cryptanalysis inventory](https://keccak.team/third_party.html) also records a practical five-round preimage attack on `Keccak[r=640,c=160]`; its round count and capacity differ from the selected SHA-3/SHAKE instances. [Tu et al.'s 19 September preprint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2107) reports the first practical collision attack on six-round SHAKE128 with 160-bit output, at about `2^47.82`, six-round SHA3-224 and SHA3-256 near-collisions differing in four and six digest bits, and practical collisions on four- and five-round SHAKE256 with its full 512-bit digest; the selected instances use all 24 rounds of Keccak-f[1600]. Preserve those parameters when assessing any impact on the premise ledger.

A [GitHub schedule-compliance experiment at revision `891ebb3e`](https://github.com/kodareef5/sha256-probe/tree/891ebb3e8296f4589242a5d346a77a902c0f31af) illustrates another boundary: its `sr=59` claim uses 64 compression rounds with only 43 of the 48 message-schedule equations enforced. The [underlying report](https://stateofutopia.com/papers/2/we-broke-92-percent-of-sha-256.pdf) explicitly frees schedule words 57 through 61. This is an altered compression problem, not a standard SHA-256 collision or a 59-round attack with the prescribed schedule. The report was inspected; its experiments were not rerun. The repository's own [9 June transfer verdict](https://github.com/kodareef5/sha256-probe/blob/891ebb3e8296f4589242a5d346a77a902c0f31af/headline_hunt/reports/20260609_structural_transfer_verdict.md) concludes that this schedule-relaxed structure has no counterpart when the schedule is fully enforced.

### Public-key cryptography from arbitrary one-way functions

**Open foundational construction problem; speculative diversification connection.** Does existence of arbitrary one-way functions suffice to construct public-key encryption or secure key agreement? Known black-box barriers exclude broad generic approaches, not every possible non-black-box construction. See [Barak's survey](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2017/065/download/) and [limits of random-oracle constructions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3554).

A positive answer might diversify the assumptions available to future session protocols beyond today's structured lattice and classical choices. It promises neither small keys nor practical speed, and one-way functions would still be an assumption unless proved to exist. Any replacement would need concrete security, protocol composition, implementation refinement and the existing [admission and qualification path](../assurance/pq-reference-contract.md).

A neighboring [May 2026 secret-key private-information-retrieval result](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/067/) uses arbitrary one-way functions and approximately square-root online communication. Its offline client secret-key processing is essential; it does not construct public-key encryption or key agreement from arbitrary one-way functions.

**AI-assisted barrier, August 2026.** [Li, Li, Li and Liu](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03824v1) prove a query-complexity impossibility for perfectly complete key agreement with quantum local computation and classical communication in the Boolean-output quantum random-oracle model. For arbitrary finite rounds and independent initial private states, an eavesdropper recovers the key with certainty using `O((q_A+q_B)^5)` classical oracle queries. Computation is unbounded; pre-shared entanglement, correlated setup and quantum communication are excluded. The authors credit an AI-discovered argument that they checked and refined. Their peer-reviewed [CRYPTO 2026 paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/639) proves the corresponding QROM impossibility for perfectly complete quantum public-key encryption with classical keys. Perfect completeness is essential. This strengthens a black-box barrier without resolving arbitrary non-black-box constructions or supplying an efficient attack on a deployed protocol.

### Explicit low-error two-source extraction

**Open construction frontier; direct entropy-root connection.** Obtain a deterministic polynomial-time extractor for two independent `n`-bit sources, each with polylogarithmic min-entropy, producing at least one output bit with negligible statistical error `n^(-omega(1))`. The entropy and error requirements must be met together; more output is a further target. [Chattopadhyay and Goodman](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/075/download/) identify improving low-error two-source extraction as an outstanding challenge.

Better constructions could reduce raw samples, buffers and latency for a specified statistical guarantee in the [TRNG source contract](../hardware/trng-source-model-contract.md). They cannot create missing entropy, prove physical independence or automatically cover quantum side information. The broad `O(log n)`-entropy target at constant error is already achieved by [Li's 2023 result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06802). Building and qualifying a finite instance of an existing extractor is project work, not an unresolved theorem.

Revision checks matter here: [the May revision of TR26-011](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/011/) withdraws its efficient negligible-error DAG-source construction while retaining an existence result, and [TR26-089](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/089/) was retracted in June because of an error in Lemma 5.9. Neither supplies the required efficient two-independent-source theorem.

The local [finite composition and parameter proof contract](../hardware/trng-source-model-contract.md#finite-composition-and-parameter-proof-contract) retains the existing inner-product construction. Its extension adds the conditioner-image error to the final seed budget and proves that some parameter records passing the arithmetic admissibility test have no realizing source pair. These strengthen the existing proof's use at its stated parameters; they do not claim an improved extractor or supply missing physical premises.

### Tight multi-target bounds for SLH-DSA hashing

**Conjectured idealized-model bound; direct immutable-verifier connection.** The tight SPHINCS+ EUF-CMA reduction underlying SLH-DSA, by [Hülsing and Kudinov](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/346) and mechanized in EasyCrypt by [Barbosa et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/910), bounds forgery by PRF, interleaved-target-subset-resilience, target-collision, undetectability, preimage and single-function multi-target decisional second-preimage (SM-DSPR) advantages of its tweakable hash. For `p` targets and `q` quantum queries to an `n`-bit tweakable hash in the QROM, is the SM-DSPR advantage `O((q+1)^2/2^n)`, independent of `p`? [Do et al.'s April 2026 preprint, section 2.2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/632), records the best proven QROM bound as linear in `p` and NIST's tight parameter figures as relying on the `p`-independent conjecture.

That preprint replaces the SM-DSPR step by reducing open preimage resistance to multi-target preimage resistance, with loss proportional to the `q_s*k` targets revealed by `q_s` signatures, and bounds the latter through average-case search. Its Table 4 gives SLH-DSA-256s 187 classical and 91 quantum bits for `q_s = 2^64`, against NIST's tight 255 and 127 and loose 169 and 82. The preprint is unreviewed, and its reduction's success probability approximates `(1-1/c)^c` by `1/e`; the argument was read but not verified here.

This bears on [R-05-058a's SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256s](../spec.md#r-05-058a) in the immutable ROM verifier, whose parameters cannot change after a bound is revised. A proof of the conjecture would close the gap between NIST's tight figures and proven QROM bounds; a generic attack gaining with `p` would lower the justified margin. Either outcome concerns an idealized hash: neither shows that SHAKE256 behaves as a quantum random oracle, and FIPS 205's encoding still needs the correspondence recorded in the [SPHINCS+ proof entry](../assurance/proof-reuse/crypto.md#sphincs-tight-security-proof).

### Optimal binary-code rate versus distance

**Open mathematical optimization problem; direct reliability and capacity connection.** Determine `R_2(delta) = limsup_n log_2(A_2(n,ceil(delta*n)))/n`, where `A_2(n,d)` is the largest binary code of length `n` and minimum Hamming distance at least `d`. Exact general tradeoffs remain unknown. See [research on coding bounds](https://chrisjones.space/assets/papers/ho-delsarte.pdf) and [Alrabiah and Guruswami's August 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09347v1); the latter announces mixed-qubit-channel upper bounds strictly improving the first MRRW bound throughout `0 < delta < 1/2`, and a masked construction strictly improving the second MRRW bound throughout that interval, for both linear and nonlinear codes. This is a substantial bound improvement, not the exact tradeoff or a constructive decoder.

**AI-produced formal artifact, August 2026.** [OpenAI's `MetricCodes.lean` at revision `94bc0feb`](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs/blob/94bc0feb6a9ff12c7d31d6de640a725c9d43d2b6/MetricCodes.lean) defines the same asymptotic binary-code rate and proves `exists_binaryRate_mrrw_improvement`: for every `0 < delta < 1/2`, a positive gap below the second MRRW rate exists. The source theorem has a proof body; the separate Comparator challenge placeholders are not that proof. [Barg's 1 September comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01860v1) explains why this subspace-certificate argument and Alrabiah-Guruswami's channel argument yield the same bound. This review inspected the rate definition and theorem; it did not replay the build or audit the imported proof closure. The [metadata](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs/blob/94bc0feb6a9ff12c7d31d6de640a725c9d43d2b6/formalization.yaml) reports only standard Lean axioms. This artifact is a proof source for an upper bound, not an encoder or decoder.

**Further upper-bound preprints, August-September 2026.** [Gay, Jeronimo and Liu's 20 August preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20287v1) builds a representation-theoretic honeycomb hierarchy with explicit spectral certificates and proves an upper bound strictly below Alrabiah-Guruswami's masked-channel bound throughout `0 < delta < 1/2`. [Salmon's preprint, Theorem 1.1, revised 30 September](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03736v2), determines the asymptotic Delsarte linear-programming bound at rate one half: `R_D(1/2 - 1/pi) = 1/2`, and `R_D(delta) < 1/2` for every larger `delta`. Since `R_2 <= R_D`, this gives `R_2(1/2 - 1/pi) <= 1/2`, the best known bound at that distance, and shows that Delsarte's original linear program cannot prove a smaller one there. The author discloses GPT-5.6 Sol assistance. No formal artifact for either preprint was located; both remain asymptotic upper bounds, not finite code limits or constructions.

Constructive progress could reduce redundancy or improve protection within fixed memory and frame budgets. The [hardware ECC and link design](../spec.md#r-15-175) still requires concrete short block lengths, decoding latency, area, failure behavior and a qualified error model. An asymptotic existence bound does not supply a fixed-latency SECDED or DECTED implementation. Capacity under random errors and minimum-distance protection against adversarial errors are different questions.

### Deterministic fully polynomial list decoding

**Open algorithmic target, alongside a recently solved fixed-gap case; conditional recovery connection.** For suitably parameterized families of folded Reed-Solomon codes of rate `R`, can every candidate within error fraction `1-R-epsilon` be found deterministically in time polynomial in both block length `n` and `1/epsilon`? [Ashvinkumar, Habib and Srivastava, SODA 2026](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611978971.35), give deterministic near-linear time for fixed `epsilon`, and randomized `poly(1/epsilon)` times near-linear-in-`n` time. Their [discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12548v1#S1.SS3) leaves the combined deterministic fully polynomial target open. The folding parameter must match the capacity gap, and the deterministic dependence on that gap is large.

Progress could reduce redundancy while retaining predictable recovery algorithms for sufficiently large storage or communication blocks. These are large-alphabet list-decoding guarantees, not binary SECDED bounds. A list can contain several plausible messages; authenticated selection and bounded processing remain necessary. The [fixed-frame link contract](../spec.md#r-15-228b) still needs finite block lengths, fixed traffic, latency and failure behavior. A separate recent result improves [decoder scratch space](#capacity-approaching-decoding-with-small-workspace).

**Separate September 2026 preprint advances for ordinary Reed-Solomon codes.** [Brakensiek et al., 5 September revision, Corollary 5.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/164/revision/1/download), give deterministic capacity-approaching list decoding for every distinct evaluation set over prime fields `q >= C(R,delta)*n`, for fixed rate bound `R`, fixed gap `0 < delta < 1-R` and sufficiently large block length. The formal corollary bounds both runtime and list size by `q^{O_{R,delta}(1)}`; the informal introduction instead states an `n`-polynomial list bound. The formal statement supports polynomial time in `n` when `q` is polynomially bounded in `n`, not unrestricted polynomial time in the encoded input size `O(n log q)`.

[Jeronimo's preprint, posted 5 September and revised 6 September](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/169/), covers every prime `q >= n` and dimension `1 <= k <= (1-gamma)n`, for fixed slack `gamma > 0` and sufficiently large `n`. It finds all codewords within relative distance `1-k/n-gamma` in deterministic `q^{O_gamma(1)}` time, with final list size `n^{O_gamma(1)}` independent of `q`. The runtime still depends polynomially on the field size. Neither result settles the deterministic bound jointly polynomial in block length and inverse gap; ordinary RS and folded RS also remain distinct code families.

[Dao, Kominers and Thaler's 16 September preprint, Theorem 9.16](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2056), reduces that field-size dependence to `O-tilde(log q)` at each fixed gap `0 < delta < 1`. For any field `F_q` supplied with explicit fast arithmetic, `q = p^e >= n`, with `p > k-1` when `k >= 2`, any distinct evaluation points and sufficiently large `n`, a deterministic decoder returns exactly the polynomials of degree below `k` with at least `k + delta*n` agreements. It uses `B_delta * n^O(exp(1.5/delta)) * O-tilde(log q)` bit operations, polynomial in the input length `n log q`, with list size `n^O_delta(1)` independent of `q`. At fixed rate, the paper also decodes above the Johnson radius in `O-tilde(n log q)` bit operations in every characteristic. The authors credit GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 with discovering and refining the main results. The [ArkLib Lean development at revision `a5aa2677`](https://github.com/quangvdao/ArkLib/tree/a5aa2677fee4e3a79d6bb05136631cce4a08587d) states the capacity list-size and correlated-agreement bounds, and a separately pinned simplified decoder has an exact-output theorem; the fast reconstruction algorithm and its bit complexity are unformalized. This review inspected the paper's statements and the headline list theorem's source, without building the development or auditing its imports. The inverse-gap dependence remains exponential. Characteristic-two fields, the usual hardware representation, meet the characteristic condition only for `k <= 2`, and the prime-field results above exclude them, so none of these beyond-Johnson capacity decoders covers binary-extension RS codes.

**Ordinary-RS barriers, late September 2026 preprints.** [Al Kindi's 28 September ePrint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2245) constructs, over every field containing the required roots of unity, a received word with at least `binom(s-1,m+1)/s` codewords at agreement `m*h + 2h - 1` for the dimension-`m*h` RS code on a multiplicative subgroup of order `n = h*s`, where `h >= 2` and `1 <= m <= s-2`. At fixed rational rate `rho`, this gives lists of size `2^((2-o(1))H(rho)/eta)` at agreement fraction `rho + eta`, doubling the exponent of the antipodal bound stated by Arnon, Boneh and Fenzi. With `h = 2` and fixed `m/s`, the gap is `3/n` and the list is exponential in `n`; since a list decoder must output every listed codeword, none for ordinary RS on such subgroups runs in time polynomial in both block length and inverse gap. [Wan and Zhang's 24 September preprint, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29120v1), proves the decision version of bounded-distance decoding NP-complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions over explicitly represented extension fields of size `2^o(n)` and characteristic above `floor(n^alpha)`, for odd `n`, evaluation points produced by the reduction, dimension `(n+1)/2 - floor(n^alpha)`, radius `(n-1)/2` and fixed rational `0 < alpha < 1/2`. That radius lies `floor(n^alpha)/n` below capacity, so a deterministic decoder for ordinary RS codes over arbitrary explicitly represented fields and evaluation sets, polynomial in input length and inverse gap, would imply `P = NP`. The authors disclose ChatGPT Pro assistance with the reduction's development and exposition, drafting and consistency checks. Both results concern ordinary RS codes, so the folded-RS target above remains open; neither proof was checked in this review.

### Finite-length optimality of single-deletion VT codes

**Named optimality conjecture; prospective framing connection.** Let `VT_0(n)` contain the binary strings satisfying `sum_i i*x_i = 0 mod (n+1)`. Is it a largest possible length-`n` code correcting one deletion for every `n`? Asymptotic optimality does not settle exact finite lengths. [Weindel and Heckel, TMLR, June 2026](https://openreview.net/pdf/3877b05d22c2dc202b1750b4a1ecf7b3aaafbf50.pdf), still treat this as a conjecture, with exact optimality established only through length 11; recovering the known construction does not prove maximality.

A resolution could establish short-block redundancy limits for a matching synchronization-loss channel. Deletions are different from substitutions or erasures at known locations. The present memory and link contracts do not identify that channel model, so this is a possible future framing application rather than an improvement to the selected ECC.

The [June revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00613v2) proves that an LLM-discovered function generates the VT family. Its broader multiple-deletion and quaternary-edit experiments remain finite construction/search results. Recovering a conjectured-optimal family, even with a proof of the construction, does not establish its finite-length maximality.

### Log-rank conjecture

**Named conjecture; conditional communication-budget connection.** For a Boolean two-party communication matrix `M`, is deterministic worst-case communication complexity bounded by `poly(log(2 + rank_R(M)))`? The rank is over the reals. [Hambardzumyan, Lovett and Shirley's CCC 2026 paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.7), published 23 July, gives new equivalent formulations through signed rectangle rank; it and [Song's August 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01812v1) leave the conjecture open. The approximate-log-rank variant is a different conjecture and must not be conflated with this one.

A constructive result could reduce communication for matching exact predicates on separately held inputs, potentially reducing [ensemble slot reservations](../spec.md#r-15-228b). It would not compress arbitrary tensor payloads or automatically extend to many parties. Protocol rounds, local work, confidentiality and padding to the permitted fixed schedule must all be charged. A short unpadded transcript that reveals private information is not a valid replacement for the platform's communication contract.

[Song's August result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01812v1) improves explicit lower bounds to `Omega((log r)^2/log log r)`, while recording the general `O(sqrt(r))` upper bound. This narrows one side of the gap; it neither gives the conjectured polylogarithmic upper bound nor refutes the existence of some polynomial in `log r`.

### Li-Li undirected multiple-unicast conjecture

**Named conjecture; speculative ensemble connection.** For independent unicast sessions in an undirected capacitated network, network coding offers no throughput advantage over fractional routing. [Liu, Que, Li and Li's August 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06070v1) proves further special cases while leaving the general conjecture open.

A proof would delimit coding-based capacity optimization; a counterexample could identify topologies and traffic where coding helps. Applying this to a future [ensemble](../spec.md#r-15-228b) first requires a matching multi-hop network model. A single point-to-point link, directed time-slot graph, multicast, or correlated inference traffic does not automatically fit. Coding would still have to preserve fixed schedules, bounded buffers, session integrity and authority confinement.

The [August paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06070v1) includes all instances with at most five terminal locations, planar networks with at most three designated faces and both endpoints of each session on the same designated face, and demand graphs contained in the union of a triangle and a star. These identify concrete solved topology families; the unrestricted undirected multiple-unicast statement remains open.

## More indirect mathematical opportunities

### Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions

**Named conjecture; limited offline number-theory connection.** The nontrivial zeros of these L-functions are conjectured to have real part one half. This is stronger than the ordinary Riemann Hypothesis. Its algorithmic consequences include short-witness bounds used in deterministic number-theory algorithms; see [Bach's explicit bounds](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1990-55-191/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8.pdf) and [Miller's primality work](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf).

A proof could remove a hypothesis from some offline field-parameter or witness-search procedures. This is a weak connection to the project's [finite-field arithmetic generation](../implementation/fiat-crypto-emission.md), whose selected constants can instead carry direct finite certificates. Deterministic polynomial-time primality testing already exists without GRH. GRH would prove neither factoring hardness nor the security of this cryptographic suite, and no current runtime speedup is claimed.

**Unconditional proportion results, August-September 2026.** [Alpöge and Furman's revision 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13637v2) proves that more than two thirds of the Riemann zeta zeros, counted with multiplicity, are simple and on the critical line; its Theorem B states the same for each fixed primitive Dirichlet character as the height grows. The paper attributes the argument to Claude, verified by the listed authors. The [Lean project at revision `2bafb8c8`](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/2bafb8c88f177284a2123b5fefa2ff84e2365eb6/zeta23) states both families; its metadata reports a sorry-free proof using only standard axioms, with all Lean written by Claude. This review inspected the statement file and metadata, without building the project. [Lamzouri's revision 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882v2) gives a conceptually simpler proof of the zeta proportions. A proportion of zeros on the line supplies none of the witness bounds above, which assume GRH for every relevant character, so this entry's consumer is unchanged.

**Claim audit, June 2026.** [Zhang's version 16 preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202506.0481/v16) derives GRH through Theorem 1, which asserts that a symmetric, absolutely convergent product of conjugate-root quadratic factors with bounded real parts and summable inverse squared imaginary parts has all its zeros on the symmetry line. Its stated hypotheses admit `F(s) = prod_{n>=1} (1+(s-1/4)^2/n^2)(1+(s-3/4)^2/n^2)` with `k=1`. Each factor differs from one by `O(n^-2)` on compact sets, so the product defines an entire function; reflection `s -> 1-s` swaps the two factors for each `n`. Every zero `1/4 +/- i*n` or `3/4 +/- i*n` is simple and off `Re(s)=1/2`, while all real parts lie in `[0,1]` and the inverse-square sum is `2 sum_{n>=1} 1/n^2`. The proof's equations (5)-(7) incorrectly rule out reflection permuting distinct factors. This counterexample defeats that theorem and the manuscript's claimed derivation, not GRH.

**Quasi-Riemann proof announcement, OpenAI family 003.** The [supplied formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/003.md) claims zero-freeness in `Re(s) > 7/8` for zeta and every Dirichlet character and positive modulus, excluding the principal pole at `s=1`, as well as finite-order Hecke L-functions over `Q(sqrt(-3))`. Its later number-theoretic applications are explicitly outside that formalization. This review read the scope and verification configuration without replay. A fixed zero-free half-plane is substantial progress if established, but `7/8` is not `1/2`: neither GRH nor the GRH-based witness bounds above follow. The [prime-field factorization claim](#deterministic-polynomial-factorization-over-finite-fields) has its own companion analytic input to review.

### Hadamard matrix conjecture

**Named conjecture; limited representation connection.** For every positive integer `k`, does there exist a `4k` by `4k` matrix with entries `+1,-1` whose rows are orthogonal, so that `H*H^T = 4k*I`? [Glazyrin's August 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16116v1) identifies this unrestricted real-matrix existence problem as open. Solving particular orders or classifying complex Hadamard matrices does not settle it.

Constructions for more dimensions could broaden orthogonal sign transforms used in signal processing or quantization, sometimes avoiding padding. This is only a possible future representation choice. The [Bonsai assessment](../performance/bonsai2-assessment.md) already names block-1024 Hadamard rotations; that power-of-two transform requires no solution of the conjecture. General existence would not imply an `O(n log n)` transform, better model quality, smaller scratch space or permitted changes to frozen weight semantics.

**Twelve finite orders resolved, August 2026.** [Epoch's solution update](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/hadamard) records a three-person Anthropic team and Claude producing matrices for the formerly unresolved orders below 2000, including 668. This review independently checked all twelve matrices from the [public data mirror at revision `d8252010`](https://github.com/bbeartheancient/hoa64/tree/d825201089123126ce792d4921cba4ae1b8977a3/matrices): 668, 716, 892, 1132, 1244, 1388, 1436, 1676, 1772, 1916, 1948 and 1964. The [tracked exact-integer checker and report](hadamard-evidence/README.md) verified square dimensions, the sign alphabet and Hamming distance `n/2` for every distinct pair of rows; positive and corrupted negative controls passed. Thus `H*H^T = n*I` holds for each supplied matrix. The order-668 decompressed CSV has SHA-256 `afd95960604159441497bbc5f507aa5ff820682a5b11f19d6667c6e31a00d2ee`. This establishes those finite existence claims, not the universal conjecture or a fast transform.

[Ramos, Hulak and de Queiroz's multiplier restrictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20765v1) concern particular length-333 Legendre-pair symmetries, not nonexistence of all order-668 matrices. The same authors' [Lean certificate at revision `78fbae8d`](https://github.com/Arthur742Ramos/hadamard-668-lean/tree/78fbae8d69b6fdb66705b662469692d60e346ef0) proves existence of an order-668 integer matrix with `+1,-1` entries, row squared norm 668 and orthogonal distinct rows; its README reports no `sorry`, custom axioms, `native_decide` or `Lean.ofReduceBool` in the solution and certificate modules and cites an earlier Isabelle/HOL formalization. Only that README was inspected here. A [Lean repository at revision `d1f22fc0`](https://github.com/SamuelSchlesinger/hadamard-conjecture/tree/d1f22fc03782292dddf44b9052eb5d8aee2be96b) formalizes Paley and Kronecker constructions while leaving the universal `hadamard_conjecture` target open.

OpenAI's [family 179](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/179.md) instead claims that real **circulant** Hadamard matrices exist only at orders 1 and 4, with a supplied Lean development. Its catalogue statement was inspected, without proof replay. This restricts a special symmetry class; it does not refute unrestricted order-`4k` existence or invalidate the power-of-two rotations used by the Bonsai proposal.

### Shannon capacity of the seven-cycle

**Open exact-value problem; speculative zero-error coding connection.** For the seven-cycle confusability graph `C_7`, determine `Theta(C_7) = sup_m alpha(C_7 strong-power m)^(1/m)`, where `alpha` is the maximum independent-set size. [Polak and Schrijver](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.07438) give a constructive lower bound; [August 2026 research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30273v1) continues to improve constructions without determining the exact capacity. The best upper bound it records remains the Lovász theta value `7cos(pi/7)/(1+cos(pi/7)) = 3.31766...`, so the unresolved interval is about `0.059` wide.

The general method studies how distinguishable messages can be packed under a specified zero-error confusion model. It could inspire more efficient codes for a matching future channel. No evidence identifies `C_7` as this platform's electrical, storage or fault model. Its exact value would therefore be mathematical background, not an immediate change to [fixed-frame communication](../spec.md#r-15-228b), authentication or ECC.

**AI-assisted construction with a public checker.** [Tandon's 31 August preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30273v1) raises the constructive lower bound to `3.25883262...` using a dimension-500 construction. The [companion package at revision `c7534084`](https://github.com/tandonravi/C7-Shannon-Capacity-Heterogeneous-Recursion/tree/c753408492dde92a239708986d16c15dbf6c3235) contains exact finite-certificate and recursive-arithmetic checks. Source inspection found that the default arithmetic check assumes supplied certificate counts; `--recomputed` derives them from the base certificate. Neither path was executed in this review. The package explicitly does not formalize the new heterogeneous theorem in Lean. A separate [Buys-Polak-Zuiddam Lean development at revision `aa21eeb1`](https://github.com/spectra-research/shannon-capacity-lean/blob/aa21eeb12b75b0413d3fa9fb4208b5d0bf2c4d65/ShannonBounds/Main.lean), accompanying their [31 July preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29681v1), proves the weaker lower bound `3.258827985920007` for capacity defined as the supremum of `alpha(G^n)^(1/n)` over strong powers. Its main and definition files contain no `sorry`, `axiom` or `native_decide`; this review inspected them without building the package. It is not a formal proof of Tandon's improvement. These are reproducible-construction leads, with the finite-certificate and recursion obligations explicit, not exact determinations of capacity.

## Project-specific questions, not established named conjectures

The closest opportunities need not await a famous theorem. The [existing research agenda](static-memory-research.md) owns these questions; this survey does not create another implementation backlog.

- **Exact planning near laminar lifetimes.** In the binary-encoded, unit-alignment, unrestricted-base model, let `k` be the minimum number of intervals deleted to make the lifetime family laminar. Is exact placement possible in `f(k) * poly(input length)` time? What hardness can be shown, potentially even at a small fixed `k`? The [structural analysis](../implementation/static-memory/structure.md) already solves the deletion parameter and gives exact packing for bipartite crossing graphs. The remaining general parameterized problem is different from those solved cases.
- **The deletion-number-two load gap.** In that same model, must every instance with `k = 2` attain its maximum simultaneous load as legal span? The [structural analysis](../implementation/static-memory/structure.md) proves equality for `k <= 1` and for bipartite crossing graphs, and gives a gap at `k = 3`. The remaining `k = 2` question concerns unrestricted crossing graphs, necessarily involving a triangle if there is a gap. A counterexample would not itself prove computational hardness.
- **Compositional public-phase reuse.** Can component certificates establish global safe reuse efficiently without enumerating every state combination, while accounting for asynchronous completion, retained authority and revocation? Existing [phase experiments](../implementation/static-memory/phases.md) do not establish the full implementation theorem.
- **Capacity sharing versus private demand.** Under an explicit observation model, what headroom, padding, public release rule or declassification permits useful sharing without leaking another label's demand? [Lending experiments](../implementation/static-memory/lending.md) give scoped counterexamples. Fixed-tier cross-owner lending remains excluded; this explores changed assumptions rather than an authorized allocator feature. It is not a universal impossibility conjecture independent of the chosen service guarantees.
- **Reclamation-aware scheduling and bounded segmentation.** Do these transformations admit a larger real workload after charging metadata, copy traffic, zeroization, quarantine and deadlines? These are workload hypotheses, not universal mathematical conjectures. The [experiments and byte ledger](../implementation/static-memory/experiments.md) provide the comparison framework.

### Eleven-square packing and certificate trust

**Finite geometric optimality with a supplied Lean verification record; proof-production connection.** At [revision `cdc746ed`](https://github.com/Queuingtheorydotcom/11SquaresFormalized/tree/cdc746ed907d258057c283aeb6d077cb2c27e349), `ElevenSquare.optimality` states both an attaining packing and a lower bound for every packing of eleven unit squares in a square container. The [assembly theorem](https://github.com/Queuingtheorydotcom/11SquaresFormalized/blob/cdc746ed907d258057c283aeb6d077cb2c27e349/ElevenSquare/Optimality.lean) is unconditional in its statement; its transitive trust still matters.

### Integer 3SUM and APSP formalization

**October preprint refutations with public Lean proofs; algorithm-status and proof-review connections.** [Alman and Vassilevska Williams's 5 October preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06783v1) derives the improvements from computing selected entries of thin matrix products. It also claims Las Vegas real-RAM 3SUM in `O(n^1.998)` expected time and APSP/min-plus in `O(n^2.998)` expected time; those real-input execution bounds are outside the formal machine results below.

Anthropic's [formalization at revision `e1a4e650`](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/e1a4e6508154ea59f030480661590a9fe3018011/3sum-apsp) separates the five headline claims in [EndStatement.lean](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/e1a4e6508154ea59f030480661590a9fe3018011/3sum-apsp/EndStatement.lean) from their proofs. They assert deterministic word-RAM programs for 3SUM in `O(n^1.9992)`, min-plus product and APSP in `O(n^2.99942)`, Exact Triangle in `O(n^(3-0.0017))`, and zero-weight `k`-Clique in `O(n^(k-0.0017*floor(k/3)))` for every fixed `k >= 3`. Inputs are polynomially bounded integers; APSP concerns directed graphs without negative cycles and distinguishes unreachable pairs. For each bound exponent on input magnitudes, the statement chooses a program and a constant word-width multiplier that must work for every size and every sufficiently wide word. The 3SUM predicate requires distinct positions; the output problems require the result written after the input.

These are fixed polynomial improvements, so the corresponding integer hardness hypotheses cannot be presented as simply open while omitting this evidence. The [README's scope and checking instructions](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/e1a4e6508154ea59f030480661590a9fe3018011/3sum-apsp/README.md) exclude machine running-time proofs for real inputs and several conditional paper results. The configurations request statement/dependency comparison, an audit allowing only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and checking with Lean and nanoda through Comparator. Its challenge placeholders are intentionally separate from solution proofs. The ordinary check script does not run Comparator, and the README records no Comparator execution. Separately, [upstream CI run 37411656515](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/actions/runs/37411656515/job/112101231128) reports successful build and Comparator steps for `3sum-apsp` at the inspected revision. This review inspected source and supplied CI status without independently replaying either check.

The [machine and implementation account](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/e1a4e6508154ea59f030480661590a9fe3018011/3sum-apsp/docs/MACHINE.md) prevents a practical-speedup inference. It gives existential programs with some noncomputed choices, no space bound for the headline claims, zero-initialized memory and unit-cost arithmetic on words growing with input size. Its Corollary 26 fast data-structure branch requires `D > 4^59` and `N >= D^18`, hence `N > 2^2124`; below its `D` threshold queries use direct inner products. The headline programs inherit that construction. This is evidence about asymptotic possibility, with no demonstrated finite-size win on the target machine. A [selected Wasm or producer workload](../implementation/contracts/wasm-execution.md#mathematical-research-handoff) still needs an executable algorithm, representation and overflow proof, complete memory and initialization costs, bounded execution and a measured comparison. The result supplies no general interpreter acceleration, ordinary matrix-multiplication exponent improvement, or refutation of ETH, SETH, Orthogonal Vectors or OMv without a separate argument.

### Randomized online allocation and the Luby-Naor-Orda conjecture

**Resolution claimed in an August 2026 submission; direct fragmentation connection.** [Bender et al., Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28462v1), prove an `Omega(log M)` expected competitive-ratio lower bound for randomized, nonmoving, contiguous online allocation, where `M` is peak live volume and the cost is peak address span relative to `M`. The bound holds even when `M` is known, matches the upper bound, and resolves the Luby-Naor-Orda conjecture in that model. The [28 August preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28462v1) is marked "in submission"; no independent proof reproduction was performed here.

The same paper studies deliberately splitting requests. For a fixed aggregate fragmentation factor `k > 1` and unknown peak volume bounded by `Mbar`, it gives a tight `Theta(log log Mbar)` ratio. The budget compares all-time peak simultaneous fragments with all-time peak simultaneous requests. A fixed per-request fragment limit is different and retains a logarithmic ratio in `M`.

The lower bound limits what randomization alone can promise for an elastic heap. The [bounded-pieces research handoff](static-memory-research.md#bounded-pieces-instead-of-mandatory-contiguity) owns possible representation experiments and their capability, metadata, revocation and access costs. These online bounds neither solve fixed offline placement nor provide a deadline or finite-arena exhaustion guarantee.

### Stochastic first-fit packing

**Expanded result in a 24 September 2026 preprint revision.** [Ernst, Stolyar and Wang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03797v3#S4), analyze stationary first-fit packing on the unbounded positive half-axis. Arrivals are Poisson with rate `r`, lifetimes are independent unit-mean exponentials, and positive item sizes are independent and identically distributed with finite mean `M`; their support is a finite increasing sequence or a countable increasing sequence tending to infinity. As `r` grows, empty space within `[0,r*M)`, divided by `r`, converges to zero in probability. This extends the earlier two-size result to the stated discrete distributions.

This motivates an allocation comparison for a workload that can justify those assumptions. It does not bound the rightmost occupied address, finite-capacity allocation failure, adversarial fragmentation or per-request latency. A bounded elastic-domain experiment must measure those quantities separately and account for quarantine and reclamation; the asymptotic occupancy result alone cannot size the platform's reserved pool.

### Finite matrix multiplication with 48 products

**Concrete 2025-2026 preprint constructions.** [Dumas, Pernet and Sedoglavic's June 2025 algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13242v7) replaces the complex coefficients of AlphaEvolve's `48`-product scheme for `4` by `4` matrices with rational ones, valid over rings containing an inverse of `2`. Their [29 July revision of a more accurate variant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18699v3) improves numerical error bounds. [Dumas, Pernet, Sedoglavic and Tichavský's 10 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12027v1) gives a construction using `48` multiplications and `216` additions, subtractions or constant scalings, with recursive leading cost `7.75*n^log_4(48)`; an alternative-basis version lowers the leading term to `6.5*n^log_4(48)`, with basis changes as additional work. In characteristic two, AlphaTensor's scheme uses `47` products. [Perminov's scheme catalogue at revision `60a06a6c`](https://github.com/dronperminov/FastMatrixMultiplication/tree/60a06a6c61e21f2abd50f2e04a6925962c28ef3a) lists rank `48` over the rationals but `49` with integer or `{-1,0,1}` coefficients, so the best listed scheme for integer arithmetic without division by `2` still uses `49` products. These are explicit finite algorithms, without establishing optimality of `48` or resolving [the exponent-two conjecture](#matrix-multiplication-exponent-equals-two).

**Small-format frontier.** [Wang's 11 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06725v2) records that the rank of `3` by `3` multiplication over arbitrary fields remains between Bläser's lower bound of `19` and Laderman's `23`. Over `F_2`, it raises the lower bound to `21` by exhaustive computation, with [code and certificates at revision `f845fb5b`](https://github.com/wcgbg/matrix-multiplication-n333r21f2/tree/f845fb5b87650645478fee70269533d26a722511). [Yang et al.'s 16 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18722v1) gives an independent structural proof attributed to the Qiushi Engine AI system, with Codex-assisted formalization; its [Lean package at revision `4649c032`](https://github.com/Oxelra-AI/Qiushi-Engine-Matmul-Research/tree/4649c03201e1974ee2f97b7de53c5f96e17cf3a8) defines rank through bilinear algorithms for `ZMod 2` matrix products and documents a final `rank_ge_21` theorem using only standard axioms. This review inspected those definitions and records without building the package. [Rudich and Rousseau's 1 October preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01639v1) raises the `F_2` lower bound to `22`, bounding all 496 classes of Wang's subproblem table and determining 359 exactly. Through Hopcroft and Kerr's reduction modulo `2`, its Lean theorem `recursive_algorithm_multiplications_at_least_22` states that every straight-line program with integer scaling constants that multiplies `3` by `3` grids of real square blocks of every size performs at least `22` multiplications. Since `log_3 22` exceeds `log_2 7`, no such recursive `3` by `3` algorithm can beat Strassen's exponent. Rational constants, such as an inverse of `2`, and ranks over other fields lie outside the theorem, and whether `22` products suffice remains open. The paper reports a Lean 4.30.0 and Mathlib build using only the three standard axioms, and states that Claude agents wrote the search code and the Lean proofs outside its human-designed audit surface; the search code is withheld until the gap closes or the paper is accepted. This review read the definitions and theorem quoted in the paper, without downloading or building the [Zenodo archive, version 1 of 30 September](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23047901).

For the rank-23 scheme, [Karunaratne and Idamekorala's 28 July preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28676v1) needs `55` additions with `{-1,0,1}` coefficients over any associative ring. Alternative-basis kernels need fewer additions but add basis conversions: [Nielsen's 6 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06588v1) uses `52` in its kernel, and [Stapleton and Perminov's 26 September note](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32388v1) `51`, plus five conversion additions, for `56` in total. That note's Table 1 counts `12` conversions, `64` in total, when replaying Nielsen's published maps. Its programs use only unit signs and no divisions, so they apply over any associative ring; it discloses AI-assisted tools for code, experiment orchestration and review, and its [certificate and NumPy checker at revision `473661e5`](https://github.com/Joshua-Stapleton/51-addition-alt-basis-3x3-rank23/tree/473661e52ea9d6db7988e038212b4de9f34aa6e1) were not run here. A single literal block pays its conversions in full, so `55` remains the lowest complete count there; the note leaves open how the kernel-conversion balance affects recursive implementations. Since `log_3 23` exceeds `log_2 7`, recursive use of any of these schemes is asymptotically slower than Strassen's. [Perminov's June revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02480v2) reports rank improvements for 207 of the 680 formats up to `16` by `16` by `16`, and 23 new schemes whose recursive exponent is below `log_2 7`.

The constructions are candidates for the [compute arithmetic handoff](../implementation/contracts/compute-semantic.md#arithmetic-research-handoff). Algebraic operation counts must be supplemented by scratch, transfers, conversions, tails and target-cycle measurements. Invertibility of `2` is not available in every modular integer domain, and algebraic equivalence does not imply FP32 bit equality. The frozen pilot compares against a particular zero-ULP reference sequence; a different reduction order requires a separately admitted entry and reference. No construction or target benchmark was independently reproduced in this review.

### Pinwheel scheduling: the 5/6 theorem and relaxed synthesis

**Published resolution, STOC 2024 and PNAS August 2026.** Kawamura proves that integer-period, unit-service pinwheel instances with `sum_i 1/a_i <= 5/6` are schedulable. The [paper and supporting material](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kawamura/pinwheel/) provide computer-assisted proof data and schedules for reduced instances; the journal version, PNAS 123(32), is also [posted as a 10 August arXiv revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27104v2). Its fractional periods are a proof device for integer instances, not the general real-period theorem below. This is a sufficient condition: density above `5/6` does not alone establish infeasibility. Candidate maintenance or replenishment cadences could be checked against the [schedule-record contract](../implementation/contracts/schedule-record.md), provided each service and its overhead fit a common unit slot and additional conflicts are modeled.

**April 2026 result, accepted at FOCS 2026.** [Kleinberg and Mishra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13974v1), listed among [FOCS 2026 accepted papers](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/), prove NP-hardness of ordinary explicitly listed instances with exponentially large periods, later strengthened to [strong NP-hardness](#pinwheel-scheduling-certificates-and-complexity), and give an EPTAS that either correctly reports original periods `A` infeasible or certifies relaxed periods `(1+epsilon)A` feasible. Its parameter dependence is extremely large. This offers a theoretical service-versus-resource tradeoff, not permission to relax an admitted deadline. The [certificate-size questions](#pinwheel-scheduling-certificates-and-complexity) remain distinct.

**Real-period special case, September GitHub artifact.** Real periods require at least `ell` executions in every `ceil(ell*a_i)` slots, for every positive integer `ell`. [Fujiwara, Miyagi and Ouchi's August 2026 publication](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/18808) proves the `5/6` condition for three distinct real period values; its [final version](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24068v6) leaves four values as future work. The repository [four-real-periods at revision `21ce82b3`](https://github.com/spaghettic0de/four-real-periods/tree/21ce82b3a6c65369fb27e89146aa75f20ccc6057), created 8 September, supplies an apparently new special-case proof with disclosed Codex assistance. Its [theorem and `ValidPeriods` definition](https://github.com/spaghettic0de/four-real-periods/blob/21ce82b3a6c65369fb27e89146aa75f20ccc6057/CertifiedDiscovery/PinwheelPeriods.lean) quantify exactly four tasks with positive sorted real periods and reciprocal sum at most `5/6`, producing `S : Z -> Fin 4` with the full window property. The [counting definition](https://github.com/spaghettic0de/four-real-periods/blob/21ce82b3a6c65369fb27e89146aa75f20ccc6057/CertifiedDiscovery/PinwheelSchedule.lean) counts actual task executions.

The [external Verify run 34281269930](https://github.com/spaghettic0de/four-real-periods/actions/runs/34281269930) passed at that exact revision. The inspected [verifier](https://github.com/spaghettic0de/four-real-periods/blob/21ce82b3a6c65369fb27e89146aa75f20ccc6057/experiments/verify.py) checks compilation, per-module and fresh final-theorem replay, and rejection of three corrupted certificates. This review inspected sources and external records without independently replaying or fully auditing the proof. The manuscript extends the claim to arbitrary multiplicities of at most four distinct period values through aggregation and splitting; that extension is explicitly unformalized. The general real-period `5/6` conjecture remains open, and an infinite real-period schedule still needs a proved finite periodic realization for `ModeSchedule`.

**Dual covering frontier resolved.** In pinwheel covering, one agent covers each slot while agent `i` appears at most once in every `a_i` slots, imposing a minimum rest gap. [Kawamura and Kobayashi, ESA 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.83), and [Mishra, SODA 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22060), independently prove schedulability whenever density `sum_i 1/a_i` is at least `sum_(j>=0) 1/(2^j+1)`, approximately `1.264`, and show this universal threshold is optimal. The [author's inspected solver repository](https://github.com/a7kawamura/pinwheel_solver/tree/c3b7e4c10df0a683da4f5462152e8a0313b55aaf) provides finite state-graph searches and exhaustive lemma checks, without a local replay here. Covering's minimum-rest condition is distinct from the original model's maximum service gap; its threshold cannot certify replenishment cadence.

### Ordinary Komlós and Beck-Fiala proof announcements

**September 2026 preprint claims.** [Guo, Fang and Lu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11189v1) announce final-sum discrepancy below `3*sqrt(2*pi)` for unit-Euclidean-norm vectors, implying below `3*sqrt(2*pi*t)` for a set system in which each element occurs in at most `t` sets; they credit the Odin Automatic AI Research Agent with discovering the proof. [Karingula and Lovett's 22 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20979v2) gives a simplified proof with constant `36`, refined with ChatGPT assistance. [Akbas and Sra's 29 September revision, Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27172v2#S2.SS3), keeps a quadratic-energy proof below `3*pi` and, through a finer estimate of the product-cosine density's overlap with its translates, lowers the constant to `C_cos < 6.9013`; exact-rational scripts in its [repository](https://github.com/suvrit/komlos/tree/88db0a692951f6a12ad54717d610d7e3dab8abd6/existence) certify the constant's enclosure. The [prefix counterexample](#strong-or-prefix-komlós-conjecture) and [polynomial-time constructions](#efficient-constant-discrepancy-komlós-construction) are separate subsequent results.

Useful algorithms need not wait: [Bansal and Jiang's 2025 work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03961) gives polynomial-time `O(sqrt(t))` Beck-Fiala discrepancy when `t >= log^2 n`, plus improved general Komlós bounds. Sparse resource assignments could use such algorithms to propose checked candidates. Normalization, legal placement choices and actual capacity constraints still need to match the theorem.

**Online prefix advances, July and September 2026 preprints.** For arbitrary vectors of Euclidean norm at most one, [Aden-Ali's 5 July preprint, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04388v1), gives a randomized online algorithm running in `O(dT)` time on an obliviously fixed sequence of `T` vectors in `R^d`, keeping every prefix's maximum-coordinate magnitude at most `sqrt(18 log(2T^2/delta))` with probability at least `1-delta`. This makes Kulkarni, Reis and Rothvoss's optimal online bound, previously attained in exponential time, efficient. The time bound assumes exact real arithmetic, exponentials and continuous sampling. The author states that GPT-5.5 Pro discovered the algorithm and main proof, which the author verified and rewrote.

[Altschuler and Tikhomirov's 15 July result](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14238v1) gives an efficient randomized online signing algorithm with `O(sqrt(d))` prefix discrepancy for a fixed sequence of `T` vectors in `[-1,1]^n`, each with at most `d` nonzeros. For fixed `eta > 0`, a sufficient regime is `d >= C log(T) (log log(T))^(2+eta)`, with an explicit failure-probability bound. The sequence must be fixed by an oblivious adversary, independent of the algorithm's random choices. The authors credit ChatGPT-generated proofs followed by manual checking and rewriting. This controls every prefix with irrevocable choices, a separate contribution from final-sum Komlós. It proposes candidates for the [resource-prefix handoff](../implementation/static-memory/resource-contracts.md#research-handoff-for-ordering-and-prefix-bounds); a probabilistic discrepancy guarantee supplies neither nonnegative inventory nor deterministic capacity safety.

Their [14 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14975v1) obtains optimal online prefix discrepancy `Theta(max(sqrt(d), log log n))` with high probability for independent uniformly random `d`-sparse binary vectors, `2 <= d <= n/2`, and `T = Theta(n)`, including an efficient upper-bound algorithm. It also discloses AI assistance. The distribution and horizon restrictions are essential; this is not an unrestricted prefix theorem.

**Formal artifacts inspected.** [Dahia's Lean solution at revision `d8028574`](https://github.com/gdahia/Komlos/blob/d802857449234318d557361b1dbb0a27e0258528/Solution.lean) gives the constant-36 real-vector theorem and `36*sqrt(t)` Beck-Fiala consequence. Its solution imports the completed library, excluding the separate challenge placeholders. The [external CI build passed at that revision](https://github.com/gdahia/Komlos/actions/runs/35320354586), and [formalization metadata](https://github.com/gdahia/Komlos/blob/d802857449234318d557361b1dbb0a27e0258528/formalization.yaml) reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. This review inspected sources and that build record, without independently replaying or fully auditing the proof. The artifact documents AI assistance; it formalizes neither the sharper constants, prefix discrepancy nor the polynomial-time algorithms.

Two later developments cover the final-sum theorem separately. The `3*sqrt(2*pi)` constant has its own [Lean library at revision `ec64f1bc`](https://github.com/junwei-lu/Komlos/tree/ec64f1bcda0661b55f864627ba7e4be0fb815ac0), formalizing the Guo-Fang-Lu paper with a coauthor's repository: [`Komlos/Main.lean`](https://github.com/junwei-lu/Komlos/blob/ec64f1bcda0661b55f864627ba7e4be0fb815ac0/Komlos/Main.lean) states signs with every coordinate strictly below `3*sqrt(2*pi)` for vectors of Euclidean norm at most one, and `3*sqrt(2*pi*t)` for `t`-sparse `{0,1}` columns. Its README reports only the three standard axioms and a compile-time `sorry` gate. A second constant-36 proof, following Karingula and Lovett, is the [`Komlos.lean` file of a Formal Conjectures fork at revision `416b6597`](https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/416b6597cb193b6745748fdfef0d64342c71dae9/FormalConjecturesForMathlib/Combinatorics/Discrepancy/Komlos.lean), which the [upstream tracker at revision `e6fac203`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/e6fac203f1f66c8550969c93530ad6462e05fb7b/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/KomlosConjecture.lean) cites in tagging the conjecture solved. This review inspected those statements and found no `sorry`, `axiom` or `native_decide` in the main files; neither package was built, and no external build record was located for either revision.

### Square-root-space simulation and circuit evaluation

**2025 theorem, journal publication June 2026, and related CCC 2026 result.** [Williams, STOC 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17779v1), published in the [Journal of the ACM on 16 June 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3798104), proves that a multitape Turing computation taking time `t >= n` can be simulated in `O(sqrt(t log t))` space, building on Cook and Mertz's tree-evaluation technique. [Shalunov, CCC 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.3), gives the corresponding `O(sqrt(s log s))` workspace bound for evaluating a circuit of size `s`, through a direct reduction to tree evaluation. [Tree Evaluation in logarithmic space](#tree-evaluation-in-logarithmic-space) would remove the logarithmic factor.

[Williams's MFCS 2026 invited account](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2026.3) surveys this same bound and its tree-evaluation basis. It provides a current account of the established simulation, without a stronger space bound or a resolution of P versus PSPACE.

These results support exploring recomputation and alternative evaluation orders to reduce intermediate storage. They do not preserve the original runtime, provide a direct arbitrary-RAM transformation, or merely repack an unchanged live set. [Williams's Remark 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17779v1) covers random-access machines only when access is oblivious: if the registers touched at step `i` can be computed from `i` in `O(sqrt(t))` space, the theorem applies with a polylogarithmic extra factor. A pass with a data-independent access schedule is therefore a closer target than pointer-chasing code. A bounded implementation could be investigated in the [resident compiler](../spec.md#r-13-027) or a guest algorithm, with explicit costs for recomputation, input access and polling. This is distinct from reducing allocator fragmentation.

**Beyond multitape machines, FOCS 2026 full version posted 2 October.** [Sibgatullin and Williams](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/223/), listed among [FOCS 2026 accepted papers](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/), define a free-input parallel external memory model: `P` processors, each a RAM with `M` bits of private memory, share `S` bits of external memory. In each round a processor may compute arbitrarily within its memory with free read-only input access and exchange up to `M` arbitrarily chosen external bits. Their Theorem 1.1 simulates `T` rounds on one machine in `O(T*log(P*S*M) + M)` space, when the bound functions are computable within that space and `M >= log n`, through a [generalized Cook-Mertz DAG evaluator](#tree-evaluation-in-logarithmic-space); the bound depends on rounds, not per-round work, and subsumes Williams's theorem. Applications give `O(sqrt(n log n))` space for the P-complete lexicographically first satisfying assignment of a 2CNF with `n` variables and `O(n^2)` clauses, `O(sqrt(n log n))` space for `n`-gate unbounded fan-in circuits over `{NOT, AND, OR, XOR}`, and `O((t log t)^(1-1/(d+1)))` space for `t` steps of a `d`-dimensional cellular automaton. The theorems bound space, not simulation time. The authors call extension to random-access machines the area's most significant open problem and suggest as plausible that time-`t` RAMs fit in `t^(1-epsilon)` rounds with `M <= t^(1-epsilon)` and `P, S <= poly(t)`, for some `epsilon > 0`. They disclose Claude and ChatGPT use for figures and proofreading, and credit Claude with the transitive-closure observation behind the `O(n^omega)` time baseline for that 2SAT problem. This review read the introduction, model definition and theorem statements, not the proofs.

A resident pass benefits only when expressed in far fewer rounds than its running time, with each round's transfers bounded by private memory. The processors, private memories and shared store describe the simulated algorithm, not physical cores; the single simulating machine recomputes them. Free input reads and arbitrary-subset transfers are model idealizations, and the existing equivalence, activation and timing obligations still apply.

A [separate claim of `O(sqrt(t))` simulation space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14831v4) was withdrawn on 1 January 2026: its interval-based associative summary tree did not model the dependencies in Williams's simulation. That withdrawn strengthening does not supersede the established bound. The same author's [December 2025 revision of a second manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00607v2) restates `O(sqrt(t))` tape cells over a fixed alphabet, but its appendix takes Theorems 1 to 3, including the simulation itself, from that withdrawn manuscript as black-box inputs; it therefore supplies no independent proof.

**One-writable-tape claim, OpenAI family 137.** The [manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Simulating-One-Tape-Time-in-Two-Fifths-Power-Space-September-25-2026/article.pdf) claims simulation of a fixed machine's finite-control and halting outcome through time `T` in `O(T^(2/5)*polylog(T+2))` workspace. The catalogue specifies one writable tape, finitely many read-only heads, local head movement and polylogarithmic-space access to initial symbols; simulation time is unrestricted. No Lean link accompanies this family. This review inspected the catalogue's model and bound, not the proof. It is a narrower-model advance to evaluate, not a general RAM or multitape `2/5` bound, a proof of `P != PSPACE`, or a deadline-preserving resident-pass optimization.

### Catalytic graph and sequence algorithms

**Published 2026 algorithms.** [Cook and Pyne, ITCS 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.43), give explicit catalytic graph algorithms with `O(log n)` clean workspace. For directed reachability, [their preprint's Theorems 1 and 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06209v1) give deterministic `O-tilde(n^3 m)` time with `O-tilde(n^2)` catalytic bits, and randomized one-sided-error `O-tilde(nm)` time with `O-tilde(n)` catalytic bits, restoring the catalyst for every random choice. [Chmel et al., CCC 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.29), obtain polynomial-time algorithms using `O(log n)` clean workspace and sublinear catalytic storage `n / 2^(Theta(sqrt(log n)))` for directed reachability and several sequence problems, including edit distance and longest common subsequence.

**General-graph matching preprint.** [Chakraborty et al., revision 1 on 1 June 2026](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/080/), construct maximum matchings in general graphs in polynomial-time catalytic logspace. They also address maximum-rank completion and fixed-factor approximation of maximum rank in Edmonds' problem. This is a concrete lead for a matching-based producer pass, beyond bipartite matching and reachability. Polynomial catalyst capacity, exact restoration and bounded activations remain implementation obligations in the [resident-pass comparison](../performance/toolchain-residency.md#research-leads-for-bounded-resident-passes); the result does not put general matching in ordinary deterministic logspace.

Compiler graph passes and text tools are plausible consumers inside a private memory envelope. The retained payload remains physically present, and all work needed to restore it must be charged. [Catalytic memory's](#the-power-of-catalytic-memory) authority, quiescence, capability-tag and interruption qualifications are essential. A separate [April 2026 claim of polynomial-time, almost-logarithmic-total-space tree evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02606v2) was withdrawn because its polynomial-degree analysis was incorrect; it is not evidence of that stronger result.

**Further July-September 2026 preprints.** [Becker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08559) and [Kaplan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09475) develop multi-pass catalytic streaming algorithms, with no asymptotic clean-space advantage in the one-pass model. Replaying input and retaining catalyst capacity are part of the cost. [Vinciguerra's 16 September operator approach](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18692v1) proves at least four input accesses necessary for passive-output polynomial programs computing polynomials of degree at least three over characteristic-zero fields and constructs `x^(2t-1)` using four accesses and `t` registers in characteristic zero or greater than `2t-1`. It credits AI assistance and derives streaming and matrix-powering improvements. These scoped algebraic results leave `CL = L` and `P` containment in `CL` unresolved.

### Time-space cost of composing low-space passes

**FOCS 2026 accepted paper; March 2026 report revision.** For two passes that each run in linear time with `O(log n)` workspace, storing the intermediate output costs linear space, while recomputing each requested intermediate bit keeps logarithmic space but costs quadratic time. [Pyne and Tell's revised report, Theorem 1.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/140/revision/1/download/), whose proof they credit to Williams, gives two such length-preserving multitape-machine functions for which every algorithm computing the composition correctly on more than an `epsilon`-fraction of inputs has time-space product at least `n^1.33`. The proof writes sorting as such a composition, so it inherits the sorting time-space lower bounds, which hold even for RAM algorithms. Their Proposition 4.1 interpolates with `b` checkpoints: time `O-tilde(t^2/b)` and space `O(s*b)` for components running in time `t` and space `s`. When the consuming pass reads its input once in order, composition takes linear time and logarithmic space. The paper appears on the [FOCS 2026 accepted list](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/).

This quantifies a fusion choice for [resident producer passes](../performance/toolchain-residency.md#research-leads-for-bounded-resident-passes): a streaming consumer can fuse without an intermediate buffer, whereas random access to an intermediate artifact can force either buffer space or polynomial recomputation. Checkpointing gives an explicit curve between buffer bytes and replay time to measure.

The lower bound concerns constructed functions derived from sorting and asymptotic multitape or RAM costs; it proves no overhead for a particular compiler pass pair. The paper shows that extending it to large polynomial time or to many-fold composition would imply average-case derandomization of [`BPL`](#derandomization-p-equals-bpp), and leaves those extensions open. Bounded activations, polling and target timing still need measurement.

### Sparse linear programs and short circuit walks

**Solved restricted algorithm and 2026 circuit-walk advance.** [Dadush et al.](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.2) give strongly polynomial optimization when every row, or every column, has at most two nonzero coefficients. [Natura's 10 February revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06958v2) proves an `O(m^2 log m)` monotone circuit-diameter bound for standard-form polyhedra with `m` equality constraints; the paper is [accepted at FOCS 2026](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/). Circuit steps are more general than edge steps; this is not a proof of the ordinary polynomial Hirsch conjecture or a general strongly polynomial LP algorithm.

The sparse theorem is a concrete option when a resource-assignment subproblem actually has that structure. The circuit-walk theorem offers a new target for search algorithms. Neither turns arbitrary contiguous placement into a linear program, and the [general algorithmic question](#strongly-polynomial-linear-programming) remains open.

### Goldberg-Seymour and the number of transfer rounds

**Published resolution, September 2025.** For a loopless multigraph with maximum degree `Delta`, let `Gamma` be the maximum of `2*|E(U)|/(|U|-1)` over odd vertex sets `U` of size at least three, with zero when there is no such set. [Chen, Jing and Zang](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10878-025-01348-6) prove `chi'(G) <= max(Delta+1,ceil(Gamma))`.

Under the [endpoint-conflict transfer model](#list-edge-coloring-conjecture), this bounds the number of matching rounds, with repeated transfers represented by parallel edges. It gives a useful lower-bound comparison and target for checked schedule synthesis. Per-transfer slot lists, routed conflicts and unequal durations remain additional problems; the theorem is not a complete schedule generator for the platform.

**Constructive preprints in dispute.** The published proof above is nonalgorithmic. [Chen, Hao, Yu and Zang's July 2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09403v1), still at its first version, gives an `O(|V|^5 |E|^3)` algorithm attaining the same bound. [Jing's 13 September 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15588v7), first posted in August 2023, claims a polynomial-time algorithm using `max(Delta+1, ceil(Gamma))` colors in `O(|E|(|V|+Delta)Delta^6)` time, or `O(|E|(|V|+Delta)Delta^5)` with the target precomputed. Its Remark 2 asserts that Chen-Hao-Yu-Zang's Lemma 3.2 fails when a chain shares an endpoint with their elementary set under a stated color configuration. Jing discloses a GPT-6 Pro audit of Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 during August and September 2026, with every resulting revision evaluated by the author. This review inspected the claims and the asserted gap, not either complete proof; neither algorithm is established here.

A proper edge coloring is checkable directly, so either algorithm can propose candidates; only the guaranteed color count depends on the disputed proofs. The exponents and the platform's additional scheduling constraints still matter.

### Hashing beyond the uniform-probing conjecture

**FOCS 2024 results, with STOC 2025 and STOC 2026 progress.** [Farach-Colton, Krapivin and Kuszmaul](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02305v2) disprove Yao's conjectured optimal worst-case expected search cost for greedy open addressing without reordering. Their funnel hashing improves the dependence on empty fraction `delta` from order `1/delta` to order `log^2(1/delta)` in its stated range. Their non-greedy elastic hashing also achieves constant amortized expected search cost without relocating existing entries. The [FOCS paper](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2024-Papers/pdfs/FOCS2024-1oojWxXs5YAKfs3z3lBRMF/167400a594/167400a594.pdf) establishes the publication date. The same authors' [STOC 2026 follow-on](https://doi.org/10.1145/3798129.3800823), *Greedy Open Addressing Revisited: Beyond Yao's Lower Bound*, separates query probe order from insertion order: greedy insertion can then coexist with `O(1)` amortized expected queries and `O(log(1/delta))` worst-case expected queries. These guarantees concern that relaxed probing model.

Deletions and resizing have separate results. For classical, oblivious open addressing, [Bender, Kuszmaul and Zhou, FOCS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11280v1), give rainbow hashing at load factor up to `1-delta` with `O(1)` expected queries and `O(log log(1/delta))` expected insertions and deletions, including dynamic resizing, and prove `Omega(log log(1/delta))` expected time per operation necessary. Their [STOC 2025 non-oblivious construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13628v1) stores only elements and free slots yet supports constant-time operations with high probability even when the table is full, using `O(1)` hash functions of `O(1)`-wise independence.

**July-September 2026 preprints on linear probing.** [Krapivin, Kuszmaul and Wang's 11 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17494v2) gives a non-greedy insertion strategy without reordering: with target load `1-1/x` known in advance, worst-case expected insertion time falls from `Theta(x^2)` to `O(x log x)`, and stable strategies need `Omega(x sqrt(log x))`. [Zamir](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16390v1) defines locality as the distance from the first probe to the farthest cell inspected or used. No open addressing without reordering achieves `o(1/epsilon^2)` locality simultaneously at every load `1-epsilon`, and immutable schemes need pages of `Omega(1/epsilon^2)` slots for `1+o(1)` expected page span. Locality, unlike probe count, bears on cache-line and bank traffic. [Azarmehr, Bender, Kuszmaul and Silver's 30 September preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00688v1), accepted at [FOCS 2026](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/), gives each key a choice of two hash functions in ordered linear probing, analyzing insertions and queries. If a key's choice never changes, `O(log(1/epsilon))` expected queries with `O(1/epsilon)` amortized expected insertions at load `1-epsilon` are optimal up to constants; letting keys switch hash functions gives `O(1)` expected queries, even in a full table, with `O(epsilon^(-1/2))` expected insertions. A query can then start at two table positions, so its cache-line traffic needs separate measurement.

This can inform denser fixed-capacity application tables, reducing spare slots and avoiding some entry movement. Expected and high-probability costs are not deterministic WCET, successful searches are not every dictionary operation, and deletion or resizing guarantees come only from a result that states them. Hash-table slack is also distinct from general heap fragmentation. Applications must qualify a bounded implementation within their existing memory and authority envelope.

An [experimental Rust implementation, `opthash-rs` revision `d7a408a3`](https://github.com/aaron-ang/opthash-rs/tree/d7a408a3dccdd48ccd5cc62f83397f35a0995412), makes the elastic and funnel constructions available for finite-workload evaluation. Its own comparison documents deterministic mixing, tombstones, growth, rebuilds and exhaustion fallbacks outside the paper's fixed-table insertion-only analysis. This is an implementation lead, not evidence that every library operation inherits the theorem; no target benchmark was run in this review.

### Compact static dictionaries and dynamic ordered indexes

**Published 2025 and 2026 results, with an August 2026 preprint advance.** [Hu et al., STOC 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10655v2), obtain static dictionaries with worst-case constant query time and `OPT+n^epsilon` total bits for fixed positive `epsilon`, under stated word-RAM and universe assumptions; `OPT = log_2 binom(U,n) + n log_2 sigma`. Construction uses randomness, and auxiliary tables/hash descriptions count toward the total. [Kuszmaul, Liang and Zhou, SODA 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19175v1), obtain dynamic ordered dictionaries supporting insertion, deletion, rank and select with sublinear redundancy for polynomial-size universes and optimal `O(1 + log n/log log U)` amortized expected operation time. For a static ordered set, [Liang and Zhou, ICALP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19350v1), give rank and select in `O(t + log log n)` time with `log_2 binom(U,n) + n/(log U/t)^Omega(t)` bits, matching known lower bounds for `t <= log^0.99 n`.

[Blelloch et al., 6 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06077v1), address clustered keys through difference encoding. Their preprint gives space `(1+O(epsilon))*gap(S) + O(n log(gap(S)/n))` bits and expected amortized operation time `O(log(1/epsilon)/log log(1/epsilon))`, for `0 < epsilon < 1/4` and `n = U^(1-Theta(1))`, with a matching tradeoff lower bound even for static queries. Its corollaries add predecessor and successor queries with an additive `O(log log U)` term and rank and select with an additive `O(log n/log log U)` term. Gap entropy can be much smaller than the unrestricted-set information bound; this is a distinct opportunity for clustered application indexes, without a per-operation deadline guarantee.

**Worst-case dynamic rank and select, October 2026 preprint.** [Domingues's 1 October preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01853v1) gives deterministic dynamic rank/select dictionaries with worst-case operation times in the multiplication word-RAM with `w`-bit words. It allows lookup tables of `polylog w` words computable in linear time and measures space by the largest memory address used. For `n = w^O(1)` keys from a universe of size `u` and `1 <= k <= log* w`, rank, select and updates take `O(k)` time in `log_2 binom(u,n) + O(n log^(k) w)` bits, with `log^(k)` the `k`-fold iterated logarithm; a fully indexable dictionary of any size takes `O(log_w n + k)` time. Its Theorem 4 shows `Omega(k)` time necessary for membership or updates at redundancy `O(n log^(k) n)` when `log u = Theta(w)` and `n <= sqrt(u)`. This redundancy exceeds the SODA 2026 structure's sublinear term, but the deterministic worst-case bounds avoid the expected-time limitation noted below; the target cost of each word operation still decides any deadline.

**Implementation lead, PVLDB 2026.** [Tang, Mai, Kuszmaul and Conway](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28892v1) engineer tiny-pointer hash tables with deletions, resizing and `O(1)` expected-time operations. Their chained variant uses `B + O(n log log n)` bits, with `B` the information-theoretic optimum, and measured footprints below the raw key-value size on an x86 server. The [BSD-2-Clause C implementation at revision `d021bf85`](https://github.com/Xilinion/TPHT/tree/d021bf85cfba8241f0e01804785e52c8067dcaaf) accepts 32- or 64-bit keys and 1- to 8-byte values. Its README states that fixed tables absorb overflow by growing internally, whereas the paper's analyzed variant returns an error on a failed allocation; a fixed-memory adaptation needs a declared exhaustion result with all backing charged. Sources were inspected; no benchmark was run here.

The static result is especially compatible with composition-time construction of immutable metadata. The dynamic result could reduce index overhead inside applications. Neither eliminates CHERI representation costs or supplies a bound in target cycles. A checked construction needs to account for every lookup table, hash seed, encoded field, initialization step and construction workspace; expected update bounds do not establish a fixed-tier deadline.

### Dynamic entropy-encoded arrays

**August 2026 preprint, accepted at FOCS 2026.** [Blelloch et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06066v1) present mutable arrays with constant-time indexed reads and updates and space approaching empirical zero-order entropy, plus alphabet-support and lower-order terms; the [conference acceptance list](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/) records the paper. This addresses the longstanding tension between compression and constant-time modification. Crucially, the theorem uses a virtual-memory model and gives its time and space bounds with high probability. It does not claim the same bounds for every random choice on this platform. Its additive `n/polylog n` term makes the space `(1+o(1))` times the entropy only when the entropy is at least `n/polylog n`.

Possible consumers include compressible application metadata and symbol arrays. The introduction also claims a succinct dynamic unordered dictionary with resizing, using `(1+o(1))(log_2 binom(U,n) + n log_2 |V|)` bits for values in `V`, with constant-time operations with high probability in the current `n`; that application's proof was not reviewed here. The paper's virtual memory is an abstract resizable-buffer model with a software simulation, so hardware address translation is not a prerequisite. A VerifiedOS implementation still needs to qualify that simulation and charge its physical backing, pointers, capability bounds and allocation overhead. Content-dependent compressed size must stay within the authorized observation model. This is a representation lead; it does not change the fixed-memory contract or make incompressible data smaller.

### Directed shortest paths below the sorting barrier

**Published 2025 breakthrough and 2026 improvement.** [Duan et al., STOC 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17033), give deterministic directed single-source shortest paths with nonnegative real weights in `O(m log^(2/3) n)` comparison-addition operations. [The ICALP 2026 improvement](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.81) gives `O(m sqrt(log n) + sqrt(m n log n log log n))`. These results break the old sorting barrier for sparse graphs; they do not settle the [weighted APSP hypothesis](#weighted-all-pairs-shortest-paths-hypothesis).

**Measured implementation.** [Castro, Clementino and de Freitas's February 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03007v3) implements the STOC 2025 algorithm in C++. Dijkstra remained 3 to 4 times faster on every tested sparse random graph, grid and road network, up to 10 million vertices, and the authors estimate that their worst-case variant needs vastly more than `10^67` vertices to win.

**Negative-weight extensions, July and September 2026 preprints.** [Hair, Li, Li and Zhang, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19346v1), give a Las Vegas randomized SSSP algorithm for directed graphs with possibly negative real weights, running in `m^{1+o(1)}` time with high probability. This broadens the admissible weight domain; it does not strengthen the deterministic nonnegative-weight guarantee or provide a deterministic deadline. For integral weights at least `-W`, [Duong's 14 September revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05590v2) gives a deterministic `O((m + n log log n) log^2 n log(nW))` algorithm, a `log n` factor below the previous fastest deterministic bound, due to Li. These single-source bounds do not themselves supply truly subcubic APSP; the [October integer result](#integer-3sum-and-apsp-formalization) is a separate construction.

**Undirected advance, September 2026 preprint.** [Kadria and Roditty](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15247v1) give a randomized comparison-addition algorithm for undirected nonnegative real weights running in `O(m sqrt(log n) (log log n)^(1/4) (log log log n)^(1/4))` time with high probability, improving the FOCS 2023 undirected bound by a `(log log n / log log log n)^(1/4)` factor.

**Universal optimality of distance ordering.** [Haeupler et al., FOCS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11793v4), prove Dijkstra with a working-set heap universally optimal, in running time and comparisons, for the distance-order problem: on every fixed graph, its worst case over weights is within a constant factor of the best algorithm that outputs vertices sorted by distance. [Van der Hoog, Rotenberg and Rutschmann, ESA 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.71), simplify the heap requirement, and a [pointer-machine version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24134v2), published in the [ESA 2026 proceedings on 25 August](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.45), comes within an additive `O(m alpha(m))`. The sub-sorting algorithms above return distances without that order, so the results are compatible. A pass that consumes vertices in distance order has Dijkstra with such a heap as its baseline; these are amortized bounds, not per-operation deadlines.

Matching graph kernels in planning or guest applications could gain from the sub-sorting algorithms only far beyond the measured sizes; Dijkstra with a suitable heap remains the comparison baseline. These results do not imply general faster sorting or faster interpreter dispatch. Concrete weight encoding, overflow, graph representation, scratch space and actual workload sizes must be evaluated before changing an implementation.

**AI-produced formal artifact, September 2026.** [Vals AI's 20 September C-HD announcement](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/faster-shortest-path-algorithm) reports that ten Claude Opus 5.5 agents produced the algorithm and its Lean proof within 15 hours, checked against the specified theorem with the Lean Comparator. It links a [frozen proof package at revision `98c53acc`](https://github.com/spicylemonade/c-hd-proof/tree/98c53accb47a505482a1781597ae14bf67e81cec). Its closed Lean theorem covers exact directed nonnegative-real SSSP and charged runtime in its RAM/comparison-addition model. The improved branch requires `m <= n*floor(floor(log2 n)^(3/4))`; along `m` of order `n log^(3/4) n`, its bound is `O(n log^(11/12) n)`. Small inputs and other densities use a Bellman-Ford fallback. The inspected theorem and package report a full build and kernel replays with standard Lean axioms; these records were not rerun here. One internal agent review is complete and another remains provisional. The broader paper range and linear-space bound are not formalized. This is a concrete new research artifact with enormous constants, no demonstrated practical speedup, and computational-model correspondence and novelty still requiring external review.

### Almost-linear maximum flow and its simplification

**2022 breakthrough, deterministic 2023 version, 2025 combinatorial algorithm and 2026 simplification preprints.** [Chen, Kyng, Liu, Peng, Probst Gutenberg and Sachdeva](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00671v2) compute exact maximum flows and minimum-cost flows on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integral capacities, demands and costs in `m^(1+o(1))` time, using an interior-point method whose steps are approximate minimum-ratio cycles maintained by dynamic data structures. [Van den Brand et al., FOCS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16629v1), make both results deterministic. [Bernstein, Blikstad, Li, Saranurak and Tu, FOCS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17182v1), give a randomized combinatorial exact maximum flow in `O(n^2 log^19 n log U)` time for capacities in `{1,...,U}`, with a complete C++ implementation that the authors call "not at all practical" because of the polylogarithmic factor. Building on [Li's April balancing-weights technique](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14633v1), [Li and Wice's 18 August preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17384v1) removes the outer interior-point method: a deterministic `m^(1+o(1))` pseudo-circulation step, repeated `O(log m)` times on rounded residual graphs, yields exact maximum flow for integral polynomially bounded capacities. It still uses a dynamic minimum-ratio-cut data structure and does not address minimum-cost flow. For minimum-cost flow, [Kyng, Meierhans, Probst Gutenberg and Sulser's 20 September preprint, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23852v1), keeps the reduction to a sequence of approximate minimum-ratio cycle problems but extracts each cycle directly from Kyng, Meierhans and Probst Gutenberg's dynamic distance oracle, giving a simpler deterministic data structure than the earlier algorithms' cycle machinery. For polynomially bounded capacities and costs, it decides whether a flow meeting the demands costs at most a threshold `F`, and returns one if so, in total time `m * exp(O(log^(20/21) m * log log m))`, also while edges are inserted. This review inspected the theorem statement without verifying the proof. The [FOCS 2026 accepted list](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/) also includes Haeupler, Jiang and Saranurak's *Combinatorial Minimum Cost Flow in Almost-Linear Time on Dense Graphs*; no public text was located, so its model and bound are not recorded here.

Bipartite matching, b-matching, transportation and assignment subproblems in [schedule synthesis](../implementation/contracts/schedule-record.md#research-handoff-for-schedule-synthesis) and [resident assignment passes](../performance/toolchain-residency.md#research-leads-for-bounded-resident-passes) reduce to these problems when their constraints are exactly capacities and linear costs. Their outputs have independent certificates: a flow with a cut of equal capacity, or a minimum-cost flow with potentials making every residual reduced cost nonnegative. A faster producer therefore changes offline composition time, not what an independent checker must validate. Until a workload's graph sizes are measured, classical augmenting-path, push-relabel or network-simplex producers remain the comparison baseline.

The almost-linear bounds assume polynomially bounded integers, so byte-granular capacities need that premise checked or a strongly polynomial method. For arbitrary capacities, [Dadush, Orlin, Sidford and Végh](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20368v1) record `O(nm)` as the best general strongly polynomial bound and an `O((nm)^(1-epsilon))` algorithm as open; they give faster strongly polynomial algorithms for structured networks, including deterministic `n^(omega+o(1))` maximum bipartite b-matching. Lifetimes, contiguity, precedence and fabric conflicts fall outside this capacity-and-cost model, and the [general strongly polynomial LP question](#strongly-polynomial-linear-programming) remains separate.

### Capacity-approaching decoding with small workspace

**August 2026 preprint announcement.** [Fathollahi, Ron-Zewi and Wootters](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15937v1) give explicit code families, for infinitely many block lengths `N`, with rate at least `R`, deterministic list decoding at error fraction `1-R-tau`, time `N^(1+tau)` and workspace `N^tau`, for fixed `R` and suitable constant `tau`. Alphabet and list sizes are constant in `N`. The workspace definition excludes read-only random-access input and sequential write-only output. This is not sublinear total resident storage or a streaming-input theorem.

The result could inform scratch budgets for bulk recovery while preserving a high code rate. Finite constants, authenticated selection among candidates, bounded output and target WCET still matter. It does not directly replace tiny fixed-latency memory ECC or imply an attack on a lattice cryptosystem. The [deterministic fully polynomial gap-dependence problem](#deterministic-fully-polynomial-list-decoding) remains a separate frontier.

## How to use this survey

The [owner links above](#how-the-connections-fit-this-machine) lead to research
handoffs beside unfinished proof work. Each handoff identifies a matching
consumer or explicitly deferred workload, the missing reduction or refinement,
and useful positive/refuting examples. The
[static-memory handoff](static-memory-research.md#survey-handoff-to-unfinished-proofs)
also routes the project-specific questions to their existing owners. Relevant
open checklist leaves link to these contracts; the survey owns research status,
while those contracts own the local application. These notes change no estimate,
completion state, accepted premise or implementation selection.

For allocation work, start with Dynamic Storage Allocation, bin-packing gaps and the project's near-laminar questions. For scheduling, start with the precise job and service models, then test whether discrepancy or ordering results preserve their dependencies; record whether each job's core is fixed, since fixed-core precedence scheduling resists constant-factor approximation. For guarantees, distinguish the cost of finding proofs from their size, checking cost and truth under stated assumptions. For Wasm, profile the selected interpreter and guest algorithms before assigning value to an asymptotic result. For cryptography, maintain the existing concrete premise and attack ledger.

Among recent results, compact static dictionaries have a particularly natural composition-time consumer. Pinwheel and edge-coloring theorems offer checkable schedule candidates when their service models match. Memory reallocation and catalytic computation address different ways to use a tight private envelope, but require explicit movement or restoration semantics. Compressed arrays and small-workspace decoding merit finite-size memory measurements before any performance claim. Maximum-flow and matching producers, like edge colorings from the disputed constructive Goldberg-Seymour preprints, emit outputs an independent checker can validate, so their proofs decide producer success rather than admission. Pass-composition bounds bear on whether a resident pipeline fuses streaming stages or buffers random-access intermediates. These are evaluation priorities inferred from the design, not new implementation commitments.

A proposed adoption needs a precise mathematical statement, a matching workload or reduction, and a constructive implementation or checked finite witness. Charge its memory, metadata, communication, initialization, reclamation and worst-case timing; preserve numerical semantics and the authorized information flows. Existing checkers must validate the resulting artifacts. A paper, a heuristic success, or a conjectural ratio supplies none of that evidence by itself.

Refresh status before using an entry in a design decision. Record the actual remaining question when a special case is solved, and distinguish an announced proof, a reviewed theorem, a mechanized theorem and its implementation bridge. This document is a research map; the owning contracts decide whether any result can change the machine.
