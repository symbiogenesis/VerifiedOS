# Critique

*Non-normative. The canonical critique, in three movements: a gap catalogue for the current state of [spec.md](spec.md) and its companions; a review of what the dispositions in [architectural-alternatives.md](architectural-alternatives.md) and [inspirations.md](inspirations.md) rest on, which is not a second copy of the inventories those documents own; and a review of the register machinery itself. All of it is checked against the artifacts: the atomic-requirements register and the eight derived views. Where an entry here and the register disagree, the register wins and this document is defective.*

*References are symbolic: requirement IDs, crown-jewel rows, and section headings, never line numbers, per the register's **traces are bookmarks, not line numbers** rule; a line number is a derived fact restated in a second artifact with nothing checking it, and it goes stale on the next edit to the artifact it cites. Counted figures a document owns are either recomputed by `tools/check.py` from that artifact or stated loosely on purpose; a hand-restated exact figure is a derived fact with nothing checking it. A disposition, a lineage, or a status another document owns is cited here and never restated, for the same reason. When a finding closes or a work-list item lands, delete it, renumber where the numbering is positional, and retarget every cross-reference that pointed at it: no departure notes, no strikethroughs, no closed section; git history is the narrative, and what stands here is the open agenda. The same rule governs a simplification that is adopted or declined outright, and a partly adopted one keeps only the part still in question.*

The review machinery makes the project's real position **countable**, and it is worse than the prose reads. Twenty-six crown-jewel specifications are named; **two** are authored. Ten theorem targets are named; **none** can start, because each is premised on a specification that does not exist. Every hedge the design deleted was deleted on the strength of a verified primary, and **no primary is verified**.

**Not one of the numbered findings below is a legibility problem.** A legibility gap closes by writing a paragraph, and no finding does. What does close by writing, or by a call somebody has to make, is not charged as a finding; it stands on the work list, priced by what closing it costs.

## The architecture's irreducible compromises

*This section is judgment rather than a second findings ledger. Not every unattractive trade is a defect, and a project may remain the wrong product even if every theorem it names is proved. The point is to separate necessary ugliness, limitations of the present verification toolchain, and risks the design's own accounting underweights.*

### The two theses

**The security thesis is strong and the product thesis is unproved, and they are separate propositions.** The first is capability isolation, artifact-level admission, explicit authority, bounded resources and a proof chain reaching down to hardware. The second is that a phone remains useful after giving up most of the mechanisms conventional computers use to accommodate unpredictable work, while depending on a memory process not qualified for this machine. Success at the first does not establish the second. The numbered findings below are mostly about the first; this section, the memory question after it, and the product paragraphs among the unknown unknowns are about the second.

This is a serious research direction for a tightly provisioned, high-assurance appliance, and not yet a credible commitment to a broadly useful personal computer. The danger is not slowness. It is a device that refuses ordinary work, depends on external infrastructure to change its software, and cannot spend idle resources on the foreground task that is waiting, with every refusal correct under its specification. Engineering effort may be declared free; battery energy, usable capacity, physical latency, manufacturing availability and a user's patience cannot, and independent scrutiny is not interchangeable with unlimited project labour. The design should keep its security objectives and become much less protective of the particular mechanisms chosen to achieve them.

The calls this reading raises are carried as work rather than repeated as prose. [The implementation plan](implementation-checklist.md)'s Q-series holds them: Q1 the baseline and its rejection gates, Q2 one single-source lowering route, Q3 the assurance instruments connected to a slice, Q4 and Q5 the demand and placement measurements, Q6 through Q8 the topology, remanence and acceleration decisions, Q9 the compatibility reconsideration, Q10 the product the slice demonstrates, and Q16 the messaging scope the communications reading below raises. This document carries the judgment and names the item that would answer it, and an item that lands deletes the paragraph it answers.

### The three objections

**The two-class memory system is a rational defeat, and still a defeat.**
The conceptually clean machine has one uniform latch-based memory, with 6T cells where density matters and higher-transistor-count cells wherever stability, voltage margin, or read isolation matters more.
That machine has one access contract, one retention story, one power-off story, and no placement decision whose meaning depends on which kind of cell holds an address.
It also cannot supply the capacity this project's workload floor asks for, local models above all, inside a reticle-limited device.

R-15-247 answers with oxide-semiconductor 2T0C gain-cell decks for bulk storage.
The answer is defensible, but it gives back much of the conceptual compression the all-SRAM design bought: a second fixed latency, a retention deadline, scheduled refresh and discharge, bank-current and power-delivery constraints, a second power-off confidentiality story, and a placement rule with no promotion or migration escape hatch.
Worse, the second class is normative before R-15-247m's repaired-macro qualification exists and before the [bank-count exploration](bank-count-dse-contract.md) has the physical coefficients that prune or rank a candidate.
The higher-transistor-count all-SRAM machine remains the prettier architecture; the gain-cell machine is the capacity compromise, not an equally elegant refinement.
R-17-063b states the sharpest form of the compromise itself: the combined process stack is an axiom of the design and an offering of no foundry, which is a central feasibility assumption and not a supply-chain inconvenience.
What is unresolved is the combination rather than any one axis of it, capacity, bank bandwidth, activation current, repair and yield, predictable access, thermal compatibility with logic and manufacturable integration together, and a good result on one axis establishes none of the others.

**The executable golden model does not become the product merely because it runs.**
On the hardware side the golden artifact is a C emulator generated from Sail, while the product is synthesized RTL that must be shown to refine the Sail term.
On the software side the host oracle is Gallina lowered through CertiCoq to Wasm, while the device artifact is GC-free purecap machine code produced through CompCert-C, Fiat/Bedrock synthesis, Vélus, or the arena route.
The [implementation plan](implementation-checklist.md) does preserve one important identity: once produced, the same purecap device binary runs on the emulator, the RTL in co-simulation, and the FPGA.
But that binary is not the host oracle, and the Sail emulator is not the RTL.
What separates each executable artifact from the product differs in kind, and the distance is where much of the hardest work sits:

| Artifact | What executes | What still separates it from the product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Sail-generated emulator | A host-native simulator of the target machine | The simulator is not a fabricated processor; the RTL and its correspondence remain |
| The Gallina functional oracle | Extracted host-side Wasm, or the OCaml fallback | Its runtime and memory behaviour are not the admitted GC-free device implementation |
| The refined GC-free software | CompCert-C implementations of the load-bearing components, compiled purecap | The C must refine the Gallina specification, and the plan defers those proofs through bring-up |
| The generated imperative software | Synthesis, arena lowering and verified DSL compilation where each applies | Generation and compilation need their correctness arguments, and need not create a second maintained source |
| The device image | One purecap binary on the emulator, the RTL in co-simulation and the FPGA | Nothing: the identity is real, and it is what spares those environments separate ports |

The accurate charge is therefore **separate semantic and operational representations, with independently authored implementations on significant paths**, and not two unrelated codebases everywhere.
For kernel and storage code an executable specification may use convenient values and abstract state where the implementation needs explicit layout, bounded storage, ownership transfer and device effects, so the project maintains both descriptions and their refinement, and the implementation easiest to relate to the model need not meet the physical budget.
That is two ways to fail after the reference works, the real implementation too expensive or the practical one too hard to connect to the reference, and a change can cost a model edit, an implementation edit and a proof repair together: the model's development velocity is not the product's.
Compiling Gallina directly supplies neither GC-free execution, bounded allocation, capability enforcement nor the timing contract, and running the emulator as the product on commodity hardware would make the host processor and its runtime part of a different assurance argument.
The direction that reduces the split is less hand-maintained semantic duplication: an executable bounded implementation language as the authoritative operational source where one fits, the abstract model derived from it or the code compiled through a correctness-preserving path, manual refinement kept where abstraction earns its cost rather than as the default consequence of starting every component in unrestricted Gallina, and no further grand compiler project before one small component reaches the device through the route proposed.
Q2 is that experiment, on the wire-parser consumer M1.6 measured the route fits.

Substantial combinations already exist in the [strategy's closest-prior-art comparison](verification-language-strategy.md#closest-matches-to-the-complete-workflow): DeepSEA generates representation/refinement machinery for a restricted systems language, Sepref refines abstract algorithms into imperative implementations, and the verified Dafny subset and Bedrock2/Kami example connect different source workflows to stronger execution endpoints.
Q2b and Q19c therefore ask which of those mechanisms fits the actual client and selected anchors before new machinery is authored; the [capability-law results](verification-language-strategy.md#capability-aware-and-parametric-verification) make that question concrete for CHERI reasoning as well.
The remaining constraint is the conjunction of this platform's semantics, proof authority, runtime restrictions and artifact guarantees. The project still pays for the missing lowering, assembly, linking, image construction, RTL refinement and connections, with no saving credited merely for finding an upstream theorem.

**A unified authoring language needs a combined acceptance experiment.** Q19 qualifies proof interaction and reusable source-level reasoning; that does not establish the conjunction of everyday productivity, precise ownership, rich dependent verification and competitive optimized code. Q20a defines the comparison, Q20b tests one operational source with contracts and proofs through the qualified tooling, and Q20c joins the same optimized bytes to the available production evidence. A generated implementation is useful reuse; a second handwritten implementation needed to make the pilot run defeats this particular promise. The [language product contract](verification-language-strategy.md#language-product-contract) separates source assurance, the connection to exact bytes and production qualification so a successful earlier result cannot stand for the last.

**Feature composition needs evidence beyond individual examples.** The [proposed core](verification-language-strategy.md#a-compositional-core) separates stable type indices from mutable observations, closure repeatability from invocation counts, and erased facts from resource evidence; those distinctions must survive an actual combined client. Q20a selects the supported interactions and Q20b checks resource restoration on failure, capture effects, runtime witnesses and stable resolution alongside their rejected neighbors. Q19b checks that dependent definitions compute without adding forbidden equality axioms. A clear syntax and consistent proposed rules are useful design results; only the selected elaboration and semantic proofs can establish their sound composition, and neither the current notation nor a theorem from a different calculus supplies those proofs.

**The library pilot does not determine the eventual language.** `VerifiedComponents` can qualify useful construction and restoration rules without establishing a coherent dependent language or ergonomic flow-sensitive loans. Q21a owns that separate design and pricing decision: one local authority explanation with distinct memory, hardware, effect, security and budget interpretations, plus a bounded refinement tier, typed holes and checked source elaboration. Q20 measures the supported pilot rather than treating callback rewrites as the final UX; implementing the wider core and proving its composition require separately priced work. This preserves the independent contract and exact-byte evidence instead of mistaking a more unified syntax for stronger assurance.

**Clight's role needs a duty-preserving comparison, not an assumed replacement.** M1.6's non-capability verified exit and unproved C-printer exit make the missing connection an architectural input to Q21b. It compares capability-aware direct lowering and a bounded emission-lemma foundation against the full CHERI-C/Clight route, with Q2c supplying the existing foundation's costs. R-05-043 specifically mandates the decoder waypoint; R-18-014 mandates named TCB and Rust compilers, not arbitrary route diversity, and R-05-023a requires final validation in the existing program logic. A direct route must retain or explicitly amend those duties with substitute evidence for optimization, representation, linkage, leakage and robust preservation. A finite compiler grammar does not remove adversarial-context reasoning, and neither route qualifies when its artifact endpoint is missing.

**The state-of-the-art comparison changes what gets measured.** Q20b tests actionable diagnostics, stability of proof search after equivalent edits, review cost and deliberate contract weakening beyond the existing non-vacuity checks; it records where lexical borrowing forces awkward rewrites. M1.3 compares applicable Chamois optimization mechanisms, and Q2b/Q19c compare VeriFFI's contract-generation design before authoring equivalents, under the [focused research comparison](verification-language-strategy.md#research-that-changes-the-experiments). These are candidate mechanisms with concrete target, assumption and licence boundaries, not missing packages whose installation alone would complete the architecture. Q20 credits no productivity percentage or Rust-performance parity before the bounded comparisons produce it.

**The useful adoption is staged against real consumers.** Q19a/Q19b can compare diagnostics and selective lemma reuse on `CopyRingService`'s existing helpers and invariant proofs without waiting for a machine image. Q2c owns the missing foundation's scope and implementation pricing before Q19c factors any CHERI rules, and Q3c owns the fault-model specification Q3b needs. Q19c's parser destination and independently reserved copy-service staging client test reuse against project work, with unsupported guarantees explicitly rejected. Q20 counts setup, instance, review and maintenance costs once at equivalent evidence endpoints and keeps measured savings apart from projected reuse. Q21 adds design and comparison ownership, not a hidden implementation budget; general grading, a frontend, another target and a new compiler still need separately approved scope and evidence.

**Reuse is broad in the bibliography and narrow in the shipped artifact.**
The project does pull in many third-party projects, but predominantly as pinned readings, executable references, differential oracles, proof lineages, or designs to re-home in Coq.
Very little can be lifted unchanged into the final machine because the platform rejects the POSIX/Linux contract, standard privilege and memory machinery, ordinary executable loading, runtime allocation, firmware controllers, and any load-bearing proof checked outside the selected kernel.
The custom capability dialect, typed IDL profile, package format, kernel, memory system, and admission language then narrow the reusable set again.

Most individual refusals are defensible.
Their compound cost is still an ecosystem island: less hardware choice, fewer mature drivers and libraries, less upstream testing on the exact configuration, and more code for which this project is the first serious integrator.
Standards buy more than compatibility.
They buy independent implementers, adversarial interoperability, accumulated failure knowledge, test corpora, replacement suppliers, and a second interpretation of prose that can disagree with the first.
The design is right to reject a standard whose mechanism violates a security invariant, but it should count the loss of that epistemic diversity every time, not only the loss of applications that happen to speak the interface.
Which of the refusals are the price of the guarantees and which are policy stronger than the guarantees require is sorted [in the second movement](#refusals-that-are-policy-rather-than-isolation).

The sharper form of this objection is not about code, and putting it in code understates it.
What the design deletes is every **ratchet**: this profile is its own application profile rather than a member of a ratified one (R-15-001, R-15-012), which the [version matrix](cheri-version-matrix.md) names as the single largest structural difference from RVY and the reason a row there reads *integrate* where this profile reads *delete*; no MMU means no mainstream kernel boots even as a stopgap; no on-device loader leaves the world's binary tooling read-only useless against the image format; there is no JIT; Wasm enters exactly once, as a version-frozen curated guest subset (R-14-013b); and the dialect forks the CHERI mainstream far enough that the imported conformance methodology arrives without its encoding, R-15-005's single-encoding requirement refusing the one that carries it.
Each disposition is defensible where it is made, and their sum is a machine with no degrade-to-boring configuration, which is where the two premises stop being symmetric.
The hardware premise carries an evidence tier and a ladder to climb it (R-01-002a, R-01-002b, R-18-010), so a unit can ship while its isolation claim stands below the design's fixed point; the software premise admits no interval at all, R-18-002 foreclosing a capability-degraded interim by name and R-18-020 making the TAL producer and the on-device checker hard prerequisites with no trusted-toolchain fallback.
What that costs is critical-path exposure rather than compatibility, and R-01-002a books it.

### The memory question, read as a product question

**More transistors per cell buy stability and voltage margin, not capacity.**
A latch-based all-SRAM machine has the simpler retention story, and read-decoupled cells can raise read stability and lower the minimum operating voltage, which at one operating point repays their area in energy and at another does not; extra ports, longer lines, sensing choices and periphery all count, and [the performance estimates](performance-estimates.md) already qualify both the density and the access speed of the low-leakage option.
All-SRAM becomes the right answer by choosing a workload that fits it, never by assuming better SRAM circuits deliver dense-memory capacity: a bigger latch does not cure a shortage of bits, and stacking latches on this monolithic process wants complementary-device manufacturing that R-15-173a's grading does not treat as solved.
None of that proves all-SRAM impossible.
It means the current product floor and the current physical restrictions have no demonstrated all-SRAM realization.

**Flat addressing is not uniform physical cost.**
A large SRAM is not a register file scaled up: decoders, wires, bank arbitration, ECC and the interconnect remain, and one cell technology does not give every location the latency of a small nearby array.
Uniform latency is bought by padding to a worst case, which simplifies reasoning and can slow the common case; explicitly banked local memories under fixed access classes expose the geometry without a transparent cache, and are the less elegant and possibly the better engineering.
Three questions travel under one word here and none entails the others: one capability-addressed space, one cell technology, and one latency contract.
What the design rightly refuses is uncontrolled, hidden, history-dependent behaviour, and that is not the same thing as more than one physical cost.

**Two classes are not the offence.**
Explicit SRAM beside bulk storage is a sensible architecture and implies neither cache replacement, coherence, dynamic migration nor a second authority model, so the design is better than a tiered-memory reading of it suggests.
The problem is the combination: an unqualified bulk technology, rigid placement, no absolute performance evidence, and an insistence on the physical restrictions even where they might defeat the product.
Two proven memory classes would be a better product foundation than one hypothetically perfect class.
The compromise also spends security: R-17-058f leaves the power-off plaintext window without a confidentiality construction and R-17-058e books the refresh's power and near-field readout, finding 8 carries both, and erasing capability tags stops the machine using stale authority without erasing the plaintext a physical observer recovers.
R-15-247s's refusal of a trust gradient is defensible for the architectural authority checks, which are shared, and is not a statement of equal physical confidentiality; the residuals say otherwise.

**Capacity has more than one ceiling, and the format is the one still cheap to move.**
R-15-002a's address space is an encoding limit; R-15-002c's product limit covers both memory classes and the non-memory apertures; fabricated usable bits, the bits an island can reach, and the bandwidth its workload is granted are three limits again.
Widening the address field supplies no cells, no bandwidth and no energy, and restricting one fabrication proposal today is no physical proof that a permanent width stays sufficient, the register itself calling model-size sufficiency a bet.
The wider format costs pointer density and changes representation and tooling, but this is an unreleased design: R-15-007d freezes the width at the profile and stored authority is what makes the freeze permanent, so the growth question is judged before a deployed population turns it into a migration, which is Q6's decision and the register act it raises rather than takes.

**The escape routes are alternatives to evaluate, not designs that keep every current axiom.**

| Option | What it preserves or improves | What it costs | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A smaller workload on qualified all-SRAM | Simple retention, no bulk refresh, fewer physical assumptions | Smaller local models and less application breadth | The best near-term demonstrator, with the scope reduced openly |
| An explicit bulk store for public immutable weights | Authority-bearing working state stays local, and external data is checked against a signed root | Transfer bandwidth, verification buffers, stalls and visible access patterns | The strongest candidate for capacity, at a limited confidentiality concession |
| Encrypted, authenticated mutable external bulk | Capacity without giving external memory authority over plaintext or capability validity | Freshness metadata, crypto throughput, access-pattern exposure and more proof work | A serious alternative and not a free substitute |
| Separate SRAM dies behind a protected interface | Latch-based storage kept, fabrication choices separated | Density and leakage remain, and link and packaging obligations return | A manufacturability option, not a capacity or power cure |
| Public weights in read-mostly nonvolatile storage | Public model weights no longer treated as secret volatile state | Read bandwidth, endurance where writes occur, interface behaviour and image integrity | Worth testing as a weight store, not assumed suitable as general RAM |
| Quantization, smaller models, shorter contexts | Less traffic and storage under the present architecture | Quality and function, with application state still needing capacity | The low-mechanism lever, measured for actual usefulness |

Public weights are consumed only after authentication against a trusted model identity, no executable authority or hardware tag is imported from an external store, keys, trusted metadata and the small verification buffers stay on the die, and transfers are bounded and explicit rather than demand-paged.
The limits are real: with sparse or expert-routed inference the addresses can reveal prompt-dependent behaviour even when the fetched weights are public, a fixed transfer schedule or a larger fetched set narrows that leak at a bandwidth cost, confidential KV state and user-derived media inherit nothing from the public-weight argument, and an untrusted store can always deny service, so a hard deadline wants buffering, redundancy or a separately established delivery bound.
For mutable confidential bulk, encryption alone is insufficient, integrity, freshness, address binding and atomic data-and-metadata updates all mattering, and a bounded explicit store that avoids rebuilding an MMU and a cache hierarchy still adds a substantial verified subsystem, which may nonetheless be a smaller total dependency than a new memory process.
R-17-059a already scopes the attacker who reaches the plaintext after a first-class key is gone, which is a reason to compare an authenticated-encryption design concretely rather than to assume the unencrypted memory path R-15-199 states dominates every alternative.
The experiment to run first is public immutable bulk and not general external RAM: it tests the flagship capacity demand with the least authority and confidentiality baggage, it conflicts with the present one-die topology (R-15-162) and so would change the architecture, and it abandons neither the capability thesis nor the proof-carrying-software thesis.
Q6 is where the comparison is taken and Q7 where the remanence decision is.

### Unknown unknowns and underweighted consequences

**A model that fits in memory may still be unusable for inference.**
The dense class is justified most visibly by local language models, but capacity is not their binding performance quantity here.
R-15-247p and R-12-085 make the second-class bank grant set the admitted token rate, and the performance account states that inference is bandwidth-bound while the matrix array's compute multiple acts on a term that is already slack.
At low batch size, generation repeatedly streams much of the resident weight set; fitting the weights proves neither useful tokens per second nor acceptable joules per token.
Until the qualified macro supplies sustained bandwidth, bank activation current, bitline energy, retention work, and thermal behavior together, the flagship justification for the second class remains a capacity result in search of a product result.
The bound is first-order and it is enough: for dense low-batch decoding that reads the participating weights each token, tokens per second are at most the sustained bandwidth granted to inference over the weight bytes read per token, and KV traffic, dequantization and scheduling make the achieved rate worse; batching, reuse, sparsity and different algorithms change the denominator and are assessed on their own behaviour.
More matrix operations per second do not move that ceiling, more banks buy bandwidth at periphery, current and capacity efficiency, and quantization helps directly at an accuracy and decoding cost that does not disappear.
What is wanted is a useful model at an acceptable response rate inside a sustained power budget, not a capacity figure or an operations-per-second figure, and Q4 measures the demand side before any supply is added.

**Private inference and efficient sparse inference pull in opposite directions, and the design resolves it by scope rather than by mechanism.**
R-15-171 admits a mixture-of-experts model only with every expert resident and fixed top-k, making the amount of work per token constant while routing still selects expert addresses.
R-05-070 rejects any secret-labeled value that reaches a memory address, so the two coexist only because R-12-085 keeps session content outside the secret label: a prompt carries its client's confidentiality label and never the constant-time one, the server is not a secret-touching compartment, and a client holding secret-labeled material has no typed channel to it.
The same scoping admits variable context lengths, token-dependent sampling, early exits, sparse attention, and paged KV-cache techniques on the same footing and no other.
What it costs is stated in two places: the routing's data dependence is confined to the server's partition by the schedule and rides the near-field residual R-17-058e books, and a secrecy class that wanted those techniques constant-time gets no local inference at all.
The workload used to justify dense memory is thus also the workload whose modern efficiency tricks the constant-time policy could never admit, which is why they are kept outside it.

**Static perfection spends utilization twice.**
The offline memory plan does excellent work: it colors non-overlapping live ranges, charges a compartment on the peak of its aggregate live set rather than the sum of separate peaks, and makes exhaustion local and explicit.
It cannot pool across an island boundary, and R-15-172 still sizes an island to its peak rather than the machine to the sum of averages.
The [occam/transputer precedent](inspirations.md#occam-perihelion-helios-the-transputer-and-xmos-xcore-static-channels-in-silicon-and-the-boundary-every-rendezvous-machine-stops-at) sharpens the name: the planner really does delete classic allocator external fragmentation, so this is not the transputer's dynamic-heap "Swiss cheese" recurring under another spelling (R-08-012).
What remains is **reservation fragmentation**: an empty pre-colored slot or pool member is physically idle yet unusable by a compartment the static plan did not assign it to, and a bank bound to another island cannot be borrowed at all (R-08-012c, R-08-047).
The machine's aggregate idle capacity is therefore a sum of residues that need not be spendable by the workload peaking now.
A conventional virtual-memory system combines address indirection with machine-wide physical-page allocation, allowing a page no longer backing one address space to back another; the advantage is statistical multiplexing across owners, not a better packing algorithm.
This machine deliberately forgoes that general free-page pool (R-08-045).
The cyclic executive makes the same trade in time: R-07-036 burns an idle slot across confidentiality labels rather than donating it.
Population rungs, same-label rotation, suspension, and declared shedding soften the result, but no mechanism lends an idle bank, pool member, or slot across the boundary that made it safe.
The binding failure is refusal at composition rather than pressure at run time, R-15-171 admitting no swap and no overcommit and R-08-047 refusing a borrow, so a roster can fail to compose because no packing across owners, bank grants, and latency classes exists rather than because total demand exceeds total silicon, and the one instrument that prices a packing, [the placement search](placement-search.md), ranks span used, unused reservation and padding inside one island over a witness plan and carries no bank or latency-class term, so a packing across owners is priced by nothing still.
And the sizing judgment recurs one level down rather than retiring: each compartment is now its own memory planner declaring its own static pools, so what the doctrine abolishes at the machine it relocates onto every author.
The product therefore pays for peak isolation in both bytes and cycles, so effective capacity and throughput can fall together on bursty interactive workloads.
Read as a product rather than as an allocator, that is the objection in its sharpest form: a correct refusal can still be a product failure.
Excellent offline packing does not abolish uncertainty about what a person does next, proving that a declared population fits does not prove its limit comfortable, and variable demand must fit a predetermined envelope that resources outside it cannot rescue.
For a fixed communications terminal that is excellent; for an exploratory personal computer it is a loss of function and not a slower implementation of the same function, which is why [the README](../README.md)'s memory heading claims *no allocator waste* and not *no wasted memory*: a class of allocator waste is removed, and fabricated memory is not thereby usefully occupied.

**Usable is a product property the floor does not measure.**
For ordinary expectations of a phone or laptop the risk is serious, and for a curated appliance it is not inevitable: a slower machine can interact well under sensible foreground budgets, and a fast arithmetic unit cannot make up for missing applications, incompatible services, capacity refusals or a long update round trip.
R-18-004a's floor is better than a feature list, its members having to coexist on one configuration, and a minimum demonstration is not a representative personal workload: comfort wants headroom and the awkward transitions, a call during camera work, a large incoming object while inference holds its context, lost connectivity, a full pool, and an update with durable work pending.
Browser deferral excludes a large share of ordinary phone workflows, which may be the right scope reduction and makes the first product a specialized communications device rather than a general smartphone replacement. The communications scope is narrower than that phrase carries: the floor's telephony member is a call, and no artifact in the corpus carries a messaging capability of any kind, so a release satisfying every clause of the floor ships a phone that cannot answer a text. Q16 is where that is either carried as a member or booked the way R-17-051 books the generation floor's coverage cost, stated rather than left to read as an unimplemented gap.
Fail-stop is not availability: correctly refusing corrupt memory, a bad clock state or an unsafe transition can still interrupt a call, and the emergency-call isolation the specification already carries manufactures neither coverage, spare physical resources nor availability through faults.
Q10 is where useful behaviour, correspondence, resource accounting and physical feasibility have to meet on one configuration, and where the scope is revised if the mobile floor does not close.

**Proof simplicity can become a proxy for physical truth.**
R-15-108 correctly makes proof simplicity an objective of design-space exploration, because a smaller state machine is cheaper to refine and easier to audit.
The Goodhart risk is that a compact formal model can be obtained by pushing difficult behavior below the model boundary: a memory access becomes one fixed constant, while process variation, retention, droop, yield, and temperature become qualification assumptions.
The second memory class is the clearest instance, adopted partly because carrying one architecture with two constants is formally smaller than carrying a branch over whether the class exists.
A design that is easy to state is not thereby easy to fabricate, and a constant in Sail can be the name of an unsolved analog system rather than a simplification of one.

**A small implementation trust base can still have a large conceptual trust base.**
One Coq kernel and one Sail anchor minimize the code that may grant a theorem, which is a real strength.
They also concentrate common-mode error: an omitted observation, a wrong capability rule, a weak non-interference statement, or a mistaken physical abstraction can be shared by every proof above it.
The review gate and the independent QEMU, RTL, parser, and protocol oracles are the right responses, but those oracles are deliberately evidence rather than authority.
They can reveal disagreement and cannot make agreement evidence that the shared abstraction is the right one.
The project's smallest trusted *implementation* therefore depends on a much larger trusted act of judgment: choosing the right semantics and leakage model in the first place.
The bespoke pieces compound it: a custom capability encoding, a custom code encoding, a new admission language, a modified compiler path, a new kernel and a bespoke memory system are not independent simplifications, each able to shrink a local state machine while growing the correspondence work the project owns, and one proof kernel makes the trusted implementation of inference small without making the specifications or the physical assumptions small.

**Energy is a weaker gate than correctness, despite being existential for the reference product.**
The architecture moves codec, graphics, radio, sensor, and inference work onto general-purpose verified cores to delete firmware trust bases.
It also burns schedule slack, keeps large memory populations at declared retention states, performs background refresh, ECC scrub, and revocation reclamation, and gives up the energy efficiency of mature fixed-function media blocks.
Some of those costs are individually small and several are offset by static power states, but they compose inside a mobile or laptop thermal envelope.
The critique below already observes that no scheduled artifact can falsify the design in joules.
A system can close every logical theorem and still fail as a portable computer because the battery, skin temperature, acoustic cooling budget, or sustained clock cannot carry the proof-friendly realization.

**The memory bet is a yield and economics bet as well as a device-physics bet.**
A repaired test macro can establish cell behavior and still say little about the yield of a reticle-scale logic die carrying many memory decks, native capability tags, stronger tag ECC, repair structures, power delivery, and tight cross-tier alignment.
Repair must preserve the fixed-latency contract and the island binding rather than merely return the nominal bit count.
If usable yield or test time is poor, the architecture can be functionally correct and commercially impossible, with no external module or commodity memory vendor available to absorb that risk.

**Evolution remains proof-gated even where initial construction is modular.**
A component whose implementation changes under a stable contract can often be re-certified locally, and the design has a fast containment path while the full fix is proved.
A change to a semantic anchor, capability encoding, protocol grammar, memory map, leakage model, or shared interface reopens the seams above it; support for a new radio generation or memory technology may also require new silicon.
This is the security benefit of freezing the machine and the operational cost of doing so.
The risk is not merely slow feature delivery: it is a long degraded interval after the outside world changes in a way the frozen assumptions did not anticipate.

**The install path's expense is not where the design's own account of it looks.**
The checker that could be slow is the one an install does not run: R-06-008 and R-13-027 scope the on-device CIC kernel to the artifact-local source-correspondence theorem and hold Tier-0 refinement, non-interference, and the composed proofs at release time, while the type check carries no budget at all, its termination and its linear work bound being properties of the pinned language rather than of this platform (R-05-129, R-05-135a, R-06-015a).
The source closure that reads as the expensive part is the cheap one: R-13-010c keeps closures, derivations, and proof artifacts out of execution SRAM entirely, and R-13-008 makes a transfer a content-addressed set difference, so a shared dependency's source moves once per device.
What an install actually costs is a boot, a round trip to an off-device composer, and a re-lowering of the whole package closure outside the base image (R-13-001a, R-13-001c, R-13-028).
R-17-035a names the whole-roster memory plan, the schedulability proof, and the cross-roster merge among what that boundary buys and prices them as immediacy, which is a constant, while what R-13-028 describes is not: composition re-runs the whole-image passes, the slot plan, and the schedule over the closure, so every object in it is re-emitted with its derivation and its correspondence theorem whatever the change was.
The quantity a user meets is therefore a composer's turnaround against the size of the roster rather than the complexity of any check, and the corpus carries neither figure.
Nor is proof-checking time the whole of the visible cost: R-13-001a makes a package change a generation activated at boot and R-13-001c keeps the composer off the device, so a mundane software change is a distributed build-and-admit operation whose cost to the user includes network access, the composition turnaround, the restart and the restoration of declared durable state; and R-02-008's exclusion of self-hosting makes a development-oriented user dependent on another machine however good the editor and the coding-agent interface, so the external build infrastructure's availability is part of owning the device.
Q9 measures that turnaround and weighs the bounded local workflows that would narrow it.

**The largest unknown unknown is correlation.**
The bespoke memory, capability dialect, compiler path, static kernel, scheduling model, package format, and server ecosystem are often justified independently.
They are not independent bets.
Most depend on the same premises: workloads can be bounded ahead of time, fixed latency is affordable, offline composition can replace adaptation, and enough optimized code can be recovered without dynamic machinery.
If one premise fails at product scale, several layers can fail together: the memory plan no longer fits, the schedule no longer admits, the compiler cannot hide the latency, and compatibility offers no substitute implementation.
The project is strongest when it deletes one mechanism after another from a proven base; it is weakest where several novel mechanisms lean on the same unmeasured physical or workload assumption.

### Overall assessment

The architecture is unusually coherent, and many of its bespoke choices are defensible when judged one at a time against the stated goal of minimizing trust.
The criticism is that local defensibility does not establish global practicality.
The strongest idea here is a capability-confined, proof-admitted machine whose authority and resource use are explicit; the weakest is that the cleanest local restriction must remain the best global architecture after enough other restrictions have accumulated around it.
Higher-transistor-count all-SRAM is the cleaner memory design if the useful workload fits and is not a demonstrated escape from the capacity constraint; two explicit memory classes are not intrinsically a mistake; making an unqualified one mandatory while closing off mature physical alternatives is the much larger gamble.
The golden-model concern belongs beside that gamble: the convenient thing that executes is not automatically the thing that ships, the distance between them holds much of the hardest work, and it is minimized before the components that must cross it multiply.
The programme most needs early falsifiers rather than further obligations: a qualified dense-memory macro exercised at the bandwidth and energy point local inference needs, a production-path vertical slice connecting the executable specifications to real device binaries and RTL, and independent implementations that attack the semantic anchors rather than merely agree with them.
Until those exist, the design's conceptual beauty is real, but it is the beauty of a conditional construction whose hardest conditions are physical, economic, and ecological rather than logical.
The consistent course is a smaller, measurable appliance and a real implementation chain, no promise yet of a general personal computer, and no acceptance of architectural purity as sufficient justification for a device that cannot comfortably perform the work used to justify building it.

## The dominant fact, which is not a finding

**Every hedge is spent, no primary is verified, and the interval between is claimed at the tier it holds.** This is stated where it belongs, so it is not a defect and not an entry. It sits ahead of the findings because it is the single most important true thing about the project's assurance position, and because several of the findings below are read against it.

The *verify rather than hedge* rule is applied consistently and its logic is sound at the fixed point: a mechanism whose only job is to catch the failure of a formally verified primary buys little against the cost of a second modeled mechanism. On that rule the design deletes the MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, the initialization-tag plane, MTE, shadow stacks, the Harvard split, and every disjoint backstop on the machine (R-17-037, R-17-045). §17 states the hedge that remains: "the **formal verification of CHERI itself**," resting on "the **RTL ⊑ Sail** arrow … and its Coq-native restatement for the CHERI-RISC-V profile as the residual." That arrow is the layer the same document calls **the least-built layer of the stack** (R-17-039, R-18-010). The CHERI-TAL soundness metatheorem that replaces the initialization-tag plane is theorem target `CJ-TAL-SOUND`, premised on the ISA profile and **not started**. The certifying compiler, the on-device checker, the Cerise universal contract, the secure-compilation theorem: none exists.

So the rule is sound at the fixed point and its premise is false everywhere before it. On the entire trajectory to that fixed point (a multi-year interval on this roster, and the only state the project has ever occupied) the primaries are *unverified*, a hedge against an unverified primary is ordinary defense in depth, and any device that executes in that interval runs with the elegance and without the argument. A single CHERI logic error in monotonicity, revocation, or the tag plane fails every isolation boundary on the machine at once, with nothing beneath it and, until `CJ-TAL-SOUND` and the Coq refinement close, no proof above it.

**The interim claim is the knob that turns, and it is the only one that can.** Demanding retained backstops asks for the wrong thing: MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, MTE, and the Harvard split are **silicon**, so "retain until the theorem closes" means fabricating them, modelling them in Sail, and paying the second-modeled-mechanism cost the rule exists to price, and R-18-002 forecloses a capability-degraded interim by name. What can move is what the interval claims for itself, and §1 carries that on G2 (R-01-002a, R-01-002b): the software premise admits no interval, since nothing runs uncertified and a missing certificate costs delivery rather than admission; the hardware premise stands at **evidence tier** (rvfi, Sail-generated SystemVerilog under commercial FEV, the imported-core netlist audit) rather than **theorem tier** (the Kami/Kôika Coq refinement); every FPGA and early-silicon unit asserts G2 at that tier and no higher; the tier rises per core when that core's unbounded refinement is admitted, never at an intermediate rung; and an attestation or datasheet asserting G2 without its tier is a review-gate finding. §3 carries the inheritance (R-03-006). The retirement condition is R-18-010's rungs, on R-05-022's two-list discipline.

**What the tier statement buys is legibility, not safety, and the exposure is untouched.** The interval is multi-year and the tier is the weaker one for all of it; what a reader of §1 and §3 alone learns is that this is so. Two consequences run into the findings below. Whether a future datasheet honours R-01-002b is a semantic question `check.py` cannot reach, which is finding 2. And the tier claim is load-bearing for exactly as long as the interval lasts, which makes it the easiest thing in this catalogue to let go stale.

## What is not built

**1. Nothing load-bearing is built, and the specification is precise enough to say how little.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the instrument that says so, and its status column reads: of twenty-six crown-jewel specifications, **two are authored** (the frozen ISA profile and the apex theorem's machine-checked statement), five are partial, **nineteen are not authored**. The ten theorem targets each depend on a row above; nineteen of those premises do not exist, so most of the proof program cannot begin rather than merely being unfinished.

The repository's own ledger sharpens this, and what it sharpens is *which kind* of artifact exists rather than how much of it there is. Some seven weeks, seven-hundred-odd commits, and three-and-a-half megabytes of design documents stand against a tree whose non-Markdown paths are, by size: the curated Sail model, vendored from its upstream pin with a project-authored layer over it (the dialect's custom encodings, the platform instructions, the frozen Keccak unit, the core-class and memory-class tables, the timing annotations); the consistency checker, and the assembler that stands in for a toolchain nobody has re-homed yet; a differential corpus of two dozen hand-written purecap programs that assemble to ELF and run to a HTIF verdict on the emulator generated from that model; the Gallina statement artifacts under [proofs/](../proofs/), which are the apex theorem's machine-checked statement with its assumption gate, the seam witnesses beside it, and a dozen-odd further files in the same idiom, each stating one surface the plan's Gallina lanes schedule, which the section below reads; and a score of pinned upstream submodules, which are start-froms rather than authored code. **Every one of those is a specification, an instrument, or evidence about one, and not one is the system.** No kernel, no firmware, no init, no filesystem, no crypto core, no userland, and no compiler backend: what runs on this machine today is a test harness proving that the machine's *description* is self-consistent. The loose figures are deliberate: every exact count in this catalogue sits under a `check.py` claim, and no claim holds these.

The project's only demonstrated velocity is in producing obligations. That is a defensible way to spend an early phase, and §18 and R-01-003 are candid that the as-existing assurance is low. The obligation count grows monotonically and the discharge count is two: every hardening that makes the design safer adds a crown jewel, and nothing retires one except authoring it. The inventory carries that direction as a **burn-down** with the implementation plan's milestones as its retirement mechanism, so what is open is whether the discharge count moves.
The gates that hold these documents are valuable and are also the easiest visible measure of progress while implementation feasibility stays untested, which is what makes the production-path vertical slice the next milestone that matters and Q10 its owner.

### What a statement artifact is, and is not

**The Gallina files are specifications stated in a prover, and a green proof gate over them is a claim about their form and not about the machine.** [proofs/](../proofs/) holds over a dozen `.v` files, tens of thousands of lines, landed item by item under the implementation plan: the cyclic executive and its partition context, the RoT discharge sequence, the M-mode and RoT firmware obligations, the composition-time admission path, endpoint IPC, the journal and index with the keyspace domains above them, the supervision tree, the memory plan, the generated ring contract, the handler graph, the Keccak reference, and the apex statement with its seam witnesses. Each transcribes what a register section obliges into definitions and theorems over a record whose fields are the quantities a composition fixes, and most say in their headers, in one shared sentence, what the gate's green line means: compiled, axiom-free, non-vacuous and enumerated, and it does not mean verified. [run.py proofs](../tools/vos/cli/proofs.py) decides exactly that line, every shipped constant's assumption set against the declared set R-05-164 reads from the register, which is empty until an admission axiom, the bootstrap root or an *Ax* ledger entry is authored.

What independent readings of the tree establish, stated loosely because none of these counts is this document's to own:

- **Every file is axiom-free and closes on itself.** Nothing is admitted, no top-level parameter stands, and a `Require` names a sibling in the same directory or nothing at all, so no theorem reaches the Sail model, an upstream, or a library beyond the prelude. That is the R-05-163 gate holding, and it is also the boundary of what the tree can say: a theorem over a record the file defines constrains that record, and the model in [model/](../model/) is not a term any of these files can mention.
- **The witnesses inhabit the statements and do not describe the machine.** Where a file instantiates a machine's carrier types to show its statement is inhabited, it instantiates them at unit, bool or nat. That is what non-vacuous means at this gate: a witness of that shape says the statement is satisfiable, and says nothing about whether the product satisfies it.
- **A refutation rules out a phrasing, not a system.** The constructions the files refute, a detector that searches the graph, a partition switch that drops a tag, a loader discovered at runtime, a rho recurrence started one step early, are alternative readings of a register clause, and refusing each one shows the clause as stated is not dead. None is an alternative design, and no refutation is evidence that the stated clause is the right one; that question is R-17-016's, and no proof reaches it.
- **One file constrains an object outside the tree.** The Keccak reference is checked against the standard's published known answers and held against the Sail unit's by K-91, so its theorems can be false of something that exists. Every other file's theorems are true or false of definitions the same file makes.
- **The apex statement is stated and nothing discharges it.** Its Vocabulary record is the coverage checklist, and every row of the [field-binding map](field-bindings.md) reads `none yet`: no proof workstream instantiates any field, so every seam is named and not one is closed.
- **A rule binds a constant to an entry now, and what it binds is a claim rather than a discharge.** K-42 holds the apex record's field list against its view, K-89 holds the ring artifact against its generator, K-91 holds the Keccak answers against the Sail unit's, K-103 holds every requirement id a file cites to a live entry, and K-105 holds every discharge annotation to a live entry and to a statement rather than a term, which K-106 joins with the register into a generated ledger. The ledger's column is named `claims` and not `discharges`, and the distinction is the whole of what the instrument decides: the join establishes that a constant asserts an entry, and nothing in it establishes that the constant's statement is that entry's obligation. So the reading a person makes has moved from a completion note to the line above the theorem, where it is legible and counted, and it is still a reading. That is R-17-016's residual meeting the newest instrument in the repository rather than being answered by it.

So the tree moves the count of what exists on the specification side, and moves it in the currency of an authored statement rather than an authored proof: where a crown-jewel row is carried in part by one of these files, the [inventory](crown-jewels.md) says so and holds the row at `partial` until the property the row names is proved of the object it names. What the tree does not move is the dominant fact. No theorem in it is about the machine, no file in it is a program the machine runs, and each file's fidelity to the register it transcribes is held by a reader and by nothing the gate audits, which is finding 2's residue meeting the newest artifacts in the repository.

## The review gate audits its own reflection

**2. The gate is referential and the risk is semantic.** §5's release gate exists because "a proof against a wrong spec verifies perfectly," and R-18-034 states plainly that the gate "audits the register rather than this prose," so an unverified extraction stands between the reviewer and the design of record and is declared the authoritative object. That an automated checker cannot decide whether a statement is the *right* statement is a property of checkers, not a repairable defect, and charging it as a gap would be a category error. What is chargeable is the residue, in three pieces.

**The instrument is strong and the wrong shape.** `check.py` checks **referential integrity**: cited bookmarks resolve, requirements carry traces, no view drops a requirement in its bearing subsections, no asserted count disagrees with its artifact, no document carries encoding damage. Its reach includes this document: every count asserted here is anchored in the checker's registry and recomputed, so an edition of this critique cannot quietly disagree with the artifacts about a number. A requirement can nonetheless resolve perfectly, trace perfectly, appear in every view, count correctly, and state the wrong thing.

**Face one: part of the review budget is spent on the document rather than the machine, and the class wants a tag.** A class of acceptance criteria is discharged by inspecting the prose rather than the artifact: *"the axiom is stated in §6 and §17, not implied"* (R-06-014); *"the consent TCB is named in §6 rather than hidden in userland"* (R-06-018); *"the cost is booked rather than omitted"* (R-12-020). How large the class is depends on where the line is drawn, so the honest figure is a band with its rule attached: counting every criterion whose test uses *stated*, *booked*, *named*, *recorded*, or *declared* while citing neither `check.py` nor a companion artifact yields **157 of 1405 acceptance criteria**; requiring the criterion to cite a `§` as the thing a reviewer opens narrows it to **31**. Both are snapshots and lower bounds, since amendments keep adding entries of exactly this class. These entries are **not** defects under R-05-153: that rule catches a claim that cannot be restated as an atomic, testable requirement, and "the consent TCB is named in §6" is atomic and testable by inspection, which is a test. The defect is one layer over, in R-05-152 making the register the audited object: a criterion that is a fact about the commentary spends the gate's scarcest resource, a reviewer's attention on the one pass that decides whether the specification says the right thing, on editorial hygiene. The repair is a **tag rather than a defect entry**: mark the spec-hygiene entries as such, so the review can price them instead of discovering them one at a time.

**The amendment rate is the same finding from the other side.** The git log is the measure: repairs routinely land within a day of the finding that names them. That velocity is the strongest evidence in the repository that the specification workstream is real, and it is the problem: a corpus amended faster than anyone re-reads it is a corpus whose review is always of a previous edition.

**Face two: the extraction-defect list opens on the wrong axis, and its own stated class is empty.** The list is empty, and the register is right that "an empty list is not the same as a swept one." But look at what the list is instrumented to catch. Its findings arrive from building a derived view and machine-checking it, which reports an enumeration two documents disagree about, never a passage that would not decompose, because nothing re-reads the prose against that question. **R-05-153's actual class, a normative claim that would not decompose, has zero entries**: not one of the 1405 requirements has yet been booked as standing for a passage that resisted restatement, and face one's entries are not the missing ones, because they decomposed fine, into requirements about the document. So two readings stand. Either the prose was atomic throughout, implausible for a document whose §17 entries routinely run five clauses deep with three parenthetical qualifications, or the extraction **accommodated** the prose where it should have confronted it. The register does not claim otherwise: its standing instruction is that sweep 2's question "has not been asked exhaustively, so further instances should be assumed present rather than absent." The channel that reports what the *prose* refused to decompose is not running, and an agenda drawn from enumerations alone means the meeting R-18-035 calls the review gate's agenda has barely started.

**Face three: the derived views can be wrong in the half nothing checks, and there are more of them every month.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is declared "Normative as a **view**." The *quantitative* half of that class is closed the only way such things close: `check.py` parses the status column into its three classes, recomputes the ratio, and fails where a document asserts a figure the artifact does not bear. The *qualitative* half, which is most of it, is untouched: whether a row's `partial` is doing work `not authored` should do, whether a cited requirement is the *right* ground, whether a discharge mode in [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md) reports what its cited requirements actually carry, invisible by construction wherever the claim names no place. Every artifact that arrives widens that surface, machine-checked for **presence** and none for **truth**, and a view is normative whether or not it has been read. The only lever this finding has is substitution: *whether R-17-030a's seams are the right ones* is not answerable, but it is **replaced** by a mechanical question it implies, whether every requirement using the vocabulary of refusal is conferred or dispositioned (finding 3). The matrix is where that lever's reach is easiest to read and hardest to extend: one of its discharge modes, `residual`, names §17 rather than a construction, so a cell reporting its remainder as booked there is decidable against the section it names, while the rest of the vocabulary names constructions and stays a reading. That substitution is available wherever a qualitative claim has a lexical shadow it can be held against, and most of the list above has none.

**3. A membership claim is decidable against its own members and undecidable against the world.** R-17-016 closes the crown-jewel list by conferral: membership is asserted by each requirement that has it, [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the one place the conferrals are collected, and `check.py` fails on a conferral with no row or a row with no requirement. R-17-030r and R-10-013a carry the identical rule: a requirement specifying a mechanism whose failure action is to stop records the refusal against itself on a `· Fail-closed:` property line, a requirement placing state under the monotonic counter records it on a `· RoT-fresh:` line, the R-17-030 seams and R-10-013 collect them, and the checker fails on a conferral no collection carries and on a seam standing under no conferral. Thirty-six conferrals against nineteen seams, four against one enumeration, both directions decidable. What no checker can grep for is not membership; it is the *predicate*, and conferral does not need to.

**Conferral closes completeness for none of the three sets, and the registers say so.** A specification that ought to confer crown-jewel status and does not is exactly as invisible as a refusal that ought to confer and does not; what conferral closes is the *collection's disagreement with the requirements*, which is the smaller property. Each register claims the decidable half in its own text and declines the other, which is the only honest form of the claim.

**The machinery is not the result, and a completeness convention fails exactly where it is most expensive.** Against the completeness residue the checker carries the only instrument available to it: the vocabulary of refusal is over-approximated across every requirement body, and each candidate it catches must confer, be collected, or be dispositioned by name with a reason. The scan earns its keep: the seam register carries four members the enumeration alone missed, and one of them is the sharpest member in it. **Every fault detector on this machine answers detection by stopping** (uncorrectable ECC, tag integrity, the control-flow signature on the three protected sequences, the sentinel's lockstep comparator, residual synchronizer failure), each of them right to stop, and the adversary who provokes them is the radiated-EMI and electromagnetic-fault-injection adversary §3 admits by name and §15 shields against. A probe held near the enclosure stops a device that is behaving correctly, needs no software access at all, and gives R-17-030l's life-safety composition a fourth cause: a user who cannot place an emergency call because a detector caught an injected fault. The other three members are the entropy health test, whose sole failure action is fail-stop and whose meeting is every operation needing fresh randomness; the display path, whose visible blank is a caught fail-stop on the surface the consent prompt is drawn on, reaching denial of the prompt from a direction the compositor seam does not cover; and budget admission refusal, which also costs R-17-030e its claim to be the only member whose denial cannot reach a running unit.

**What stands is the residue.** The vocabulary agenda is lexical, proves no totality, and is defeated by an author who rewords rather than decides; the dispositions are judgments recorded in a tool; neither is a quantifier over the semantic predicate, because there is none to build. So the only thing worth building is the agenda that forces a person to decide, on every run, for every candidate the vocabulary can reach. Sixty candidates stand dispositioned across the fail-closed, RoT-fresh and formal-discharge agendas. The thirteen on the RoT-fresh side all resolved the same way, that they enforce the floor or provide the counter and place no state under it, which is the only evidence in this repository that an enumeration closed by convention was right.

**The absence register is decidable about what exists and silent about what a permitted structure reaches.** [absence-contract.md](absence-contract.md) is a fourth collection checked in both directions, and the only one whose members are absences: each row is a structure whose absence is claimed, its evidence column is what an auditor must fail to find, R-15-100 fixes the enumeration it draws from, and R-15-100a makes its agreement with the register mechanical. None of that machinery reaches a structure the design keeps, because keeping one claims no absence and so writes no row, and the document adds no obligation of its own in any case. R-15-104 draws the one boundary that decides a keep, a fetch-path state element whose write data depends on a prior execution failing and one whose contents are a function of the fetched stream passing, and the same obligation declines size and run-ahead depth by name. That is the right test for the property it serves: R-15-101 states the hyperproperty as timing and memory traffic being a function of the instruction stream and architectural state alone, and concedes in its own criterion that what the contract discharges is a sufficient structural condition rather than the property. What it is not is a bound. The static-path fetch buffer is admitted and its requests are confined to the statically determined path (R-15-022), the structure retained as one whose contents are a function of the program text rather than of access history (R-15-165) and classified into the class the fence's pipeline drain empties (R-15-217). No artifact bounds the range or the depth of that chain: the successor sequence may run past the end of the region the executing PCC authorizes, no entry requires a run-ahead request to be checked against those bounds before issue, and R-15-108's frozen parameters carry no fetch-buffer depth. The cost side is unowned in the same shape. The buffer sits in R-15-217's stream-determined class, which the fence's pipeline drain empties, while R-15-218's padded constant is stated over the store buffer and R-15-015b keeps device stores out of it, so an outstanding fetch toward a slow endpoint is a term neither entry prices, and R-15-015c's reach is a reach by capability where a fetch reaches by address. What stands between the two is the endpoint rather than the chain: R-15-002d requires every device region to be non-executable and the model's platform-attribute check refuses an instruction fetch into a region that is not, so a stray fetch faults where it lands. That closes what a run-ahead request may reach and not how far the chain reaching for it may run, which is the residue Q11 takes.

## The opposing artifact, and the instruments it waits on

**4. The floor exists and most of its instruments do not.** The register states what the first release does for a user: eight named workload members carried together on one composed configuration (R-18-004a), five capacity, bandwidth, and area comparisons pricing them (R-18-004b), a demonstration set fixed before the candidates are known (R-18-004d), and both bound to the parameter exploration as hard constraints that prune rather than price (R-15-109a), all stated on the branch where R-15-163's grading does not return so that a materials result adds headroom and never defines the floor. A deletion that costs capacity, or that costs a payload the handler that would open it, is contradictable on the composed system, which is a negative result the method could not previously produce at all.

The rest of the floor is stated against artifacts nobody has built, and R-18-004c books which gate decides each clause rather than letting the clauses read as decided: the schedule R-11-017 emits for every schedule-derived clause, [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) row 15's latency magnitudes for every cycle-valued one, R-15-247m's qualification of a macro nobody has built for the second-class supply figures and the per-island grant, and the design-space exploration [implementation-checklist.md](implementation-checklist.md) schedules no item for. **5. ## The theorem tier covers the hardware nobody has written

**6. Single-prover purity holds exactly where nothing exists, and is abandoned where artifacts do.** The Kami/Kôika Coq refinement is "the only unbounded vehicle" (R-17-039) and is authored **for the net-new blocks that have no legacy RTL**: the capability/tag-carrying DMA fabric, the TDM NoC, the fixed-function sequencers. Those blocks do not exist. The cores that *do* exist (CHERI-CVA6, Ara, Gemmini) close by riding "Sail-generated SystemVerilog plus commercial FEV," which R-17-039 concedes "trusts the equivalence tool," or by a deferred proof over shipped SystemVerilog conditioned on "**if** a foundational Verilog-semantics account matures."

The absence contract has the identical shape: Kôika blocks close in-prover as a predicate over the Coq term; imported cores close on "a structural **audit, not a theorem**." [absence-contract.md](absence-contract.md) states this plainly, and states that day one *is* the imported-core half, "which is exactly the half that closes on audit."

The composed statement is uncomfortable and is nowhere made: **the strongest claims this design makes about the processor it will actually run on rest on a commercial tool's say-so and a netlist search, while the Coq tier (the whole argument for the single-prover discipline) covers only hardware that has not been designed.** That is not dishonest; both halves are booked. It is unassembled, and assembled it says the trust story for the CPU is evidence-tier for the foreseeable future. The absence contract's day-one availability, which §18 celebrates, is availability of the *weaker* discharge form. R-01-002a names the evidence-tier vehicles where G2 is claimed, so the *isolation* claim carries its tier; the observation here, that the single-prover discipline's own tier covers only blocks nobody has designed, is stated in neither §17 nor the absence contract. The tier is carried by the claim that rides the hardware and not by the argument for the discipline that is supposed to underwrite it.

## Booked but open

**7. Duress books its own failure modes and not the dynamic that creates them.** R-17-054's third limit books the honest half: the erase is "a countermeasure rather than a guarantee," it protects future recoverability only, which concedes the adversary who images the device first and compels second, and it is irreversible on accidental entry. R-17-030h carries the seam, stating that the erase is irreversible on the wrong side of a human protocol this design does not model. What neither books is the dynamic a publicly specified duress feature creates: an adversary who knows the feature exists compels *both* credentials, or watches for the erase and escalates against the person rather than the device. The mechanism's real protection is that human protocol; the document names it as unmodeled without saying what its failure costs; and the erase is irreversible on exactly the side where the coercion continues. The entry is narrow and it is not closed, because overclaiming this feature endangers the user who relies on it most.

**8. Physical and sub-partition side channels grow more load-bearing as the device model grows more adversarial.** Probe-level power/EM beyond the masking theorems' model, TEMPEST coupling, RF fingerprinting against link-layer unlinkability, macro-internal and inter-tier coupling, and the event-driven sensor readout's data-dependent timing are all booked (R-17-003b, R-17-055, R-17-058/059) and none is closed. The *seized-and-once-unlocked* and *duress* cases put a physical adversary in scope **by design**, which raises the weight of exactly these residuals. R-17-059 states the sharpest version itself: "the design has **no defence-in-depth layer beneath the package boundary** on the memory path … and if it is ever judged wrong there is no second mechanism behind it." That is the dominant fact again, at the physical layer, stated by the document against itself, and unlike the isolation tier it has no ladder, because a package boundary admits no staged discharge form.

The second static latency class adds two residuals here rather than inheriting any, and both are worse in kind than what this section already carried. **R-17-058e is the sharper of the two**: the class's refresh is a read-and-rewrite of every cell running unconditionally and periodically over the whole array, so a power or near-field probe yields a recurring whole-memory Hamming-weight readout **with no access induced at all**, which is the only member of this class that needs neither software nor a request to provoke. Its reach is narrower than the array and the narrowing is real rather than a mitigation, model weights dominating bulk capacity and being public; what it bites on is the user-derived fraction, the KV cache above all. R-15-247q narrows the window to domains live at power loss and does not close it. Both are stated as accepted costs of the capacity rather than as open work, which is the honest form, and neither has the shape R-17-058a's construction has.

One residual is answered in the form the rest want: R-17-058a carries the masked crypto core, with *d*-probing and composition theorems verified on the artifact against a probing-model statement conferred as a crown jewel, and the entry reduced to the axiom the theorems rest on, a physical model of the silicon whose faithfulness is characterized at bring-up rather than assumed. **9. Three budgets are well-posed and none names its figure.** R-17-035 splits containment (fast, monotone, no proof) from remediation (proof-gated) and budgets *the window*; the quantity in which repeated forcing of the degraded subset is visible is **windows per unit time**. R-03-007/008 put denial in scope and R-17-030m supplies the quantity: entries into containment are budgeted as a rate and recorded as an attested countable event class rather than absorbed as containment succeeding, and the `B-06`×`P-5` cell reads `proved / detected`, the honest mode for it. What is open is what the ceiling *is*. The obligation says the quantity must exist and be recorded, no requirement names a figure, and a well-posed budget with no figure in it is materially better than an unposed one and is not the same as a closed one.

**The second instance is the counter's, and its unstated end is the one that binds.** R-10-013d computes the sustained epoch ceiling from the monotonic counter's rated endurance and the target service life, refuses a composition whose declared commit rates sum past it, and reserves a platform share no application rate can crowd out, which is the same well-posed shape from the supply side. What it does not state is a floor of update generations per device lifetime. Incident response is what spends generations fastest, so the condition under which the remaining headroom decides whether a device can still be fixed is exactly the one the budget's unstated end governs, and a device approaching that end has no specified behaviour because the end is not specified.

**The third instance is admission's, and it is the one every install meets.** R-06-015a puts every proof the CIC kernel checks on the install path under a step and working-set budget, makes exhaustion identical in outcome to a proof that fails to check, and is explicit that the budget is a delivery parameter and not a soundness one, since setting it lower narrows what installs and can never widen what is admitted. That is the well-posed shape a third time. The numeral itself lives in the generation's admission artifact, no entry states a floor or a ceiling for it, and the one direction the register constrains is that exhaustion refuses, so what a device will actually admit is a composer's parameter rather than a platform property. Its two failure directions land on different parties, a figure chosen low costing deliveries and one chosen high costing the user the interval R-17-035a books, and nothing in the corpus decides between them.

## Rejections whose reasoning is premise-dependent

*Scope for this movement: [architectural-alternatives.md](architectural-alternatives.md) (AA) and [inspirations.md](inspirations.md) (IN), spot-checked against [spec.md](spec.md) and [requirements-register.md](requirements-register.md). These are not errors. Each is a place where a disposition rests on something the documents themselves book as unfinished, and the disposition is stated with more finality than its premise supports.

**The single-prover axiom names its ground, and the ground is premised on the theorem this catalogue is waiting for.** R-05-011a grounds the rule on composition rather than on a defect ranking among kernels: T is one linked development, and a constituent discharged in a second logic joins its union only across a semantics bridge nobody has built. That is an argument, and it displaces the value judgment the rule used to read as. It also moves the dependence rather than retiring it, because T is the artifact the dominant fact above says does not exist: the apex statement is authored and the composition meta-lemma is unproved, so the reason for holding to one logic is itself an obligation nothing has discharged. What bounds the exposure is scope, not maturity. R-05-011b confines the rule to trust-base membership and maps any citation of it onto one of three commitments taken elsewhere, R-05-011c reads it as one logic rather than one checking program against a design whose own admission runs two, and R-05-016a takes proof diversity in the only direction that costs nothing, a re-check that can reject and cannot grant. Most of the family stands on other grounds once that scoping is applied, and the residue that still turns on the axiom alone is the trust-base half: the seL4 and VeriBetrFS/Perennial re-proofs and R-17-047's Lean-as-checker refusal, all three waiting on the same unproved composition.

**Verify rather than hedge rests on a proof that does not yet exist**, which is the dominant fact above; what belongs here is the list of dispositions hostage to it, and the list is shorter than the five that cite it. The PMP-backstop, IOMMU, MTE, shadow-stack, and initialization-plane rejections all name CHERI as the mechanism the design verifies most deeply, while R-17-039 records the Sail-to-RTL layer as the least-built and notes no artifact at full-application-core scale. So the disjoint-failure-domain hedge is dropped on the strength of a future artifact for the first pair, R-17-037 books that concentration honestly, and what stays open here is that pair rather than the five. One further reading belongs beside it: a verified primary is not immune to every reason for an independent backstop, since removing duplicate protection shrinks the implementation and its proof obligations without removing specification mistakes, model mismatch, fabrication defects or every transient fault. A second mechanism may share failures and may not earn its cost, and that is a fault-domain analysis rather than a consequence of the primary being verified, which is the assessment Q3's fault-model clause asks for at the touched boundary; it is no argument for reintroducing an MMU as a precaution.

**The out-of-order rejection's premise is two magnitudes nobody has measured.** It is no longer the vertical tier count. The entry states its ground per class, R-15-247 putting bulk behind a slower constant by design and R-15-247j keeping every §11 hard task and all hot code on the first, and it states its position under R-15-173a's single-planar-tier outcome, which moves capacity and neither constant. What the disposition still spends is the size of both, and finding 5 carries them as unauthored: the ground is that the first class's constant is small enough that an in-order core leaves little on the table, and that the second class's is larger but known ahead of the access and therefore schedulable rather than shadowable. Known latency does not make latency hiding irrelevant either: knowing that a load takes a fixed time creates no independent instruction to fill it, reveals no runtime dependency and lifts no register or window limit, so the verification cost is the argument that justifies rejecting dynamic scheduling, and the claim that a deterministic memory removes the latency problem is the weaker one.

**The memory-encryption rejection rests on a scope line the documents call load-bearing.** R-17-059 states it plainly: the memory path is defended by absence of surface, and if that line is ever judged wrong there is no encryption to slow an attacker and no freshness check to catch a replay. The equal-reach claim behind it is asserted per memory class and against a powered die (R-17-059a), the separate-key-holder constructions are dispositioned against it, and one attacker is booked outside the ground rather than absorbed into it: the second memory class after power loss, whose retention outlasts a first-class key, and whose residual R-17-058f already carries open. What stays premise-dependent is the scope line itself, which no analysis narrows.

*The documents are internally disciplined about booking honest residuals, and most of the weaknesses above are acknowledged somewhere in the text. The charge is that the dispositions cite those premises as settled when arguing against alternatives.*

## Refusals that are policy rather than isolation

*Some of the compatibility the design gives up is the price of the guarantees it chooses, and some follows from policies stronger than those guarantees require. AA often shows that a proposal violates a commitment already selected, which establishes consistency and not that the commitment is the best trade. This section sorts the refusals by which they are; the calls it raises are Q9's and Q8's, and no disposition is restated here.*

**The losses that are the price of the guarantees.** Unrestricted legacy binaries, arbitrary privileged firmware, unrestricted DMA and transparent execution of uncertified native code each conflict directly with the authority and admission model, and a stock Linux personality is not a small compatibility patch; the absence of speculative execution and of unrestricted cross-domain resource borrowing has a concrete security rationale as well. None of these is casually restored to recover a benchmark.

**Source-level APIs are not ambient authority.** Bounded adapters for useful library interfaces, explicit-context filesystem APIs and familiar typed application interfaces can preserve capability confinement; full POSIX semantics may be the wrong shape, and refusing a useful subset because it resembles an existing API throws away reusable code without establishing an extra theorem.

**Computing code as data is not executing newly generated native code.** W^X does not by itself forbid a compiler producing an artifact, a local proof search, or execution inside a verified interpreter, and the design already admits compartment-private interpreter caches (R-14-008c) at a conceded intra-compartment timing variability. The wider ban, R-02-008's exclusion of self-hosting with R-13-001c's off-device composer, is therefore a resource and product policy as well as a security choice; a bounded local development facility would want new work and new admission constraints, and it is not logically incompatible with capability isolation or immutable executable memory.

**An external authenticator is not necessarily a platform protection authority.** R-12-020's roaming-key exclusion avoids firmware and parser exposure, and a bounded, capability-confined protocol client need give the key no access to platform memory or control. The key's behaviour stays outside the platform's end-to-end theorem, above all for the credentials it holds, which is an honest external-service boundary and not automatically a failure of platform isolation; a separate possession factor also has a value the built-in authenticator does not reproduce, independence from the loss or compromise of this device, and the current choice gives that up on a very broad reading of what counts as a foreign computer.

**Firmware-free does not entail general-purpose software.** A verified fixed-function media block with bounded interfaces satisfies the core authority and verification goals, the design already admitting fixed-function arithmetic, cryptographic and maintenance mechanisms; moving another bounded operation into hardware adds proof work, and keeping it in software adds timing, power, compiler and scheduling obligations, so what is compared is the total cost. The principle against opaque firmware is strong and it does not entail a ban on specialized implementation; Q8 is where a demonstrated bottleneck is weighed against such a block.

**A permanent dialect deserves a stronger justification than local compactness.** The narrow capability format (R-15-007) has a genuine footprint benefit and the custom instruction representation answers real image pressure; together they forfeit standard binaries, part of upstream validation and tooling reuse while adding local representation obligations. That may be worth it, and the comparator is the entire delivered system and its maintenance rather than a smaller field or decoder. Compatibility supplies independent implementations, testing, replacement components and accumulated failure knowledge, so losing that diversity can lower practical assurance even as the trusted code count falls.

## Where the documents admit a simpler design suffices

The MultiZone entry is the bluntest: PMP-only's appeal is real and almost entirely about realization, and it is rejected as the base *on the goal function, not the effort function*, so for a goal short of maximal assurance the simpler design suffices and the document says so. KataOS is the foil for that reading, an assurance tier short on every shared axis and shipped. The gen-2 deferrals are the same admission from the other side: barrel multithreading, deferred-fault poison loads, and wider superscalar are all held against the in-order tax binding, so if performance never binds the simpler base suffices, and if it does the base was over-simplified. The capacity ceilings, the graded realizations, and the costs booked for judder, throughput, credential portability, and radio coverage are conceded where they are made, each a limit on the product rather than a defect in the argument.

## Redundancy and complexity the documents admit

**There is no duplicate revocation mechanism.** A derivation tree would be a second mechanism for a property the CHERI machinery delivers over strictly more of the machine, and R-08-004 carries the CHERI mechanism alone. IN records the argument rather than the duplication, which is the right residue: the exclusion is the load-bearing half of taking the CHERIoT-shaped object model.

**Temporal safety is covered at three layers.** The static memory plan fixes placement, the CHERI-TAL linear and affine discipline covers compile time, and the load filter with its budgeted sweep and quarantine covers runtime. The documents frame this as composition, and R-05-159 states it as a conjunction rather than a hedge, but it remains the largest standing stack of overlapping mechanisms for one property.

**Two interface formalisms plus a label layer.** A WIT-derived type layer joined to a FIDL-derived wire layer, with flow labels as a first-class concern, is deliberate and is still real surface.

**Five retained instruments are kept on performance arguments alone.** Fractional LMUL, three VLENs, vector masking, indexed gather/scatter with segment operations, and hardware divide and remainder each fail the deletion gate on cycles rather than on proof benefit. The rejected-simplifications table records these as judgment calls.

**Three asymmetries a critic can name.** Macro-op fusion is admitted precisely because it is architecturally transparent while other transparent extensions are cut; the sentinel gets the only lockstepped core, since a detector cannot report its own corruption; the eUICC is the sole admitted foreign computer. Each is justified in place.

**Deferred machinery still looms.** Static code overlays and generation-tag temporal safety are both preserved as options, and each would retire a removal the absence contract enumerates: the `Zifencei` exclusion, whose ground is that no runtime write-then-execute consumer exists, together with the deleted on-device loader for the first; the second-metadata-plane exclusion for the second. Both are named in that register against the trigger that would fire them (R-15-100b, R-15-047, R-13-010, R-15-035), so the removals read as contingent on the capacity bets exactly where they are.

## Not gaps: the ceilings this document keeps naming

*These are stated correctly, they are permanent or nearly so, and numbering them as findings would pad this catalogue's own count in the way it charges the specification with inflating claims elsewhere.*

- **Specification error is first-ranked, and is the reason the inventory exists.** Twenty-six crown jewels, each a small oracle that must be *stated* right and whose wrongness no proof catches: the delimited-release bound, the ASN.1/UPER codec, the dedup keyed-digest interface, the HAL's DMA postconditions, the IDL initialization annotations, the WCET latency magnitudes, the `Zkt`/`Zvkt` leakage model, the Ztso and static-prediction fetch models, every wire-format mapping, each Lustre program. Unchanged in kind, large in extent, and measurable, which is the whole point of having counted them.
- **Fabrication trust is evidence-based, not proof-based.** IRIS backside inspection makes fab an obtainable-evidence question rather than an act of faith, and the tier grading is a genuinely strong position: everything that *acts* is imaged, and what is not imaged cannot act. The ceiling stands where R-17-063 puts it: coarser than the smallest features, *evidence not proof*, and a sub-resolution fab adversary stays in scope. Narrowed, not removed.
- **The bedrock assumptions, the human, and the eUICC stay outside the trusted computer.** MLWE/MSIS and ECDLP/CDH are irreducible axioms; the user is outside every consent theorem, on the comprehension half that stays irreducible (R-17-013) and the abuse-resistance half the register books open by name (R-17-013e); the eUICC remains "the one tolerated foreign computer," contained to zero platform authority but an admitted exception to the design's defining claim. All three are permanent, and the *no foreign computers / everything verified* framing should keep saying so out loud.
- **Verifying the checker is the wrong instrument, and the register has decided the question three times already.** A checker's real risk is not unsoundness but a rule that decides nothing: a pattern encoding its own answer, a count with no predicate, a comparison made against an empty set. A soundness proof is useless against that, since a rule that always passes is trivially correct against a specification saying it always passes; the instrument for it is falsification, and `run.py selftest` already requires every rule to fail on a deliberately broken tree. The polarity is benign beside it, a false green costing a drifted document the R-05-150 review re-derives and a false red costing an author an afternoon, and no item of R-06-001's seven-item TCB is a checker of this kind. R-05-020 admits a new anchor only on three shown conditions of which retiring an interim is one, and this retires none. What settles it is precedent rather than argument: R-05-064 deleted a whole verified translation-validation route rather than deferring it and booked the cost, and R-05-066 states the general rule, that an untrusted producer whose output an existing checker re-validates is admissible and free to be aggressive. Verifying a producer instead of checking its output is the named anti-pattern, and the tools are producers.
- **A record's field list is a lexical fact, so the apex parse stays in Python and on the host wave.** The standing proposal is that where a prover run already happens the artifact should emit its own metadata rather than be parsed for it, and that [apex.py](../tools/vos/apex.py)'s regex over the `Vocabulary` record of [ApexTheorem.v](../proofs/ApexTheorem.v) should become a `Compute` printing its own Prop-field list. It is refused on the proposal's own principle, which splits *lexical* facts, a comment annotation or a constant's name, decided by Python on the host wave with no toolchain, from *semantic* facts about terms, what a theorem quantifies over or whether a witness inhabits a type, which are the prover's. A field list is a declaration read off the source text and sits on the first side, so the move would contradict the rule it is filed under. The cost is concrete rather than stylistic: that one parse backs K-42, K-43 and K-44 and a floor of K-47, and `tools/check.py` runs in CI on a clone with no submodule checked out and no prover installed, so the move would take four rules CI decides today and make them undecidable there. The prover's reader is guest-only besides, and a harness is staged outside [proofs/](../proofs/) deliberately, the proof gate compiling everything it finds there. What the proposal was right about is the failure it feared, a parse that silently returns the wrong thing while every gate stays green, and that is answered where it arises rather than by moving the parse: the guard that could not report the one shape it was added to catch is the defect worth having found.
- **The booked residuals are booked, not overlooked.** Wrong responses to a failed verdict, runtime-value-dependent overflow, analog power and EM leakage, human consent, protocol-standard flaws, and fail-closed denial are each recorded as residuals rather than as overlooked bugs; where one earns a finding above (3, 8), it earns it for what the booking still lacks, not for being missing. A booking says nothing about whether a deployment can bear the residual, which no finding here charges and the product reading above prices.
