# Critique: Remaining Gaps in the Architecture and Specification

*Non-normative. A gap catalogue for the current state of [verification-maximal-os.md](verification-maximal-os.md) and its companions. Rewritten after the previous edition's gaps 8, 13, 14, and 15 were answered by the atomic-requirements register and the four derived views, and after the artifact roughly doubled again. This edition was checked against the artifacts rather than against its own predecessor, which cost the previous edition's availability finding (closed by [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md)), its count of prose-discharged acceptance criteria (unreproducible), and its claim that the isolation substrate was left unstaged (it is staged; the staging is in the wrong section). Findings that were retired or corrected are said so in place, because a gap catalogue whose errors leave no trace is a worse instrument than the specification it audits.*

The previous edition found the mechanism-level architecture settled and the remaining gaps to be "execution, specification, scope-honesty, or human factors." That framing was too generous, and the artifacts that landed since prove it: the specification acquired the review machinery it was told it lacked, and the machinery's first act was to make the project's real position **countable**. It is worse than the prose reads. Twenty-two crown-jewel specifications are named; **one** is authored. Seven theorem targets are named; **none** can start, because each is premised on a specification that does not exist. Every hedge the design deleted was deleted on the strength of a verified primary, and **no primary is verified**.

Gaps are numbered fresh and sorted by kind, not severity. The most important (1) is that the design has already spent its defense-in-depth and has not yet bought the proofs it spent it on. That is still true; what changed while this edition was open is that the specification now says so where the claim is read, which converts gap 1 from a composition defect into a booked exposure with a retirement condition and leaves the exposure itself exactly where it was.

**Six of the eighteen are two findings, and they are composed here rather than left to the reader.** Gaps 9, 14, and 15 are three instances of *no discipline for composing residuals*: each mechanism is booked honestly alone, and the position they jointly describe is stated nowhere. Gaps 4, 5, and 6 are three instances of *the checker is referential and the risk is semantic*: what the toolchain decides is always a question about reference, and every question about whether a statement is the right statement is still owed to a review that has not run. Read as six separate items they understate both, which is the failure the closing note charges the specification with and this document would otherwise repeat.

## Retired since the last edition

Stated briefly, because the closures were real and the rest of this document is not generous.

- **Protocol-level security** is now in §3's Defended/Residual split by name, as "the largest scope boundary the word *hyper-secure* crosses", the previous critique's exact complaint, adopted verbatim.
- **The end-to-end composition obligation** is now a first-class §17 residual (R-17-064/065) and the §18 capstone workstream (R-18-031/032), with its statement, its seam interfaces, and its boundary ledger (*D*, *Ax*) named as deliverables.
- **The review artifact** exists: [requirements-register.md](requirements-register.md), 1034 numbered requirements with acceptance criteria and traces, plus four derived views and `tools/check.ps1`. §5's gate now has a subject.
- **The RoT-fresh state set** is normatively enumerated and closed (R-10-013), against the previous edition's complaint that it was left to per-feature judgment.
- **The CHERI single-mechanism concentration** is now a substantial §17 entry (R-17-037) stating the four-part trade and naming *verify rather than hedge* as the posture, rather than a residual footnote.
- **The coverage argument became decidable**, and it took the previous edition's availability complaint with it. R-17-001a claims coverage top down (every boundary against every property, discharged or booked) and R-17-001b lands the artifact that claim quantifies over: [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md), nine boundaries by seven properties, every pair of them carrying its construction, its discharge mode, and the requirements carrying it, with `tools/check.ps1` failing on a pair that is missing, duplicated, or rests on nothing. **Availability and progress is one of the seven** (`P-5`), which is the closure the previous edition's availability gap asked for and did not expect; what survives of it is gap 8, narrowed to the two places the matrix does not reach.

Four of these landed within twenty-four hours of the critique that asked for them, and the coverage matrix inside a day of the one after. That responsiveness is the strongest evidence in the repository that the specification workstream is real, and it is also the source of gap 5.

## The dominant fact

**1. Every hedge is already spent, no primary is yet verified, and the interval between is now claimed at the tier it holds.** This was the deepest problem in the document and it was a *timing* error, not a reasoning error, and an error of **placement** rather than of omission, which the previous edition got wrong and which is what made it cheap to close. It has been closed, in the one paragraph this entry asked for; the rest of the entry is retained because it is the argument the closure rests on, and because the exposure the closure makes legible is unchanged.

The *verify rather than hedge* rule is applied consistently and its logic is sound at the fixed point: a mechanism whose only job is to catch the failure of a formally verified primary buys little against the cost of a second modeled mechanism. On that rule the design deletes the MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, the initialization-tag plane, MTE, shadow stacks, the Harvard split, and every disjoint backstop on the machine (R-17-037, R-17-045). §17 states the hedge that remains: "the **formal verification of CHERI itself**," resting on "the **RTL ⊑ Sail** arrow … and its Coq-native restatement for the CHERI-RISC-V profile as the residual."

That arrow is the layer the same document calls **the least-built layer of the stack** (R-17-039, R-18-010). The CHERI-TAL soundness metatheorem that replaces the initialization-tag plane is theorem target `CJ-TAL-SOUND`, premised on the ISA profile and **not started**. The certifying compiler, the on-device checker, the Cerise universal contract, the secure-compilation theorem: none exists.

So the rule is sound at the fixed point and its premise is false everywhere before it. At the fixed point, hedges against verified primaries are waste. On the entire trajectory to that fixed point (which on this roster is a multi-year interval, and which is the only state the project has ever occupied or will occupy for a long time) the primaries are *unverified*, and a hedge against an unverified primary is exactly ordinary defense in depth, so any device that actually executes in that interval runs with the elegance and without the argument. The design has taken delivery of the elegance now and deferred the proof that pays for it, and **G2 ("compromising any component yields only its explicitly granted authority") rests on evidence rather than proof for the whole interval**, which is the fact the closure below makes the goals and the threat model state and does not alter. A single CHERI logic error in monotonicity, revocation, or the tag plane fails every isolation boundary on the machine at once, with nothing beneath it and, until `CJ-TAL-SOUND` and the Coq refinement close, no proof above it.

**What is missing is the composition, not the staging, and the distinction is what makes the finding actionable.** The spec does stage this arrow, in both of the two forms available to it, and each is honest where it sits. On the compiler and type-system half there is deliberately *no* interval: the certifier is a hard prerequisite, no uncertified app is ever admitted, and R-13-022/R-17-033 book the consequence as a **delivery** risk rather than a period of degraded admission, which is the strongest possible answer to "what holds in the interim": nothing runs. On the RTL half there is a ladder: R-18-010 stages the discharge form (rvfi, then Sail-generated SystemVerilog plus commercial FEV, then Isla obligations, then the Kami/Kôika Coq refinement) and R-18-013 states plainly that base bring-up is not blocked on the full proof and that **only the unbounded claim** waits on it. That is precisely the *what the platform claims before then* this gap wants, and demanding retained backstops instead would be asking for the wrong thing: MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, MTE, and the Harvard split are **silicon**, so "retain until the theorem closes" means fabricating them, modelling them in Sail, and paying the second-modeled-mechanism cost the rule exists to price, and R-18-002 forecloses a capability-degraded interim by name. The deletions are irreversible in a way the proofs are not; the interim *claim* is the only knob that still turns.

The defect was that the knob was never turned where anyone reads it: R-18-013's ladder lived in §18 and was stated per workstream, while §1's G2 and §3's Defended list were stated at full strength with no tier qualifier, so a reader of the goals and the threat model got the fixed-point claim and the document holding the interim claim sat eleven sections away, phrased as schedule.

**That paragraph has been written, and it is the shape this entry asked for.** §1 now carries the discharge tier on G2 itself (R-01-002a, R-01-002b): the software premise admits no interval, because nothing runs uncertified and a missing certificate costs delivery rather than admission; the hardware premise stands at **evidence tier** (rvfi, Sail-generated SystemVerilog under commercial FEV, the imported-core netlist audit) rather than **theorem tier** (the Kami/Kôika Coq refinement), every FPGA and early-silicon unit asserts G2 at that tier and no higher, the tier rises per core when that core's unbounded refinement is admitted and never at an intermediate rung, and an attestation or datasheet asserting G2 without its tier is a review-gate finding. §3 carries the inheritance (R-03-006): the defended set enumerates scope and not strength, and the mechanism most of its entries share is at evidence tier until the ladder's unbounded rung closes. R-18-013 now points up at those three rather than holding the disposition alone. The retirement condition is R-18-010's rungs, on R-05-022's two-list discipline, applied to the one anchor that carries every isolation boundary on the machine.

**What this bought is legibility, not safety, and the distinction is the whole of what survives.** No mechanism changed and none could have: the deleted backstops are silicon, "retain until the theorem closes" means fabricating and modelling them and paying the second-modeled-mechanism cost the rule exists to price, and R-18-002 forecloses a capability-degraded interim by name. So the exposure is untouched. A CHERI logic error still fails every boundary at once; the interval is still multi-year; the tier is still the weaker one for all of it. What changed is that a reader of §1 and §3 alone now learns this, which is precisely what §17 says a reader cannot be trusted to work out for themselves. Two things are worth keeping in view. The tier statement is a semantic claim about how strongly a claim is underwritten, so `check.ps1` sees its referential integrity and nothing else: whether a future datasheet honours R-01-002b is gap 4's class, owed to a review that has not run. And the closure is a claim about the interval, so it is load-bearing for exactly as long as the interval lasts and retires with it, which makes it the cheapest thing in this catalogue to have written and the easiest to let go stale.

**2. Nothing load-bearing is built, and the specification is now precise enough to say how little.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the honest instrument the previous critique asked for, and its status column reads: of twenty-two crown-jewel specifications, **one is authored** (the frozen ISA profile), three are partial, **eighteen are not authored**. The seven theorem targets each depend on a row above; eighteen of those premises do not exist, so most of the proof program cannot begin rather than merely being unfinished.

The repository's own history sharpens this. As of 2026-08-10: thirty-six days, 307 commits, roughly 2.0 MB of design documents, and, outside a single 43 KB PowerShell consistency checker, **zero lines of system code** (`git ls-files` returns exactly one non-Markdown path). The figures are dated because they are derived facts and this document is the one place in the repository that copies them by hand rather than recomputing them, which is the drift the register's own discipline (R-05-151a, and every count `check.ps1` rebuilds) exists to prevent, reappearing in its critic. Read them as a snapshot with a direction, and recompute before citing. The project's only demonstrated velocity is in producing obligations. That is a defensible way to spend an early phase, and §18 and R-01-003 are candid that the as-existing assurance is low. What is *not* stated anywhere is the ratio's direction: the obligation count is growing monotonically and the discharge count is one. Every hardening that made the design safer added a crown jewel; nothing in the process retires one except authoring it.

**3. The five day-one deliverables are the project's only fair scorecard, and the highest-value one is missing.** R-18-003b names five things that "gate on nothing" and need no engineering: the profile freeze and its Sail curation; the microarchitectural absence contract; the **machine-checked statement of T and the seam lemmas**; the atomic-requirements register; and the proof-artifact hygiene gates (assumption gating and the non-vacuity witness, each a predicate over whatever proof artifact exists, so both are wired ahead of the development they will police). Three exist as artifacts in the repository and the fourth is stated as a predicate with nothing yet to police. The one that does not exist in any form is the one R-18-032 itself calls "the higher-value half" and "**the review artifact the crown-jewel gate most needs**", and it is crown-jewel row 1, status *not authored*.

§5 contains T in prose, and that prose is good. But the deliverable is the machine-checked statement with its four elements, the seam interfaces aligned so each conclusion types as the next's premise, doubling as the coverage checklist. Until it exists, the seam list is a list of *names*, and the claim that the seams' conclusions and premises meet is unchecked in exactly the way §17 says a chain of individually-sound proofs can fail. The composition obligation was booked (retired gap) and then not started, which is a smaller victory than it looked.

## The review gate audits its own reflection

**4. The gate that guards the first-ranked risk cannot see the first-ranked risk.** §5's release gate exists because "a proof against a wrong spec verifies perfectly." R-18-034 then states plainly that the gate "audits the register rather than this prose." So an unverified extraction now stands between the reviewer and the design of record, and is declared the authoritative object.

Nothing checks that the extraction is faithful. `check.ps1` checks **referential integrity**: that cited bookmarks resolve, that requirements carry traces, that no view drops a requirement in its bearing subsections, that no asserted count disagrees with its artifact. A fourth group checks punctuation and encoding damage, which is the same shape one layer further down. The reach is wider than it sounds and includes this document: every count asserted here is anchored in the checker's registry and recomputed, so an edition of this critique cannot quietly disagree with the artifacts about a number, and the figures in gap 2 are stale only because no claim holds them. That is a genuinely strong instrument, and it is the wrong shape for this risk: a requirement can resolve perfectly, trace perfectly, appear in every view, count correctly, and state the wrong thing. The tooling produces exactly the reassurance the gate must not have.

Worse, part of the gate is satisfiable by reading the document back to itself. A class of acceptance criteria is discharged by inspecting the prose rather than the artifact: *"the axiom is stated in §6 and §17, not implied"* (R-06-014); *"the decision is stated as a design choice with its consequence, not omitted"* (R-10-011); *"the consent TCB is named in §6 rather than hidden in userland"* (R-06-018); *"the split is stated rather than blurred"* (R-13-023); *"the cost is booked rather than omitted"* (R-12-020); *"the trade is stated in §15, §12, and here"* (R-17-051).

**The size of the class depends entirely on where the line is drawn, and no single number is defensible.** Matching only criteria of the quoted shape (the test being that the prose says a thing, in those words) yields **seven of 1034 acceptance criteria**. Widening to any criterion whose test uses *stated*, *booked*, *named*, *recorded*, or *declared* without naming an artifact or `check.ps1` yields **116**; narrowing that to the ones citing a `§` as the thing to open yields **25**. A single figure quoted without its rule (an earlier edition of this document asserted seventy-seven, ~8%, and no rule produces it) is the same defect as the one being charged, so the honest statement is the band and the method: **7 strictly, 25 on the reading most people mean, 116 at the loosest**, each reproducible against the register.

**And these entries are not defects under R-05-153, which is where the previous edition mis-stated the finding.** That rule catches a claim that *cannot be restated as an atomic, testable requirement*; "the consent TCB is named in §6 rather than hidden in userland" is atomic, and it is testable by inspection, which is a test. Convicting the register on that rule requires silently reading *testable requirement* as *testable property of the system*, and the register never says that. The real defect is one layer over: R-05-152 makes the register **the audited object** and the prose commentary, so a requirement whose acceptance criterion is a fact about the commentary spends the gate's scarcest resource (a reviewer's attention, per requirement, on the one pass that decides whether the specification says the right thing) on the document's editorial hygiene rather than on the machine's behaviour. That is a real cost, it is measurable in the band above, and it wants a **tag rather than a defect entry**: mark the spec-hygiene entries as such, so the review can decide how much of its budget they deserve instead of discovering them one at a time.

**5. The extraction-defect list opened on the wrong axis, and what it still does not carry is the finding.** The list is no longer empty: it books `D-CSR`, one defect with seven rows, and the register is right that "an empty list was not the same as a swept one."

But look at what opened it. All seven rows are one class (surviving CSRs that [isa-profile.md](isa-profile.md) §5 had to enumerate and that no requirement decides), and the instrument that found them was building a derived view and machine-checking it, not re-reading the prose. **R-05-153's actual class, a normative claim that would not decompose, still has zero entries**: not one of the 1034 requirements has yet been booked as standing for a passage that resisted restatement. Gap 4's spec-hygiene entries are *not* the missing entries, for the reason gap 4 gives: they decomposed fine, into requirements about the document. The list's emptiness on its own stated class is therefore unexplained by anything found so far, which is the finding.

So the two readings stand. Either the prose was already atomic throughout (implausible for a document whose §17 entries routinely run five clauses deep with three parenthetical qualifications) or the extraction **accommodated** the prose where it should have confronted it, restating hard passages as requirements whose acceptance criteria are facts about those passages (gap 4's class is the mechanism, even though its entries are not themselves defects). The register does not claim otherwise: its standing instruction is that sweep 2's question "has not been asked exhaustively, so further instances should be assumed present rather than absent," which is the concession, made in the right place and load-bearing here. The sweeps that produced `D-CSR` are a real instrument aimed at referential integrity, and the channel that matters more, the one that reports what the *prose* refused to decompose, is still not running. That list should be expected to be long and should be published long. It is the agenda R-18-035 says it is, and a one-item agenda drawn from a single enumeration means the meeting has barely started.

**6. The derived views can contradict themselves and nothing in the toolchain would know.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is declared "Normative as a **view**," and on the day it was written it carried two internal contradictions about which of its own rows were the closure defect: a heading naming five rows over a sentence substantiating four, and a bullet refuting itself mid-clause. Both were the kind of thing review catches, both sat in a *normative* artifact committed the same day the corpus was reported clean, and the machinery reported it clean anyway.

They have since been repaired, and the repair is instructive rather than reassuring: what fixed them was removing the roll-call they disagreed about (gap 7), not a check that could see the disagreement.

**The class has since been narrowed on one axis, and the narrowing is the model for what is still open.** `check.ps1` now parses the inventory's status column into its three classes, recomputes the authored/partial/not-authored ratio, and fails where a document asserts a figure the artifact does not bear, so the specific contradiction that opened this gap (a heading and a sentence disagreeing about a count of rows) is now a failing check rather than a reading. That is a genuine closure of the *quantitative* half, and it was bought exactly the way gap 7 says such things must be: by making something mechanical quantify over the claim.

What is untouched is the qualitative half, which is most of it. Whether a row's status is *honest* (that `partial` is not doing work `not authored` should do), whether a cited requirement is the *right* ground, whether a discharge mode in [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md) reports what its cited requirements actually carry, whether a note describes the requirement it names: all invisible to `check.ps1` by construction, because the checker is referential and these are not referential defects. The coverage matrix widens the exposure as it closes gap 8: every cell a semantic claim that a construction carries a property at a boundary, all machine-checked for *presence* and none for *truth*. The review that could see them still has not run, and a view is normative whether or not it has been read.

**7. "Closed by amendment" is the design's standard closure mechanism, and it holds in exactly one of its two instances, the one where it stopped being a convention.** R-17-016 closes the crown-jewel list by amendment, and that closure originally failed: specifications acquired crown-jewel status through requirements outside the enumeration, and three disagreeing sources arose. The repair was not a better-maintained list but the deletion of the list: membership is conferred by each requirement that asserts it, [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the one place the conferrals are collected, and `check.ps1` fails on a conferral with no row or a row with no requirement. Closure by amendment became closure by construction, and it is the strongest instance in the repository of the register discipline paying for itself.

R-10-013 then closes the RoT-freshness set by the *unrepaired* mechanism, and its acceptance criterion asserts the four attacks the set blocks without arguing that the map from attack to counter-protected state is total. The previous critique asked for that set to be "enumerated normatively and argued closed." Half arrived. The enumeration is real; the closure argument is still the social convention, and the pattern the crown-jewel case establishes, that a membership claim survives only where something mechanical quantifies over it, is exactly what has not been applied here. It is also not obvious that it can be: freshness-criticality is a judgment about attacks, not a phrase a checker can grep for, which makes this the harder half and the one still open.

## Availability is now owed at every boundary and claimed at none

**8. The coverage matrix made availability an obligation; the threat model still has not.** The previous edition of this document said no requirement makes availability a property the platform owes its user, and that is no longer true. R-17-001a obliges every boundary the specification names to carry every property in the coverage vocabulary, discharged by a requirement or booked as a residual, and `P-5` **Availability and progress** ("the boundary cannot be made to stop, or to block without bound") is one of the seven. All nine of its cells are filled, and six are discharged rather than booked away: fixed worst-case latency for divide, vector floating point, atomics, and the partition fence (`B-01`, hardware-enforced); WCET derived syntax-directed with no admission for a task set lacking a schedulability proof (`B-02`); metered service on the session's own slot with the switch-duty ratio counted at admission (`B-03`); bounded sequencer turnaround and admitted polling cadence (`B-04`); machine-checked deadlock and livelock freedom over a crash-refining journal (`B-05`); admitted hard radio deadlines with a stack crash costing connectivity rather than integrity (`B-06`). Three are residual and correctly so: the compositor may deny service (`B-07`), a network adversary may stall fresh time (`B-08`), and the certifying compiler is a delivery prerequisite (`B-09`). An uncovered pair is a failing check. Availability is not currency here; it is a column, and it is mostly paid.

Two things survive, and they are narrower and harder than the claim they replace.

**First, §3 still does not carry it.** Availability appears in neither the Defended list nor the Residual list, so the coverage argument obliges nine cells of it while the threat model declines to say whether an adversary whose objective is denial is in scope at all. The gap is now a *scope* statement rather than a missing mechanism: the matrix answers "does this boundary block without bound," and §3 never answers "is an adversary who wants the device silent a defended adversary." The threat model claims hostile radio infrastructure by name, and that adversary's actual objective is very often to keep a device off the network or off a call. One sentence in §3 closes it, in either direction, defended with the `P-5` row as the discharge or residual with the reason, and the absence is what makes gap 14's attacker unmeasurable.

**Second, the matrix decomposes by boundary, which is exactly the axis the real failure crosses.** Each cell asks whether *one* boundary can be made to stop. The failure in gap 9 is a device silenced by nine mechanisms each behaving correctly at its own boundary, and no cell of a boundary-indexed matrix can see it. That is not a defect in the matrix; it is the limit of its shape, and it is why gap 9 stands undiminished by gap 8's closure.

## The fail-closed set

**9. The fail-closed set has never been composed, and the design has a discipline for exactly this failure that it did not apply here.** §17's hardware seam register exists on an explicit finding: *"the residual pattern is that gaps live where two retained mechanisms meet, so the meetings are enumerated."* Thirteen hardware seams are named with owners. The proof seams are named. `P-5` of the coverage matrix now names the boundaries (gap 8), which is a different question: a boundary is a place where parties meet, and a **seam** is a place where two of this platform's own retained mechanisms meet. There is **no availability seam register** of that second kind, and the components are all booked individually:

thermal trip is fail-stop (§15) · watchdog bite is reset (R-16-005) · admission failure refuses code (§13) · a certifier that cannot emit a derivation refuses a safe program (R-17-033) · a radio crash costs connectivity until restart (R-16-002) · a driver fighting the consent transition denies the prompt (R-12-082-2) · a compromised compositor's best play is premature revocation (§8, §12) · containment drops the attacked surface to a safe subset for machine-hours-to-days (R-17-035) · duress crypto-erases irreversibly (R-17-054) · the population wall gives a background origin **on the order of one percent of one core** (R-17-005) · emergency calling requires 5G-SA/6G coverage and nothing else (R-17-052) · the sealed cutoffs are **not** overridden for emergencies, by design (R-17-052).

Each of these is individually correct and individually honest. Composed, they describe a device that an adversary who can provoke faults, thermal events, or crash-restart loops can degrade or silence at will, and whose worst case is a **life-safety** one: a user who cannot place an emergency call because the platform fail-stopped, or fell back to a coverage floor it does not have, or has a physical switch closed that no software may reopen. §17 books the last of these as an accepted residual and is right that a software override is worthless. It does not compose it with the other eleven. The document's own rule, *a mechanism admitted alone is not admitted until its meetings are*, is the missing instrument, and applying it to the fail-closed set is a day-one, engineering-free deliverable of exactly the kind §18 already knows how to name.

## Deletion has no opposing artifact

**10. The first release has no stated minimum viable capability, so deletion is unfalsifiable.** Assemble what §18 commits to for the first parts: Wi-Fi only, no cellular, no eUICC; **no browser**, deferred as the largest porting program; **1–2 GB** of planar single-tier SRAM; six C-class cores with software rendering at initial bring-up; no JIT anywhere; no fixed-function GPU; a few-active-things machine where the foreground is fast and the background is "very nearly stopped."

Nowhere does the specification state what this device **does for a user**. There is no requirement naming a target workload the first release must serve, no floor on the roster, no acceptance criterion of the form *this configuration runs this set of things at this quality*. [userspace-porting.md](userspace-porting.md) is the closest thing and declines the job in its own words: the applications are "the **elective** applications" and the roster is "elective and stageable," a sequencing document by design, so nothing on it can fail to arrive. [performance-estimates.md](performance-estimates.md) scores deltas against an out-of-order baseline and reports the honest matrix by archetype; it does not answer whether the resulting absolute is a product.

That omission is what makes *delete-rather-than-defend* structurally unfalsifiable. Every deletion is scored on the axis it improves (one fewer Sail obligation, one fewer block on the least-built arrow, one fewer trust anchor), and the ledger runs only that way, because the axis that would push back (does the surviving machine still do the job?) has no requirement to be violated and no artifact to run. Twenty-plus deletions are each booked as a win at specification time, and not one of them can be contradicted until silicon exists. A design method with no possible negative result is not being tested by its own process; it is being narrated by it.

## The theorem tier covers the hardware nobody has written

**11. Single-prover purity holds exactly where nothing exists, and is abandoned where artifacts do.** The Kami/Kôika Coq refinement is "the only unbounded vehicle" (R-17-039) and is authored **for the net-new blocks that have no legacy RTL**: the capability/tag-carrying DMA fabric, the TDM NoC, the fixed-function sequencers. Those blocks do not exist. The cores that *do* exist (CHERI-CVA6, Ara, Gemmini) close by riding "Sail-generated SystemVerilog plus commercial FEV," which R-17-039 concedes "trusts the equivalence tool," or by a deferred proof over shipped SystemVerilog conditioned on "**if** a foundational Verilog-semantics account matures."

The absence contract has the identical shape: Kôika blocks close in-prover as a predicate over the Coq term; imported cores close on "a structural **audit, not a theorem**." [absence-contract.md](absence-contract.md) states this plainly and honestly, and states that day one *is* the imported-core half, "which is exactly the half that closes on audit."

The composed statement is uncomfortable and is nowhere made: **the strongest claims this design makes about the processor it will actually run on rest on a commercial tool's say-so and a netlist search, while the Coq tier (the whole argument for the single-prover discipline) covers only hardware that has not been designed.** That is not dishonest; both halves are booked. It is unassembled, and assembled it says the trust story for the CPU is evidence-tier for the foreseeable future. The absence contract's day-one availability, which §18 celebrates, is availability of the *weaker* discharge form. Gap 1's closure assembles one consequence of this and not the finding itself: R-01-002a now names the evidence-tier vehicles (FEV plus netlist audit) where G2 is claimed, so the *isolation* claim carries its tier, while the observation this entry makes, that the single-prover discipline's own tier covers only blocks nobody has designed, is still stated in neither §17 nor the absence contract. The tier is now carried by the claim that rides the hardware; it is not yet carried by the argument for the discipline that was supposed to underwrite it.

## Booked but open

**12. Consent comprehension remains outside every theorem, and nothing has been built below the prompt.** §8/§12 close the mechanism side completely: robust delimited declassification, RoT secure-attention indicator, RoT-latched input ownership for the prompt's duration, a ceiling-bounded lease in place of an unevaluable *while-active*. That work is excellent and it is finished. R-17-013 then files "the user consented to the wrong thing" as the irreducible ceiling.

**The ceiling is two claims wearing one name, and only one of them is irreducible.** *The user did not understand what they authorized* is genuinely outside any theorem and always will be, and R-17-013 is right to book it. *A correctly-rendered, genuinely-attested prompt can be issued often enough, or shaped well enough, that a competent user authorizes something they would refuse on reflection* is a different claim, and it is **unaddressed rather than irreducible**. R-17-013c names the closest thing to it, booking that the ceiling is exercised *at machine rate* once an autonomous agent is the principal doing the asking, with the human adjudicator as the scarce resource and standing grants relieving the fatigue only by widening scope, and its acceptance criterion is that "no mechanism is claimed to decide the fatigue-versus-scope trade on the user's behalf." A repository-wide search for grant rate-limiting, grant-pattern anomaly detection, consent habituation, or comprehension scaffolding returns **nothing** across the corpus; the only rate-limiter in it guards unlock attempts (R-09-018, R-12-017), never grants. The design defends duress credentials, biometric vaults, and seized-device forensics (a threat model that presumes a high-risk user), and social-engineering an unspoofable prompt is precisely how such users are compromised. A flurry of individually-valid consents is not modeled as suspicious anywhere.

**The fix is not the obvious one, and this is the rare place where the design's refusal would be right.** Grant rate-limiting, consent-pattern anomaly detection, and habituation heuristics are policy judgments with no specification to be proven against: each would be a new oracle whose wrongness no proof catches (gap 16's class, at its worst), added to the one path §6 keeps smallest, deciding on the user's behalf by heuristic what the design elsewhere refuses to decide without a theorem. Building one to close this gap would trade a stated ceiling for an unverifiable mechanism inside the trusted path, the very trade *verify rather than hedge* exists to refuse. What is owed is therefore cheaper and entirely in this document's own currency: **split R-17-013** into the comprehension ceiling (irreducible, keep saying so) and the abuse-resistance ceiling (open, with the argument for why no admissible mechanism closes it), so that *unspoofable* stops being quoted where *abuse-resistant* is what a reader wants. Today one word does both jobs, and that is what converts a design gap into a ceiling.

**13. Duress is a countermeasure whose principal risk is its own existence.** R-17-054(3) books irreversibility on accidental entry and books the already-extracted-keys case. It does not book the coercion dynamic: a duress feature that is publicly specified invites an adversary to compel *both* credentials, or to image first and compel second, or simply to watch for the erase and escalate. Its real protection is a human protocol the document does not model, and the erase is irreversible on the wrong side of that protocol. Overclaiming it endangers the user who relies on it most.

**14. Remediation stays proof-gated on the most-attacked surface, and compounds gap 9.** R-17-035 splits containment (fast, monotone, no proof) from remediation (proof-gated) honestly and budgets the window. The cost is unchanged and correctly refused: no unproven hot-patch reaches the radio. But an attacker who can repeatedly force the degraded subset achieves durable partial denial of service *under cover of "containment worked."* The coverage matrix does not catch this and is not shaped to: `B-06`/`P-5` asks whether the radio boundary blocks without bound and answers with admitted hard deadlines and a bounded restart cost, which is a statement about one crash, not about an adversary who can cause them on demand. R-17-035 budgets *the window*; nothing budgets **windows per unit time**, which is the only quantity in which this attack is visible. And because §3 never books denial as an adversary objective at all (gap 8), there is no scope statement the accumulation could be measured against: the metric is missing above the mechanism, not beside it.

**15. Physical and sub-partition side channels grow more load-bearing as the device model grows more adversarial.** Probe-level power/EM, TEMPEST coupling, RF fingerprinting against link-layer unlinkability, macro-internal and inter-tier coupling, and the event-driven sensor readout's data-dependent timing are all booked (R-17-003b, R-17-055, R-17-058/059) and none is closed. The *seized-and-once-unlocked* and *duress* cases put a physical adversary in scope **by design**, which raises the weight of exactly these residuals. R-17-059 states the sharpest version itself: "the design has **no defence-in-depth layer beneath the package boundary** on the memory path … and if it is ever judged wrong there is no second mechanism behind it." That is gap 1 again, at the physical layer, stated by the document against itself, and unlike gap 1 it has no ladder, because a package boundary admits no staged discharge form.

One of these has been worked out in the open and is worth naming as the pattern: [bug-class-coverage-todo.md](bug-class-coverage-todo.md) item 10 states the power/EM position plainly: no masking, no probing-model claim, and masking with a machine-checked proof named as the only construction-grade answer along with its real cost in randomness, area, and a new leakage assumption. That is precisely the composed statement this gap asks for, written for one residual. It is non-normative, so §17 still carries the weaker form; promoting it is a paragraph of work and the model for the rest.

## Irreducible

**16. Specification error is first-ranked and now countable.** Twenty-two crown jewels, each a small oracle that must be *stated* right and whose wrongness no proof catches. The design became safer by adding correct-by-construction machinery and each piece added an oracle: the delimited-release bound, the ASN.1/UPER codec, the dedup keyed-digest interface, the HAL's DMA postconditions, the IDL initialization annotations, the WCET latency magnitudes, the `Zkt`/`Zvkt` leakage model, the Ztso and static-prediction fetch models, every wire-format mapping, each Lustre program. Unchanged in kind, larger in extent, and now, thanks to the inventory, measurable. That measurement is the register's real contribution, and it should be tracked as a burn-down with a stated direction rather than a status column that only ever grows.

**17. Fabrication trust is evidence-based, not proof-based.** IRIS backside inspection makes fab an obtainable-evidence question rather than an act of faith, and the tier grading is a genuinely strong position: everything that *acts* is imaged, and what is not imaged cannot act. The ceiling stands where R-17-063 puts it: coarser than the smallest features, *evidence not proof*, and a sub-resolution fab adversary stays in scope. Narrowed, not removed.

**18. The bedrock assumptions, the human, and the eUICC stay outside the trusted computer.** MLWE/MSIS and ECDLP/CDH are irreducible axioms; the user is outside every consent theorem (gap 12); the eUICC remains "the one tolerated foreign computer," contained to zero platform authority but an admitted exception to the design's defining claim. None is a defect and all three are permanent: the *no foreign computers / everything verified* framing should keep saying so out loud.

That was too kind on the third count, and this edition has to be less kind to itself about the same thing: three of its predecessor's findings (the availability inventory, the seventy-seven, the refusal-to-stage) were composed wrong or counted wrong, and two of those are the exact failure they charged.*

*What is left is one finding in two places. The specification is scrupulously honest about every residual **individually**; the discipline for composing residuals into a position now exists in one direction and not the other. [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md) is the third register the previous edition asked for and it is the right instrument: it composes across **properties at a boundary**, one cell per pair, machine-checked for presence. What no artifact composes is the orthogonal direction, **across mechanisms, at a seam**. Of the two compositions that direction owed, one has been written while this edition was open: the interim isolation claim, where §18's discharge ladder is now carried up into G2 and the defended set as a stated tier with a retirement condition (gap 1), which changed nothing about the machine and everything about what a reader of §1 alone concludes. The other still stands: the fail-closed set, whose members are nine correct behaviours whose conjunction is a life-safety case (gap 9). It is paragraph-sized, engineering-free, available now, and of exactly the kind §18 already knows how to name, and gap 1 is now the evidence that such a paragraph gets written within a day of being asked for.
