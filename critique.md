# Critique: Remaining Gaps in the Architecture and Specification

*Non-normative. A gap catalogue for the current state of [verification-maximal-os.md](verification-maximal-os.md) and its companions. Rewritten after the previous edition's gaps 8, 13, 14, and 15 were answered by the atomic-requirements register and the four derived views, and revised again after four of its own entries closed while it was open. It is checked against the artifacts rather than against its own predecessor, which cost the previous edition's availability finding (closed by [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md)), its count of prose-discharged acceptance criteria (unreproducible), and its claim that the isolation substrate was left unstaged (it is staged; the staging was in the wrong section). Findings that were retired or corrected are said so in place, because a gap catalogue whose errors leave no trace is a worse instrument than the specification it audits.*

The previous edition found the mechanism-level architecture settled and the remaining gaps to be "execution, specification, scope-honesty, or human factors." That framing was too generous, and the artifacts that landed since prove it: the specification acquired the review machinery it was told it lacked, and the machinery's first act was to make the project's real position **countable**. It is worse than the prose reads. Twenty-two crown-jewel specifications are named; **one** is authored. Seven theorem targets are named; **none** can start, because each is premised on a specification that does not exist. Every hedge the design deleted was deleted on the strength of a verified primary, and **no primary is verified**.

**Numbering is fresh for this revision, and the drop from eighteen entries to nine is not nine closures.** Four closed outright, and are recorded under *Closed while this revision was open* with the residue each leaves carried into a finding rather than dropped. Three were never findings: they sat under a heading reading *irreducible*, which is an admission that they are stated ceilings rather than defects, and numbering them inflated this document's own count in the way it charges the specification with inflating claims elsewhere. They are now unnumbered, at the end. Three more were one finding written three times, and are now written once, which is what the previous revision's own preamble said was owed and then did not do. What is left is nine, and the composition that matters most about them is that **not one is a legibility problem**: the two headline findings this document has watched close were both closed by writing a paragraph, and no paragraph closes any of these.

## The dominant fact, which is no longer a finding

**Every hedge is already spent, no primary is yet verified, and the interval between is now claimed at the tier it holds.** This was entry 1 of the previous revision and it is not an entry now, because what was defective about it was **placement** rather than omission, and the placement has been fixed. It is retained here, ahead of the findings, because it remains the single most important true thing about the project's assurance position and because six of the nine findings below are read against it.

The *verify rather than hedge* rule is applied consistently and its logic is sound at the fixed point: a mechanism whose only job is to catch the failure of a formally verified primary buys little against the cost of a second modeled mechanism. On that rule the design deletes the MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, the initialization-tag plane, MTE, shadow stacks, the Harvard split, and every disjoint backstop on the machine (R-17-037, R-17-045). §17 states the hedge that remains: "the **formal verification of CHERI itself**," resting on "the **RTL ⊑ Sail** arrow … and its Coq-native restatement for the CHERI-RISC-V profile as the residual." That arrow is the layer the same document calls **the least-built layer of the stack** (R-17-039, R-18-010). The CHERI-TAL soundness metatheorem that replaces the initialization-tag plane is theorem target `CJ-TAL-SOUND`, premised on the ISA profile and **not started**. The certifying compiler, the on-device checker, the Cerise universal contract, the secure-compilation theorem: none exists.

So the rule is sound at the fixed point and its premise is false everywhere before it. On the entire trajectory to that fixed point (a multi-year interval on this roster, and the only state the project has ever occupied) the primaries are *unverified*, a hedge against an unverified primary is exactly ordinary defense in depth, and any device that executes in that interval runs with the elegance and without the argument. A single CHERI logic error in monotonicity, revocation, or the tag plane fails every isolation boundary on the machine at once, with nothing beneath it and, until `CJ-TAL-SOUND` and the Coq refinement close, no proof above it.

**The knob that turned was the interim claim, which was the only one that could turn.** Demanding retained backstops would have been asking for the wrong thing: MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, MTE, and the Harvard split are **silicon**, so "retain until the theorem closes" means fabricating them, modelling them in Sail, and paying the second-modeled-mechanism cost the rule exists to price, and R-18-002 forecloses a capability-degraded interim by name. §1 now carries the discharge tier on G2 itself (R-01-002a, R-01-002b): the software premise admits no interval, because nothing runs uncertified and a missing certificate costs delivery rather than admission; the hardware premise stands at **evidence tier** (rvfi, Sail-generated SystemVerilog under commercial FEV, the imported-core netlist audit) rather than **theorem tier** (the Kami/Kôika Coq refinement), every FPGA and early-silicon unit asserts G2 at that tier and no higher, the tier rises per core when that core's unbounded refinement is admitted and never at an intermediate rung, and an attestation or datasheet asserting G2 without its tier is a review-gate finding. §3 carries the inheritance (R-03-006). The retirement condition is R-18-010's rungs, on R-05-022's two-list discipline.

**What that bought is legibility, not safety, and the exposure is untouched.** The interval is still multi-year and the tier is still the weaker one for all of it; what changed is that a reader of §1 and §3 alone now learns so. Two consequences survive into the findings below. Whether a future datasheet honours R-01-002b is a semantic question `check.ps1` cannot reach, which is finding 3. And the tier claim is load-bearing for exactly as long as the interval lasts, which makes it the cheapest thing in this catalogue to have written and the easiest to let go stale.

## Closed while this revision was open

Three entries closed together, and they closed together because none of them worked alone: the scope statement, the seam register, and the metric were one repair split across three numbers. That is the strongest evidence available that composing them was right, and it is also the reason the residues below are worth more than the closures.

**The denial scope statement (previous entry 8).** The coverage matrix obliged availability at every boundary while §3 declined to say whether an adversary whose objective is denial was in scope at all, so nine cells were owed against no scope statement. §3 now splits it where the artifacts already put it: denial attempted *through* a boundary is **defended**, discharged by the `P-5` row and decidable against the matrix rather than against the sentence (R-03-007); denial achieved by provoking the platform's own refusals is **residual**, and residual as a composition rather than as any single mechanism (R-03-008). What makes it more than a scope label is R-03-009, which states the invariant the fail-closed posture actually holds and declines the one it does not: no such path costs confidentiality, integrity, or authority, and the platform does not promise the device stays up.

**The fail-closed seam register (previous entry 9).** §17's hardware seam register exists on an explicit finding: *"the residual pattern is that gaps live where two retained mechanisms meet, so the meetings are enumerated."* Thirteen hardware seams are named with owners; the proof seams are named; and the refusals were named nowhere together, though every one of them was booked correctly alone. The register asked for has been written, and it is R-17-017's rule turned on the other axis: a *refusal* admitted alone is not admitted until its meetings are. R-17-030a governs, and ten fail-closed seams, each stating the refusal, the meeting, and where the member is booked, sat beneath it on the day it was written: thermal trip ⋈ the work in progress, watchdog bite ⋈ restart cadence, containment ⋈ the attacked surface's duty cycle, admission refusal ⋈ delivery, the sealed cutoffs ⋈ emergency calling, the coverage floor ⋈ emergency calling, duress erase ⋈ accidental entry, the untrusted compositor and driver ⋈ the consent path, the radio stack crash ⋈ connectivity, and the background floor ⋈ degradation. Four more were added afterwards and not by reading (detector trip ⋈ continued operation, entropy health-test failure ⋈ every operation needing fresh randomness, display underrun ⋈ the trusted path, budget admission refusal ⋈ the roster), so fifteen fail-closed seams stand there now; how they were found is finding 4. R-17-030l states the composition: one outcome with three distinct causes, which booking the members separately is precisely what hid. Two things the register does that a list of the same items would not: it separates the one member whose denial cannot reach a running unit (admission refusal costs *delivery*, R-17-030e) from the members that can, so the set is not read as uniformly a field risk; and it names the **background floor** as the floor the rest degrade *toward* rather than as a peer member, because a composition read against a work-conserving machine's idea of *degraded* reads the whole set too gently.

As with the tier statement, what this bought is composition and one obligation, not a mechanism, and R-17-030l says so rather than implying otherwise. No candidate mechanism survives this design's own currency, and the entry enumerates why: a software override of a sealed cutoff deletes the property the cutoff exists for, a legacy emergency fallback reintroduces the attach surface R-12-041 removed from the *silicon*, and continuing through a thermal event reopens the channel R-15-193 deleted. A user outside 5G-SA/6G coverage still places no emergency call.

**The containment rate (previous entry 14).** R-17-035 splits containment (fast, monotone, no proof) from remediation (proof-gated) and budgets *the window*; nothing budgeted **windows per unit time**, which is the only quantity in which repeated forcing of the degraded subset is visible, and with no denial objective in scope there was no statement the accumulation could be measured against. R-03-007/008 supply the scope and R-17-030m supplies the quantity: the degraded-subset *rate* is budgeted, with entries into containment recorded as an attested countable event class rather than absorbed as containment succeeding. The `B-06`×`P-5` cell now reads `proved / detected`, which is the honest mode for it. What remains open is what the ceiling *is*: the obligation says the quantity must exist and be recorded, no requirement yet names a number, and a well-posed budget with no figure in it is materially better than an unposed one and is not the same as a closed one.

**What all three left behind was one thing, and it was finding 4.** `check.ps1` saw the new entries resolve, trace, and count, and could not see whether a member was *missing*: nothing quantified over "every mechanism whose failure action is to stop." R-17-030a asserted its own totality by inspection, on the newest artifact in the repository, on the day it was written, and it was wrong by four members. Nothing quantifies over that predicate today either, because nothing can; what runs in its place is the conferral discipline and the vocabulary agenda that found them, which is finding 4 and is the one entry here that has closed.

## What is not built

**1. Nothing load-bearing is built, and the specification is now precise enough to say how little.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the honest instrument the previous critique asked for, and its status column reads: of twenty-two crown-jewel specifications, **one is authored** (the frozen ISA profile), three are partial, **eighteen are not authored**. The seven theorem targets each depend on a row above; eighteen of those premises do not exist, so most of the proof program cannot begin rather than merely being unfinished.

The repository's own history sharpens this. As of 2026-08-14: forty days, 328 commits, roughly 2.3 MB of design documents, and, outside a single 44 KB PowerShell consistency checker, **zero lines of system code** (`git ls-files` returns exactly one non-Markdown path). The figures are dated because they are derived facts and this document is the one place in the repository that copies them by hand rather than recomputing them, which is the drift the register's own discipline (R-05-151a, and every count `check.ps1` rebuilds) exists to prevent, reappearing in its critic. Read them as a snapshot with a direction, and recompute before citing.

The project's only demonstrated velocity is in producing obligations. That is a defensible way to spend an early phase, and §18 and R-01-003 are candid that the as-existing assurance is low. What is *not* stated anywhere is the ratio's direction: the obligation count grows monotonically and the discharge count is one. Every hardening that made the design safer added a crown jewel, and nothing in the process retires one except authoring it, which is why the inventory's real contribution is spent only if the status column is run as a **burn-down with a stated direction** rather than as a census that can only grow.

**2. The five day-one deliverables are the project's only fair scorecard, and the highest-value one is missing.** R-18-003b names five things that "gate on nothing" and need no engineering: the profile freeze and its Sail curation; the microarchitectural absence contract; the **machine-checked statement of T and the seam lemmas**; the atomic-requirements register; and the proof-artifact hygiene gates (assumption gating and the non-vacuity witness, each a predicate over whatever proof artifact exists, so both are wired ahead of the development they will police). Three exist as artifacts in the repository and the fourth is stated as a predicate with nothing yet to police. The one that does not exist in any form is the one R-18-032 itself calls "the higher-value half" and "**the review artifact the crown-jewel gate most needs**", and it is crown-jewel row 1, status *not authored*.

§5 contains T in prose, and that prose is good. But the deliverable is the machine-checked statement with its four elements, the seam interfaces aligned so each conclusion types as the next's premise, doubling as the coverage checklist. Until it exists, the seam list is a list of *names*, and the claim that the seams' conclusions and premises meet is unchecked in exactly the way §17 says a chain of individually-sound proofs can fail. This is the composition that matters, and unlike the two that closed this month it is a proof program rather than a paragraph, which is the whole reason it is still open.

## The review gate audits its own reflection

**3. The gate is referential and the risk is semantic, and this is one finding with three faces rather than the three entries the previous revision made of it.** §5's release gate exists because "a proof against a wrong spec verifies perfectly," and R-18-034 states plainly that the gate "audits the register rather than this prose," so an unverified extraction stands between the reviewer and the design of record and is declared the authoritative object. That an automated checker cannot decide whether a statement is the *right* statement is a property of checkers and not a defect anyone can repair; charging it as a gap, as the previous revision did three times, is the category error this entry replaces. What is chargeable is the residue, and there are exactly three pieces of it.

**The instrument is strong and is the wrong shape, which is a fact to build on rather than to complain about.** `check.ps1` checks **referential integrity**: that cited bookmarks resolve, that requirements carry traces, that no view drops a requirement in its bearing subsections, that no asserted count disagrees with its artifact, that no document carries encoding damage. Its reach includes this document: every count asserted here is anchored in the checker's registry and recomputed, so an edition of this critique cannot quietly disagree with the artifacts about a number, and the figures in finding 1 are stale only because no claim holds them. A requirement can nonetheless resolve perfectly, trace perfectly, appear in every view, count correctly, and state the wrong thing.

**Face one: the review budget is spent partly on the document rather than on the machine, and it wants a tag.** A class of acceptance criteria is discharged by inspecting the prose rather than the artifact: *"the axiom is stated in §6 and §17, not implied"* (R-06-014); *"the decision is stated as a design choice with its consequence, not omitted"* (R-10-011); *"the consent TCB is named in §6 rather than hidden in userland"* (R-06-018); *"the split is stated rather than blurred"* (R-13-023); *"the cost is booked rather than omitted"* (R-12-020); *"the trade is stated in §15, §12, and here"* (R-17-051). The size of the class depends entirely on where the line is drawn, and no single number is defensible. Matching only criteria of the quoted shape (the test being that the prose says a thing, in those words) yields **seven of 1152 acceptance criteria**. Widening to any criterion whose test uses *stated*, *booked*, *named*, *recorded*, or *declared* without naming an artifact or `check.ps1` yields **116**; narrowing that to the ones citing a `§` as the thing to open yields **25**. A single figure quoted without its rule (an earlier edition of this document asserted seventy-seven, ~8%, and no rule produces it) is the same defect as the one being charged, so the honest statement is the band and the method: **7 strictly, 25 on the reading most people mean, 116 at the loosest**, each reproducible against the register, all three a snapshot and a lower bound, since the amendment recorded above added entries of exactly this class. These entries are **not** defects under R-05-153, which is where an earlier revision mis-stated this: that rule catches a claim that cannot be restated as an atomic, testable requirement, and "the consent TCB is named in §6" is atomic and is testable by inspection, which is a test. The defect is one layer over, in R-05-152 making the register the audited object: a requirement whose acceptance criterion is a fact about the commentary spends the gate's scarcest resource, a reviewer's attention on the one pass that decides whether the specification says the right thing, on editorial hygiene. The repair is a **tag rather than a defect entry**: mark the spec-hygiene entries as such, so the review can price them instead of discovering them one at a time.

**Face two: the extraction-defect list opened on the wrong axis and its own stated class is still empty.** The list is no longer empty: it books `D-CSR`, one defect with six rows, and the register is right that "an empty list was not the same as a swept one." But look at what opened it. All six rows are one class (surviving CSRs that [isa-profile.md](isa-profile.md) §5 had to enumerate and that no requirement decides), and the instrument that found them was building a derived view and machine-checking it, not re-reading the prose. **R-05-153's actual class, a normative claim that would not decompose, still has zero entries**: not one of the 1152 requirements has yet been booked as standing for a passage that resisted restatement, and face one's entries are not the missing ones, because they decomposed fine, into requirements about the document. So the two readings stand. Either the prose was already atomic throughout (implausible for a document whose §17 entries routinely run five clauses deep with three parenthetical qualifications) or the extraction **accommodated** the prose where it should have confronted it. The register does not claim otherwise: its standing instruction is that sweep 2's question "has not been asked exhaustively, so further instances should be assumed present rather than absent." The channel that reports what the *prose* refused to decompose is still not running, and a one-item agenda drawn from a single enumeration means the meeting R-18-035 calls the review gate's agenda has barely started.

**Face three: the derived views can be wrong in the half nothing checks, and there are more of them every month.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is declared "Normative as a **view**," and on the day it was written it carried two internal contradictions about which of its own rows were the closure defect. Both were repaired, and the *quantitative* half of the class is now genuinely closed the only way such things close: `check.ps1` parses the status column into its three classes, recomputes the ratio, and fails where a document asserts a figure the artifact does not bear. What is untouched is the qualitative half, which is most of it. Whether a row's status is *honest* (that `partial` is not doing work `not authored` should do), whether a cited requirement is the *right* ground, whether a discharge mode in [coverage-matrix.md](coverage-matrix.md) reports what its cited requirements actually carry: invisible by construction. Every artifact this document has watched arrive widens that surface, each one machine-checked for **presence** and none for **truth**, and a view is normative whether or not it has been read. One item has come off that list since, and how it came off is the only lever this finding has: *whether R-17-030a's seams are the right ones* was not answered, it was **replaced** by a mechanical question it implies, whether every requirement using the vocabulary of refusal is conferred or dispositioned (finding 4). That substitution is available wherever a qualitative claim has a lexical shadow it can be held against, and it is not available for most of the list above.

**4. The repair transferred, the convention it replaced was wrong in fact, and this entry was wrong about what the repair buys.** This was the standing finding, nominated below as the one to keep. It is the only entry in this document that has closed in the half that was closable, and it is recorded here rather than under the closures above because what it produced is worth more than the closure.

R-17-016 closes the crown-jewel list by conferral: membership is asserted by each requirement that has it, [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the one place the conferrals are collected, and `check.ps1` fails on a conferral with no row or a row with no requirement. This entry called that closure by construction against two closures by convention, and doubted the transfer, on the ground that *freshness-critical* and *fails closed* are judgments no checker can grep for. The ground was right and the conclusion did not follow. R-17-030r and R-10-013a now carry the identical rule: a requirement specifying a mechanism whose failure action is to stop records the refusal against itself on a `· Fail-closed:` property line, a requirement placing state under the monotonic counter records it on a `· RoT-fresh:` line, the R-17-030 seams and R-10-013 collect them, and the checker fails on a conferral no collection carries and on a seam standing under no conferral. Twenty conferrals against fifteen seams, four against one enumeration, both directions decidable. What no checker can grep for is not membership; it is the *predicate*, and conferral never needed to grep for it, which is the thing this entry did not see.

**The machinery is not the result. The convention was wrong, and it was wrong exactly where it was most expensive.** Against the completeness residue the checker carries the only instrument available to it: the vocabulary of refusal is over-approximated across every requirement body, and each entry it catches must confer, be collected, or be dispositioned by name with a reason. Run once against the ten-seam register it returned four members the register did not carry. One of them is now the sharpest member in it. **Every fault detector on this machine answers detection by stopping** (uncorrectable ECC, tag integrity, the control-flow signature on the three protected sequences, the sentinel's lockstep comparator, residual synchronizer failure), each of them right to stop, and the adversary who provokes them is the radiated-EMI and electromagnetic-fault-injection adversary §3 admits by name and §15 shields against. A probe held near the enclosure stops a device that is behaving correctly, needs no software access at all, and gives R-17-030l's life-safety composition a fourth cause it did not have: a user who cannot place an emergency call because a detector caught an injected fault. The other three are the entropy health test, whose sole failure action is fail-stop and whose meeting is every operation needing fresh randomness; the display path, whose visible blank is a caught fail-stop on the surface the consent prompt is drawn on, reaching denial of the prompt from a direction the compositor seam does not cover; and budget admission refusal, which also cost R-17-030e its claim to be the only member whose denial cannot reach a running unit.

**This entry was also wrong about the crown-jewel construction, and the correction is the general statement.** Conferral closes completeness for none of the three sets. A specification that ought to confer crown-jewel status and does not is exactly as invisible as a refusal that ought to confer and does not; what conferral closes is the *collection's disagreement with the requirements*, which is a smaller property that this entry credited as the whole one and then measured the other two against. Both registers now claim the decidable half in their own text and decline the other, which is the only honest form of the claim and was not available while this entry was mis-stating the model.

**What survives is the residue, stated in its own shape.** The vocabulary agenda is lexical, proves no totality, and is defeated by an author who rewords rather than decides. The dispositions are judgments recorded in a tool. Neither is a quantifier over the semantic predicate, because there is none to build. The finding this entry should have made, and makes now that it is closing, is narrower and more useful than the one it made: **a membership claim is decidable against its own members and undecidable against the world, so the only thing worth building is the agenda that forces a person to decide, on every run, for every candidate the vocabulary can reach.** Twenty-five candidates were dispositioned to close this. The thirteen on the RoT-fresh side all resolved the same way, that they enforce the floor or provide the counter and place no state under it, which is the first evidence anywhere in this repository that an enumeration closed by convention was right.

## Deletion has no opposing artifact

**5. The first release has no stated minimum viable capability, so deletion is unfalsifiable.** Assemble what §18 commits to for the first parts: **no browser**, deferred as the largest porting program; **1–2 GB** of planar single-tier SRAM; six C-class cores with software rendering at initial bring-up; no JIT anywhere; no fixed-function GPU; a few-active-things machine where the foreground is fast and the background is "very nearly stopped."

Nowhere does the specification state what this device **does for a user**. There is no requirement naming a target workload the first release must serve, no floor on the roster, no acceptance criterion of the form *this configuration runs this set of things at this quality*. [userspace-porting.md](userspace-porting.md) is the closest thing and declines the job in its own words: the applications are "the **elective** applications" and the roster is "elective and stageable," a sequencing document by design, so nothing on it can fail to arrive. [performance-estimates.md](performance-estimates.md) scores deltas against an out-of-order baseline and reports the honest matrix by archetype; it does not answer whether the resulting absolute is a product.

That omission is what makes *delete-rather-than-defend* structurally unfalsifiable. Every deletion is scored on the axis it improves (one fewer Sail obligation, one fewer block on the least-built arrow, one fewer trust anchor), and the ledger runs only that way, because the axis that would push back (does the surviving machine still do the job?) has no requirement to be violated and no artifact to run. Twenty-plus deletions are each booked as a win at specification time, and not one of them can be contradicted until silicon exists. A design method with no possible negative result is not being tested by its own process; it is being narrated by it. No register closes this one, because the missing artifact is a product floor and writing one is a decision rather than a composition.

## The theorem tier covers the hardware nobody has written

**6. Single-prover purity holds exactly where nothing exists, and is abandoned where artifacts do.** The Kami/Kôika Coq refinement is "the only unbounded vehicle" (R-17-039) and is authored **for the net-new blocks that have no legacy RTL**: the capability/tag-carrying DMA fabric, the TDM NoC, the fixed-function sequencers. Those blocks do not exist. The cores that *do* exist (CHERI-CVA6, Ara, Gemmini) close by riding "Sail-generated SystemVerilog plus commercial FEV," which R-17-039 concedes "trusts the equivalence tool," or by a deferred proof over shipped SystemVerilog conditioned on "**if** a foundational Verilog-semantics account matures."

The absence contract has the identical shape: Kôika blocks close in-prover as a predicate over the Coq term; imported cores close on "a structural **audit, not a theorem**." [absence-contract.md](absence-contract.md) states this plainly and honestly, and states that day one *is* the imported-core half, "which is exactly the half that closes on audit."

The composed statement is uncomfortable and is nowhere made: **the strongest claims this design makes about the processor it will actually run on rest on a commercial tool's say-so and a netlist search, while the Coq tier (the whole argument for the single-prover discipline) covers only hardware that has not been designed.** That is not dishonest; both halves are booked. It is unassembled, and assembled it says the trust story for the CPU is evidence-tier for the foreseeable future. The absence contract's day-one availability, which §18 celebrates, is availability of the *weaker* discharge form. The tier closure above assembles one consequence of this and not the finding itself: R-01-002a names the evidence-tier vehicles where G2 is claimed, so the *isolation* claim carries its tier, while the observation here, that the single-prover discipline's own tier covers only blocks nobody has designed, is stated in neither §17 nor the absence contract. The tier is now carried by the claim that rides the hardware; it is not yet carried by the argument for the discipline that was supposed to underwrite it.

## Booked but open

**7. One word is doing two jobs on the consent path, and the mechanism work is finished.** §8/§12 close the mechanism side completely: robust delimited declassification, RoT secure-attention indicator, RoT-latched input ownership for the prompt's duration, a ceiling-bounded lease in place of an unevaluable *while-active*. That work is excellent and it is finished, and this entry asks for no mechanism, because the obvious mechanisms are the wrong trade. Grant rate-limiting, consent-pattern anomaly detection, and habituation heuristics are policy judgments with no specification to be proven against: each would be a new oracle whose wrongness no proof catches, added to the one path §6 keeps smallest, deciding on the user's behalf by heuristic what the design elsewhere refuses to decide without a theorem. R-17-013c already books the substance honestly, recording that the ceiling is exercised *at machine rate* once an autonomous agent is the principal doing the asking, with standing grants relieving the fatigue only by widening scope, and its acceptance criterion is that "no mechanism is claimed to decide the fatigue-versus-scope trade on the user's behalf," which is the right refusal stated in the right place.

What is left is a **vocabulary defect, not a design gap**, and it is worth one entry because of what it costs a reader. R-17-013 files "the user consented to the wrong thing" as the irreducible ceiling, and that name covers two claims. *The user did not understand what they authorized* is genuinely outside any theorem and always will be. *A correctly-rendered, genuinely-attested prompt can be issued often enough, or shaped well enough, that a competent user authorizes something they would refuse on reflection* is a different claim, and it is unaddressed rather than irreducible: a repository-wide search returns nothing on grant rate-limiting, grant-pattern anomaly detection, consent habituation, or comprehension scaffolding, and the only rate-limiter in the corpus guards unlock attempts (R-09-018, R-12-017), never grants. The design defends duress credentials, biometric vaults, and seized-device forensics, a threat model that presumes a high-risk user, and social-engineering an unspoofable prompt is precisely how such users are compromised. **Split R-17-013** into the comprehension ceiling (irreducible, keep saying so) and the abuse-resistance ceiling (open, with the argument above for why no admissible mechanism closes it), so that *unspoofable* stops being quoted where *abuse-resistant* is what a reader wants.

**8. Duress books its own failure modes and not the dynamic that creates them.** R-17-054(3) is more honest than the previous revision of this document credited: it books the countermeasure as "not a guarantee," books the already-extracted-keys case, books the **image-first-compel-second** case by name, and books irreversibility on accidental entry, and R-17-030h now carries the seam, stating that the erase is irreversible on the wrong side of a human protocol this design does not model. What none of them books is the dynamic a publicly specified duress feature creates: an adversary who knows the feature exists compels *both* credentials, or watches for the erase and escalates against the person rather than the device. The mechanism's real protection is that human protocol, the document names it as unmodeled without saying what it costs when it fails, and the erase is irreversible on exactly the side where the coercion continues. The entry is narrower than it was and it is not closed, because overclaiming this feature endangers the user who relies on it most.

**9. Physical and sub-partition side channels grow more load-bearing as the device model grows more adversarial.** Probe-level power/EM, TEMPEST coupling, RF fingerprinting against link-layer unlinkability, macro-internal and inter-tier coupling, and the event-driven sensor readout's data-dependent timing are all booked (R-17-003b, R-17-055, R-17-058/059) and none is closed. The *seized-and-once-unlocked* and *duress* cases put a physical adversary in scope **by design**, which raises the weight of exactly these residuals. R-17-059 states the sharpest version itself: "the design has **no defence-in-depth layer beneath the package boundary** on the memory path … and if it is ever judged wrong there is no second mechanism behind it." That is the dominant fact again, at the physical layer, stated by the document against itself, and unlike the isolation tier it has no ladder, because a package boundary admits no staged discharge form.

One of these has now been stated in the form this entry asks for, and it is worth naming as the pattern rather than as a repair: R-17-058a says the power/EM position plainly, in §17 and in the register. The crypto core carries no masking; resistance to a probing adversary is not claimed; the constant-time apparatus is architectural and says nothing about the analog observation; masking with a machine-checked probing-model proof is named as the only construction-grade answer, with its cost in randomness, area, and a leakage model that is itself an assumption; and the four things that would reverse the decision are enumerated, so the residual is falsifiable rather than perpetual. The `B-01`×`P-6` cell reads the same way now, carrying the architectural half and booking the analog half instead of covering the pair with one mode. What is unchanged is the ceiling: none of the other residuals here has had that paragraph written for it, and each still reads stronger in summary than in construction.

## Not gaps: the ceilings this document keeps naming

These were numbered in the previous revision under a heading that called them *irreducible*, which conceded they were not defects. They are stated correctly, they are permanent or nearly so, and a catalogue that numbers them is padding its own count.

- **Specification error is first-ranked, and is the reason the inventory exists.** Twenty-two crown jewels, each a small oracle that must be *stated* right and whose wrongness no proof catches: the delimited-release bound, the ASN.1/UPER codec, the dedup keyed-digest interface, the HAL's DMA postconditions, the IDL initialization annotations, the WCET latency magnitudes, the `Zkt`/`Zvkt` leakage model, the Ztso and static-prediction fetch models, every wire-format mapping, each Lustre program. Unchanged in kind, larger in extent, and now measurable, which is the whole point of having counted them. The one actionable part of this is not a ceiling and has moved to finding 1: measure it as a burn-down.
- **Fabrication trust is evidence-based, not proof-based.** IRIS backside inspection makes fab an obtainable-evidence question rather than an act of faith, and the tier grading is a genuinely strong position: everything that *acts* is imaged, and what is not imaged cannot act. The ceiling stands where R-17-063 puts it: coarser than the smallest features, *evidence not proof*, and a sub-resolution fab adversary stays in scope. Narrowed, not removed.
- **The bedrock assumptions, the human, and the eUICC stay outside the trusted computer.** MLWE/MSIS and ECDLP/CDH are irreducible axioms; the user is outside every consent theorem (finding 7); the eUICC remains "the one tolerated foreign computer," contained to zero platform authority but an admitted exception to the design's defining claim. All three are permanent, and the *no foreign computers / everything verified* framing should keep saying so out loud.
