# Critique: Remaining Gaps in the Architecture and Specification

*Non-normative. A gap catalogue for the current state of [verification-maximal-os.md](verification-maximal-os.md) and its companions. Rewritten after the previous edition's gaps 8, 13, 14, and 15 were answered by the atomic-requirements register and the four derived views, and after the artifact roughly doubled again.*

The previous edition found the mechanism-level architecture settled and the remaining gaps to be "execution, specification, scope-honesty, or human factors." That framing was too generous, and the artifacts that landed since prove it: the specification acquired the review machinery it was told it lacked, and the machinery's first act was to make the project's real position **countable**. It is worse than the prose reads. Twenty-three crown-jewel specifications are named; **one** is authored. Seven theorem targets are named; **none** can start, because each is premised on a specification that does not exist. Every hedge the design deleted was deleted on the strength of a verified primary, and **no primary is verified**.

Gaps are numbered fresh and sorted by kind, not severity. The most important (1) is that the design has already spent its defense-in-depth and has not yet bought the proofs it spent it on.

## Retired since the last edition

Stated briefly, because the closures were real and the rest of this document is not generous.

- **Protocol-level security** is now in §3's Defended/Residual split by name, as "the largest scope boundary the word *hyper-secure* crosses", the previous critique's exact complaint, adopted verbatim.
- **The end-to-end composition obligation** is now a first-class §17 residual (R-17-064/065) and the §18 capstone workstream (R-18-031/032), with its statement, its seam interfaces, and its boundary ledger (*D*, *Ax*) named as deliverables.
- **The review artifact** exists: [requirements-register.md](requirements-register.md), 1007 numbered requirements with acceptance criteria and traces, plus four derived views and `tools/check.ps1`. §5's gate now has a subject.
- **The RoT-fresh state set** is normatively enumerated and closed (R-10-013), against the previous edition's complaint that it was left to per-feature judgment.
- **The CHERI single-mechanism concentration** is now a substantial §17 entry (R-17-037) stating the four-part trade and naming *verify rather than hedge* as the posture, rather than a residual footnote.

Four of these landed within twenty-four hours of the critique that asked for them. That responsiveness is the strongest evidence in the repository that the specification workstream is real, and it is also the source of gap 5.

## The dominant fact

**1. Every hedge is already spent; no primary is yet verified; and the design has no name for the interval between.** This is the deepest error in the document and it is a *timing* error, not a reasoning error.

The *verify rather than hedge* rule is applied consistently and its logic is sound at the fixed point: a mechanism whose only job is to catch the failure of a formally verified primary buys little against the cost of a second modeled mechanism. On that rule the design deletes the MMU, PMP, the S/U rings, the IOMMU, the initialization-tag plane, MTE, shadow stacks, the Harvard split, and every disjoint backstop on the machine (R-17-037, R-17-045). §17 states the hedge that remains: "the **formal verification of CHERI itself**," resting on "the **RTL ⊑ Sail** arrow … and its Coq-native restatement for the CHERI-RISC-V profile as the residual."

That arrow is the layer the same document calls **the least-built layer of the stack** (R-17-039, R-18-010). The CHERI-TAL soundness metatheorem that replaces the initialization-tag plane is theorem target `CJ-TAL-SOUND`, premised on the ISA profile and **not started**. The certifying compiler, the on-device checker, the Cerise universal contract, the secure-compilation theorem: none exists.

So the rule is being applied at the one moment it is invalid. At the fixed point, hedges against verified primaries are waste. On the entire trajectory to that fixed point (which on this roster is a multi-year interval, and which is the only state the project has ever occupied or will occupy for a long time) the primaries are *unverified*, and a hedge against an unverified primary is exactly ordinary defense in depth. The design has taken delivery of the elegance now and deferred the proof that pays for it, and **G2 ("compromising any component yields only its explicitly granted authority") does not hold in the interim state and the document never says so.** A single CHERI logic error in monotonicity, revocation, or the tag plane fails every isolation boundary on the machine at once, today, with nothing beneath it and no proof above it.

The honest form is a **staged posture**: which backstops are retained until which theorem closes, and what the platform claims before then. The spec has exactly the machinery to state this: it stages the die class by class, stages storage, stages radio, stages WCET behind the timing-annotated model, and books *graceful degradation* for RTL ⊑ Sail (R-18-013), and it applies none of it to the isolation substrate, where the interim claim is simply asserted at full strength. That is the one place staging was refused, and it is the place it mattered most.

**2. Nothing load-bearing is built, and the specification is now precise enough to say how little.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the honest instrument the previous critique asked for, and its status column reads: of twenty-three crown-jewel specifications, **one is authored** (the frozen ISA profile), three are partial, **nineteen are not authored**. The seven theorem targets each depend on a row above; nineteen of those premises do not exist, so most of the proof program cannot begin rather than merely being unfinished.

The repository's own history sharpens this. Thirty-two days, 277 commits, roughly 1.9 MB of design documents, and, outside a single 24 KB PowerShell consistency checker, **zero lines of system code**. The project's only demonstrated velocity is in producing obligations. That is a defensible way to spend an early phase, and §18 and R-01-003 are candid that the as-existing assurance is low. What is *not* stated anywhere is the ratio's direction: the obligation count is growing monotonically and the discharge count is one. Every hardening that made the design safer added a crown jewel; nothing in the process retires one except authoring it.

**3. The four day-one deliverables are the project's only fair scorecard, and the highest-value one is missing.** §18 names four things that "gate on nothing" and need no engineering: the profile freeze and Sail curation; the microarchitectural absence contract; the **machine-checked statement of T and the seam lemmas**; and the atomic-requirements register. Three are done or nearly so. The one that is not is the one R-18-032 itself calls "the higher-value half" and "**the review artifact the crown-jewel gate most needs**", and it is crown-jewel row 1, status *not authored*.

§5 contains T in prose, and that prose is good. But the deliverable is the machine-checked statement with its four elements, the seam interfaces aligned so each conclusion types as the next's premise, doubling as the coverage checklist. Until it exists, the seam list is a list of *names*, and the claim that the seams' conclusions and premises meet is unchecked in exactly the way §17 says a chain of individually-sound proofs can fail. The composition obligation was booked (retired gap) and then not started, which is a smaller victory than it looked.

## The review gate audits its own reflection

**4. The gate that guards the first-ranked risk cannot see the first-ranked risk.** §5's release gate exists because "a proof against a wrong spec verifies perfectly." R-18-034 then states plainly that the gate "audits the register rather than this prose." So an unverified extraction now stands between the reviewer and the design of record, and is declared the authoritative object.

Nothing checks that the extraction is faithful. `check.ps1` checks **referential integrity**: that cited bookmarks resolve, that requirements carry traces, that no view drops a requirement in its bearing subsections, that no asserted count disagrees with its artifact. A fourth group checks punctuation and encoding damage, which is the same shape one layer further down. Those are good checks and they are the wrong shape for this risk: a requirement can resolve perfectly, trace perfectly, appear in every view, count correctly, and state the wrong thing. The tooling produces exactly the reassurance the gate must not have.

Worse, the gate is partly satisfiable by reading the document back to itself. **Seventy-seven of 1007 acceptance criteria** (~8%) are discharged by inspecting the prose rather than the artifact: *"the axiom is stated in §6 and §17, not implied"*; *"the decision is stated as a design choice with its consequence, not omitted"*; *"the consent TCB is named in §6 rather than hidden in userland"*; *"the split is stated rather than blurred."* Each of these is a real editorial virtue and none is a testable property of the system. R-05-153 is unambiguous that "a normative claim that cannot be restated as an atomic, testable requirement is treated as a spec defect", so by the register's own rule, tens of its own entries are defects, and the defect list does not carry them.

**5. The extraction-defect list opened on the wrong axis, and what it still does not carry is the finding.** The list is no longer empty: it books `D-CSR`, one defect with seven rows, and the register is right that "an empty list was not the same as a swept one."

But look at what opened it. All seven rows are one class (surviving CSRs that [isa-profile.md](isa-profile.md) §5 had to enumerate and that no requirement decides), and the instrument that found them was building a derived view and machine-checking it, not re-reading the prose. Not one of the 1007 requirements has yet been booked as a passage that would not decompose. R-05-153's actual class, "a normative claim that cannot be restated as an atomic, testable requirement," still has zero entries, while gap 4 identifies seventy-seven acceptance criteria that meet its definition on inspection. Those are the entries the list should carry, and the register concedes as much in the same breath it declines to book them.

So the two readings stand. Either the prose was already atomic throughout (implausible for a document whose §17 entries routinely run five clauses deep with three parenthetical qualifications) or the extraction **accommodated** the prose where it should have confronted it, restating hard passages as requirements whose acceptance criteria are the passages themselves (gap 4 is the mechanism). The sweeps that produced `D-CSR` are a real instrument aimed at referential integrity, and the channel that matters more, the one that reports what the *prose* refused to decompose, is still not running. That list should be expected to be long and should be published long. It is the agenda R-18-035 says it is, and a one-item agenda drawn from a single enumeration means the meeting has barely started.

**6. The derived views can contradict themselves and nothing in the toolchain would know.** [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is declared "Normative as a **view**," and on the day it was written it carried two internal contradictions about which of its own rows were the closure defect: a heading naming five rows over a sentence substantiating four, and a bullet refuting itself mid-clause. Both were the kind of thing review catches, both sat in a *normative* artifact committed the same day the corpus was reported clean, and the machinery reported it clean anyway.

They have since been repaired, and the repair is instructive rather than reassuring: what fixed them was removing the roll-call they disagreed about (gap 7), not a check that could see the disagreement. The class is untouched. Every semantic claim in every view (that a row's status is honest, that a ground is the right ground, that a note describes the requirement it cites) is invisible to `check.ps1` by construction, because the checker is referential and these are not referential defects. The review that could see them still has not run, and a view is normative whether or not it has been read.

**7. "Closed by amendment" is the design's standard closure mechanism, and it holds in exactly one of its two instances, the one where it stopped being a convention.** R-17-016 closes the crown-jewel list by amendment, and that closure originally failed: specifications acquired crown-jewel status through requirements outside the enumeration, and three disagreeing sources arose. The repair was not a better-maintained list but the deletion of the list: membership is conferred by each requirement that asserts it, [crown-jewels.md](crown-jewels.md) is the one place the conferrals are collected, and `check.ps1` fails on a conferral with no row or a row with no requirement. Closure by amendment became closure by construction, and it is the strongest instance in the repository of the register discipline paying for itself.

R-10-013 then closes the RoT-freshness set by the *unrepaired* mechanism, and its acceptance criterion asserts the four attacks the set blocks without arguing that the map from attack to counter-protected state is total. The previous critique asked for that set to be "enumerated normatively and argued closed." Half arrived. The enumeration is real; the closure argument is still the social convention, and the pattern the crown-jewel case establishes, that a membership claim survives only where something mechanical quantifies over it, is exactly what has not been applied here. It is also not obvious that it can be: freshness-criticality is a judgment about attacks, not a phrase a checker can grep for, which makes this the harder half and the one still open.

## Availability is currency, never an obligation

**8. Twelve register entries mention availability; eleven spend it and one defends a watchdog.** This is not a rhetorical characterization: it is the complete inventory. R-10-021 and R-12-024/025/029 place block services below the integrity line, "trusted only for availability." R-15-127 bounds calibration compromise so it "degrade[s] performance and availability, never integrity." R-15-138 and R-17-053 book the frozen canceller as "a link-availability cost, not an integrity one." R-17-033 makes certifier failure "an availability failure, never a safety breach." R-17-057 makes trusted-time denial "availability, not integrity." R-18-027 and R-18-030 stage it away.

The single exception proves the shape of the gap. R-11-007 ships "**monitor** availability and watchdog-before-deadline" as theorems, a schedulability result guaranteeing that the *sentinel* gets to run, i.e. that the fail-closed machinery itself is live. It is an availability obligation owed to the containment path, not to the user. Nothing anywhere obliges the platform to keep *serving* under attack.

In every other case availability is the coin some other property is bought with. **No requirement makes availability a property the platform owes its user.** It is absent from §3's Defended list and absent from §3's Residual list, the only first-class security property appearing in neither. The threat model claims hostile radio infrastructure as a defended adversary, and that adversary's actual objective is very often to keep a device off the network or off a call. Against precisely that goal the specification is silent by construction.

**9. The fail-closed set has never been composed, and the design has a discipline for exactly this failure that it did not apply here.** §17's hardware seam register exists on an explicit finding: *"the residual pattern is that gaps live where two retained mechanisms meet, so the meetings are enumerated."* Thirteen hardware seams are named with owners. The proof seams are named. There is **no availability seam register**, and the components are all booked individually:

thermal trip is fail-stop (§15) · watchdog bite is reset (R-16-005) · admission failure refuses code (§13) · a certifier that cannot emit a derivation refuses a safe program (R-17-033) · a radio crash costs connectivity until restart (R-16-002) · a driver fighting the consent transition denies the prompt (R-12-082-2) · a compromised compositor's best play is premature revocation (§8, §12) · containment drops the attacked surface to a safe subset for machine-hours-to-days (R-17-035) · duress crypto-erases irreversibly (R-17-054) · the population wall gives a background origin **on the order of one percent of one core** (R-17-005) · emergency calling requires 5G-SA/6G coverage and nothing else (R-17-052) · the sealed cutoffs are **not** overridden for emergencies, by design (R-17-052).

Each of these is individually correct and individually honest. Composed, they describe a device that an adversary who can provoke faults, thermal events, or crash-restart loops can degrade or silence at will, and whose worst case is a **life-safety** one: a user who cannot place an emergency call because the platform fail-stopped, or fell back to a coverage floor it does not have, or has a physical switch closed that no software may reopen. §17 books the last of these as an accepted residual and is right that a software override is worthless. It does not compose it with the other eleven. The document's own rule, *a mechanism admitted alone is not admitted until its meetings are*, is the missing instrument, and applying it to the fail-closed set is a day-one, engineering-free deliverable of exactly the kind §18 already knows how to name.

**10. The first release has no stated minimum viable capability, so deletion is unfalsifiable.** Assemble what §18 commits to for the first parts: Wi-Fi only, no cellular, no eUICC; **no browser**, deferred as the largest porting program; **1–2 GB** of planar single-tier SRAM; six C-class cores with software rendering at initial bring-up; no JIT anywhere; no fixed-function GPU; a few-active-things machine where the foreground is fast and the background is "very nearly stopped."

Nowhere does the specification state what this device **does for a user**. There is no requirement naming a target workload the first release must serve, no floor on the roster, no acceptance criterion of the form *this configuration runs this set of things at this quality*. [performance-estimates.md](performance-estimates.md) scores deltas against an out-of-order baseline; it does not answer whether the resulting absolute is a product.

That omission is what makes *delete-rather-than-defend* structurally unfalsifiable. Every deletion is scored on the axis it improves (one fewer Sail obligation, one fewer block on the least-built arrow, one fewer trust anchor), and the ledger runs only that way, because the axis that would push back (does the surviving machine still do the job?) has no requirement to be violated and no artifact to run. Twenty-plus deletions are each booked as a win at specification time, and not one of them can be contradicted until silicon exists. A design method with no possible negative result is not being tested by its own process; it is being narrated by it.

## The theorem tier covers the hardware nobody has written

**11. Single-prover purity holds exactly where nothing exists, and is abandoned where artifacts do.** The Kami/Kôika Coq refinement is "the only unbounded vehicle" (R-17-039) and is authored **for the net-new blocks that have no legacy RTL**: the capability/tag-carrying DMA fabric, the TDM NoC, the fixed-function sequencers. Those blocks do not exist. The cores that *do* exist (CHERI-CVA6, Ara, Gemmini) close by riding "Sail-generated SystemVerilog plus commercial FEV," which R-17-039 concedes "trusts the equivalence tool," or by a deferred proof over shipped SystemVerilog conditioned on "**if** a foundational Verilog-semantics account matures."

The absence contract has the identical shape: Kôika blocks close in-prover as a predicate over the Coq term; imported cores close on "a structural **audit, not a theorem**." [absence-contract.md](absence-contract.md) states this plainly and honestly, and states that day one *is* the imported-core half, "which is exactly the half that closes on audit."

The composed statement is uncomfortable and is nowhere made: **the strongest claims this design makes about the processor it will actually run on rest on a commercial tool's say-so and a netlist search, while the Coq tier (the whole argument for the single-prover discipline) covers only hardware that has not been designed.** That is not dishonest; both halves are booked. It is unassembled, and assembled it says the trust story for the CPU is evidence-tier for the foreseeable future. The absence contract's day-one availability, which §18 celebrates, is availability of the *weaker* discharge form.

## Booked but open

**12. Consent comprehension remains outside every theorem, and nothing has been built below the prompt.** §8/§12 close the mechanism side completely: robust delimited declassification, RoT secure-attention indicator, RoT-latched input ownership for the prompt's duration, a ceiling-bounded lease in place of an unevaluable *while-active*. That work is excellent and it is finished. R-17-013 then files "the user consented to the wrong thing" as the irreducible ceiling.

It is not irreducible; it is unaddressed. R-17-013c names the closest thing to the problem, booking that the ceiling is exercised *at machine rate* once an autonomous agent is the principal doing the asking, with the human adjudicator as the scarce resource and no mechanism claimed to decide the fatigue-versus-scope trade on the user's behalf. That states the gap rather than closing it: a repository-wide search for grant rate-limiting, grant-pattern anomaly detection, consent habituation, or comprehension scaffolding still returns **nothing** across 1.9 MB, the only rate-limiter in the corpus guarding unlock attempts rather than grants. The design defends duress credentials, biometric vaults, and seized-device forensics (a threat model that presumes a high-risk user), and social-engineering a correctly-rendered, genuinely-attested prompt is precisely how such users are compromised. A flurry of individually-valid consents is not modeled as suspicious anywhere. *Unspoofable* the spec nails; *abuse-resistant* it does not attempt, and calling the difference irreducible converts a design gap into a ceiling.

**13. Duress is a countermeasure whose principal risk is its own existence.** R-17-054(3) books irreversibility on accidental entry and books the already-extracted-keys case. It does not book the coercion dynamic: a duress feature that is publicly specified invites an adversary to compel *both* credentials, or to image first and compel second, or simply to watch for the erase and escalate. Its real protection is a human protocol the document does not model, and the erase is irreversible on the wrong side of that protocol. Overclaiming it endangers the user who relies on it most.

**14. Remediation stays proof-gated on the most-attacked surface, and compounds gap 9.** R-17-035 splits containment (fast, monotone, no proof) from remediation (proof-gated) honestly and budgets the window. The cost is unchanged and correctly refused: no unproven hot-patch reaches the radio. But an attacker who can repeatedly force the degraded subset achieves durable partial denial of service *under cover of "containment worked,"* and with availability unbudgeted (gap 8) there is no metric that would ever register this as a failure.

**15. Physical and sub-partition side channels grow more load-bearing as the device model grows more adversarial.** Probe-level power/EM, TEMPEST coupling, RF fingerprinting against link-layer unlinkability, macro-internal and inter-tier coupling, and the event-driven sensor readout's data-dependent timing are all booked (R-17-055, R-17-058/059) and none is closed. The *seized-and-once-unlocked* and *duress* cases put a physical adversary in scope **by design**, which raises the weight of exactly these residuals. R-17-059 states the sharpest version itself: "the design has **no defence-in-depth layer beneath the package boundary** on the memory path … and if it is ever judged wrong there is no second mechanism behind it." That is gap 1 again, at the physical layer, stated by the document against itself.

## Irreducible

**16. Specification error is first-ranked and now countable.** Twenty-three crown jewels, each a small oracle that must be *stated* right and whose wrongness no proof catches. The design became safer by adding correct-by-construction machinery and each piece added an oracle: the delimited-release bound, the ASN.1/UPER codec, the dedup keyed-digest interface, the HAL's DMA postconditions, the IDL initialization annotations, the WCET latency magnitudes, the `Zkt`/`Zvkt` leakage model, the Ztso and static-prediction fetch models, every wire-format mapping, each Lustre program. Unchanged in kind, larger in extent, and now, thanks to the inventory, measurable. That measurement is the register's real contribution, and it should be tracked as a burn-down with a stated direction rather than a status column that only ever grows.

**17. Fabrication trust is evidence-based, not proof-based.** IRIS backside inspection makes fab an obtainable-evidence question rather than an act of faith, and the tier grading is a genuinely strong position: everything that *acts* is imaged, and what is not imaged cannot act. The ceiling stands where R-17-063 puts it: coarser than the smallest features, *evidence not proof*, and a sub-resolution fab adversary stays in scope. Narrowed, not removed.

**18. The bedrock assumptions, the human, and the eUICC stay outside the trusted computer.** MLWE/MSIS and ECDLP/CDH are irreducible axioms; the user is outside every consent theorem (gap 12); the eUICC remains "the one tolerated foreign computer," contained to zero platform authority but an admitted exception to the design's defining claim. None is a defect and all three are permanent: the *no foreign computers / everything verified* framing should keep saying so out loud.

---

*A note on what is not here: the mechanism-level architecture remains settled, internally consistent, and in several places genuinely excellent: the seam registers, the two-class entropy record, the containment/remediation split, and the graded-inspectability position are all better than what they replaced. The previous edition closed by saying the hard part left was building it, stating it correctly, and being honest about the edges. That was too kind on the third count. The specification is scrupulously honest about every residual **individually** and has no discipline anywhere for composing residuals into a position, which is the same failure it identifies in the proofs, applied to itself. It built a seam register for hardware and a seam list for theorems, and needs a third for the honest ceilings, starting with the fail-closed set and the interim isolation posture. Until then the document's candour is real but local, and the reader is left to compose it, which is exactly the job §17 says a reader cannot be trusted to do.*
