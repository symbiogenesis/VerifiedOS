# An outside critique of VerifiedOS

*Commissioned by the project's owner; written by GitHub Copilot on 2026-09-01. Non-normative, and not a corpus document: it sits at the repository root rather than under docs/ because the house rules route working notes outside the gate-audited tree. Method: a read of the README, [spec.md](docs/spec.md), the derived views, [critique.md](docs/critique.md), [architectural-alternatives.md](docs/architectural-alternatives.md), [performance-estimates.md](docs/performance-estimates.md), and [THIRD-PARTY.md](THIRD-PARTY.md), with every specific claim below checked against the files as they stood on that date. Where a point is already booked in the corpus, the entry says so, cites the booking, and argues only what remains.*

*Three owner objections are engaged on the merits first; then the findings the brief did not contain. Verdicts are stated as agree, disagree, or a sharper form. Nothing is argued against an invented opposition; quotes are from the corpus or the brief.*

## Objection 1: the two-class memory hierarchy

**Verdict: the aesthetic instinct is right, the engineering verdict is wrong twice over, and the real defect is a third thing neither the brief nor the pitch states.**

The corpus has already fought this exact battle and the receipt is public. The single-medium dissolution is evaluated by name in the X-SRAM entry of [architectural-alternatives.md](docs/architectural-alternatives.md), which declines the candidate for both classes, declines the single-medium form outright, and then names the aesthetic argument itself: collapsing two latency constants into one is "the simplification a reader notices first," and the entry states it is not the reason. The engineering verdict against all-SRAM runs on two independent axes, not one:

- **Density.** The planar first class is priced at order 1 to 2 GB in the corpus's own capacity budget. No cell choice turns that into the tens of gigabytes the roster's flagship workload wants; higher-transistor-count cells (8T/10T) move density in the wrong direction, and the bitcell entry keeps 6T normative with the stability family as an implementation choice, which is the correct read of that family's literature.
- **Leakage.** This is the axis the brief misses. The oxide gain cell's retention-leakage collapse against 6T (R-15-189l, roughly six orders at retention voltage) is what makes tens of gigabytes idle-capable in a phone envelope at all. An all-SRAM machine at that capacity is not merely large; it has a standby-leakage floor no mobile part ships with, and the composition-time power gating that pays leakage down elsewhere cannot fix a uniformly leaky array. The beautiful machine is not buildable at the target capacity for two physical reasons, and the brief names neither.

So the two-class structure is forced, not chosen, and the corpus knows it sucks in exactly the way the owner means: it pays for the class boundary with placement discipline, a second latency constant, and the two starkest physical residuals in the register (R-17-058e, the refresh pass as a whole-array Hamming-weight broadcast needing no access to provoke; R-17-058f, hours of power-off plaintext). Both exist only because the tier exists. That is the honest price tag to hang next to "conceptual beauty."

What the pitch does not state plainly, and what this review adds:

1. **The bet is a correlation bet.** The flagship workload (local LLM inference) exists on this machine only if the least-evidenced component in the repository qualifies. The canonical critique's work item 30 holds the numbers: published 2T0C evidence sits two to three orders of magnitude short of a repaired megabit-class macro, the largest published array is sixty-four cells, and nothing published carries tag, ECC, or repair at any scale. The class is normative ahead of its own measurement. If the macro fails qualification, the R-15-173a single-planar-tier fallback is not the same product with less memory; it is a different product, and the local-LLM paragraph of the README is the first thing overboard. The pitch and the fallback cannot both be true, and no front-page document says the pitch is conditional on the riskiest component in the design.
2. **The split is welded at the worst possible moment.** The fast/bulk capacity ratio is a die-level decision taken before the macro that decides bulk density is measured; region class membership is then fixed per composition under one placement discipline (R-15-247), and the welded block size spans both classes ([block-geometry-constraint.md](docs/block-geometry-constraint.md)). Every degree of freedom that could correct a wrong ratio is spent before the evidence arrives. The [bank-count exploration contract](docs/bank-count-dse-contract.md) is admirably honest about the same shape (six of seven quantities unknown, an explicit refusal to pick), and the honesty does not unspend the degrees of freedom.

A one-sentence concession in the other direction, because it is the only all-SRAM route to the target capacity and the corpus does not name it: wafer-scale integration. It is a different program (defect tolerance, reticle stitching, power delivery) that the single-die trust story does not cover, and the second-die decline (R-15-162) forecloses the bonded version; naming it once is cheaper than leaving a reader to think the option was unseen.

## Objection 2: the golden model cannot be run as the production binaries

**Verdict: a field ceiling, correctly bounded by this corpus. The disappointment is with the state of the art in 2026, not with this design. One consequence is under-booked, and it is finding F3 below.**

The corpus has already named this thesis and its boundary at the executable-specification entry in [architectural-alternatives.md](docs/architectural-alternatives.md), which collects the five in-house instances, cites the external frontier (WasmRef-Isabelle running inside Wasmtime's CI, SpecTec, CHERIoT-Ibex proving production silicon against Sail-generated RTL), and states the admission rule: an executable specification ships, or generates what ships, exactly when its generator is verified in the one prover or its output is re-checked on the artifact. The boundary is lived, not aspirational: Fiat-Crypto, Vélus, and Narcissus ship as proof transport (R-05-021), and the platform Wasm engine ships as a verified extracted interpreter (R-14-013b).

The strongest existence proof of the owner's preferred world, CakeML's bootstrap, is instructive rather than available: its runtime model (garbage collection over a growable heap) meets a machine whose defining memory discipline deletes the runtime heap, and its throughput would fail this platform's own admission floor. The port is not impossible; it is pointless under the value function. CompCert's price tag is visible in the classical public-key row of [performance-estimates.md](docs/performance-estimates.md), and the design's actual answer (verified generators where they exist, per-install re-checking where they do not, artifact-not-pedigree throughout, with the De Bruijn root booked as an axiom at R-06-014 and a named trigger for upgrading it to a theorem) is the strongest shape the field currently offers. There is nothing defensible to want here that the corpus does not already want.

The under-booked consequence of the model not being the binary is developed as **F3** below.

## Objection 3: not enough third-party code, not enough standards

**Verdict: mis-aimed as stated. The true form of the objection is more serious than the stated form.**

As stated, the objection is half wrong. The tree pins a score of upstreams, and the silicon plan is substantially bought rather than drawn: CHERI-CVA6, Ibex, CHERIoT-Ibex, OpenTitan, the mocha bring-up SoC, the AXI tag controller, the QEMU fork, LLVM, Fiat-Crypto, Cerise, TestRIG, two independent behavioral crypto oracles. [THIRD-PARTY.md](THIRD-PARTY.md) is the most careful license register this reviewer has met in a design repository, down to the pin with no license at all being caught and dispositioned. The bespoke surface is the platform layer: the ISA dialect, the typed assembly language, the multikernel, the image format, the IDL. And the individual dispositions are argued to a standard most projects never reach; several are simply right, memory encryption being the clean case (the entry declines it because a cipher protects an interface, and this machine has no such interface; that is the correct shape of argument).

The true form: **what is missing is not third-party code, it is ratchets.** Every surface that could carry an incremental adoption path is deleted on purpose. No RVA23 alignment (R-15-043 diverges from it by name where RVY conforms). No MMU, so no mainstream kernel can boot even as a stopgap. No on-device ELF, so the world's binary tooling is read-only useless against the image format. No JIT. Wasm exactly once, as a curated guest. A 64+1-bit capability dialect that forks the CHERI mainstream itself: the CHERIoT-Ibex pin takes the conformance methodology and declines the encoding, so "the CHERI ecosystem" and this project end up sharing vocabulary and little else. Each disposition is defensible alone. Their sum is that there is no degrade-to-boring configuration: no boot-something-ordinary while the real stack matures, no ship-the-hardware at a reduced software tier. The hardware premise carries an evidence tier (R-01-002a/b); the software premise admits no interval at all, and R-18-002 forecloses a capability-degraded interim by name. seL4 could host Linux in a VM on its worst day. This machine's worst day runs the differential corpus and nothing else, because the certificate pipeline is on the critical path to every artifact above it: the first demonstration past the corpus waits on the toolchain, the TAL checker, and the CIC kernel, not on the kernel alone. That is the compatibility loss priced honestly: not nostalgia for standards, but critical-path exposure, and it is largely unbooked as such.

Two corollaries deserve one sentence each. The dialect fork has a maintenance tax: every future CHERI-RISC-V conformance suite, optimizer improvement, and debugger feature arrives needing re-homing onto the dialect, and the standing-adoption mechanism (R-15-067k) watches standards-track drift while no instrument watches the upstream *implementation* landscape; the Pydrofoil find and the sail-riscv Lean-extraction emulator were noticed by a person, on no schedule, and for a project whose evidence strategy leans on lineage diversity (R-17-048a) the set of eligible lineages is a watched variable with no watcher. And the one bespoke unit with a live standards track, the matrix extension, is also the one carrying a re-pin obligation (to AME, IME, or VME when one ratifies): that mechanism is good, and the fact that it exists exactly once shows the project knows the pattern; the finding is that the pattern has exactly one instance.

## Findings the brief did not ask for

### F1. The address-space ceiling against the flagship workload's growth curve

The frozen capability format is 64 bits over a 36-bit address space (R-15-002a): 64 GiB, total, constitutional. That space holds the fast class, the bulk decks, every MMIO aperture, and the revocation sidecar. It cannot be revisited without re-opening the capability format, which is the one artifact the TAL, the corpus, the encoders, the proofs, and the LLVM port are all laminated to; the checker's format rules exist precisely to hold it still. Against the ceiling: the flagship workload's weight footprint has grown super-linearly for every year local models have existed. Tens of gigabytes of bulk serves the 7B-to-13B class at 4-to-8-bit weights comfortably, the 70B class at 4 bits marginally (order 35 to 40 GiB of weights, plus KV cache, plus the rest of the roster), and nothing beyond. A conventional laptop answers model growth with a DIMM slot or a larger SKU; a second die is declined (R-15-162); this machine's answer is a new die, and the format's answer is never.

This is a growth bet, not a present defect, and it should be stated as one. No normative text prices the ceiling as a product promise against the named workload. The honest options are: narrow the pitch to the model classes the ceiling holds; carry a growth story and say what it is; or state the ceiling as a deliberate bet that useful local inference plateaus inside 64 GiB. Any of the three is defensible. Silence is the one defective option, because the number is frozen first and measured never.

### F2. The most dynamic workload meets the most static planner

The memory plan is "the complete and final statement of which bytes an admitted composition can reach" (R-15-189b), and the README concedes the general cost in its own words: pools sized for their peak sit mostly empty, the one case a run-time heap wins. Now observe that the inference server's memory shape is the least static in the roster: KV cache grows with context length, batch and speculative shapes vary per request, and model swaps are the ordinary operation of the feature. The workload that justifies the second memory class's existence is the workload that maximizes the doctrine's one conceded waste. The available resolutions are each visible somewhere in the corpus and are named jointly nowhere: over-provision the pool (waste, against the no-wasted-memory doctrine), cap context and batch (a product ceiling on the flagship feature), or recompose per model change (an install ceremony on the hot path of a feature whose appeal is casual experimentation). The bank-count DSE already treats the bank grant, and therefore the admitted token rate, as a composition constant; the same welding applies to capacity *shape*. A single passage naming the joint choice would do. Today the two honest documents (the no-waste concession and the DSE refusal) sit in different files and never meet.

### F2a. Fragmentation by another name: the missing slush fund

The naming is right, with one refinement. What virtual memory quietly provides is not protection but *fungibility*: every free page backs any process, so a machine-level "free" figure is spendable by whichever workload peaks next. Static per-compartment allotments delete exactly that. Memory inside compartment A's grant is structurally unavailable to B while A idles, so the machine's free memory is a sum of unspendable residues, and the amount stranded grows with the spread and non-coincidence of peaks across owners. Classic malloc-style external fragmentation, holes between live objects growing with the spread of sizes, is genuinely deleted with the allocator; what arrives in its place is coarser and permanent for the composition's lifetime. It also recurs one level down: every compartment is now its own memory planner, sizing its own static pools, so the design pushes the fragmentation question onto each author rather than abolishing it.

The README's own defense does not reach this. Offline packing "within a constant factor of the best possible, and exactly optimal for the nested lifetimes this design produces" answers the *placement* question for a known item set. The slush fund's advantage is not placement but statistical multiplexing: demand peaks across owners do not coincide, and no offline packing captures demand that is unknown at composition. The doctrine's concession, "pools sized for their peak sit mostly empty, the one case a run-time heap wins on average," prices the waste *inside* one pool and not the foregone borrowing *between* pools, which is the larger term on any roster whose members' peaks are uncorrelated.

Three second-order effects, one clause each:

- **The failure mode is roster refusal, not pressure.** Admission fits the roster to the budget or refuses it (R-15-171, no swap, no overcommit), so a composition can be rejected not because total demand exceeds total silicon but because no packing across owners, bank grants, and latency classes exists. The binding constraint on what the machine runs may be the packing, and nothing in the corpus prices packings.
- **Quantization stacks with stranding.** Bank grants, granules, the class boundary, and island exclusivity each round away bytes a page table would have kept fungible; the island partitioning tax is already booked as a capacity tax in [performance-estimates.md](docs/performance-estimates.md), and it is the same phenomenon seen from the hardware side.
- **The corpus's own escape hatches confirm the shape.** Transparent variable-rate compression and runtime deduplication, the mechanisms conventional systems use to synthesize a slush fund on fixed hardware, are declined by name in [architectural-alternatives.md](docs/architectural-alternatives.md); static code overlays are deferred as the instruction-side answer, time-multiplexing a fixed code region when a measured image fails the budget. Data has no equivalent mechanism short of recomposition.

**The precedent is the Transputer, and the corpus does not cite it.** [inspirations.md](docs/inspirations.md) and [architectural-alternatives.md](docs/architectural-alternatives.md) between them dispose of scores of ancestors, and neither names the one prior machine that took this exact trade: hardware-scheduled concurrency, no MMU, and a native co-designed language (Occam) that answered the fragmentation this produced by abolishing dynamic allocation outright, every workspace, channel, and array fixed in size at compile time, recursion excluded so that stack depth is provable. The parallel is near-total, down to the stack-depth proof, and it reads in both directions. It confirms the mechanism: under the pure static discipline, runtime fragmentation is not managed but impossible, and this design is stricter than the precedent, the Transputer still carving workspaces at runtime spawn while the composition fixes the whole compartment graph before boot; the Swiss-cheese failure has no referent here, and this finding does not claim one. And it locates the cost exactly where this finding puts it: the Occam discipline's documented price was declaring maximum sizes up front and wasting the margin, and its documented doom was not a memory pathology but the usability paradox, the industry's dynamic workloads arriving faster than the static discipline could absorb them. The ported languages and the Helios OS reintroduced heaps on the MMU-less machine and suffered the fragmentation Occam had sidestepped, which is the same lesson one level down: dynamic pressure bottled inside a boundary whose size somebody still guesses. The twist that makes the precedent a finding rather than a footnote is that this machine's roster contains the paradox. Local model serving with variable context and model swaps, and a browser rendering content of unknown size, are the Helios-shaped workloads, running inside the Occam machine. The Transputer met such demand by choking at runtime; this design refuses at composition, which is earlier, cleaner, and the same wall. An inspirations or alternatives entry owes the lineage a reading: what the Transputer got wrong that this design repairs (composition tooling, admission refusal in place of runtime failure, bank granularity against the contiguous-block demand) and what it got right that this design inherits as a ceiling.

And the fair counterpoint, so the entry is a finding and not a complaint: the slush fund *is* the runtime allocation decision, and restoring it reintroduces allocation-failure paths, cross-compartment memory pressure, and the OOM class the design deletes by construction. The waste is the price of "rejected at build time, so neither is a failure a user can hit," and it is a price worth stating in the same sentence as the optimality claim, denominated in capacity rather than in asymptotic factors. The Transputer record is the existence proof that the price is real: it was paid once in public, and collected in the currency of workloads refused.

### F3. The machine deletes its own instruments

Because the golden model is not the shipped artifact (objection 2), the model and the die are two objects forever, and keeping them honest in the field requires instruments the design deletes as side channels. Pre-silicon, the timing and latency constants are measured against the emulator and the RTL simulation. Post-silicon: the bandwidth and QoS counters are declined as cross-partition activity oracles (R-15-050, R-15-051), the debug and manufacturing interfaces are fused out by the lifecycle, and the crash record is a labeled fault from a closed enumeration, whose shape the README already books as a residual. The result is larger than the booked residual: [performance-estimates.md](docs/performance-estimates.md) is, by construction, unfalsifiable on the hardware it describes, because the hardware carries no instrument that could check it. IRIS images structures, not timings. Every "measured" figure the freeze contract will ever produce is a measurement of generated code and simulated RTL, permanently; whether a customer's unit meets the scheduling constants is a question the design makes unaskable. The same hole swallows fleet operations: a field anomaly presents as identical labeled faults with no telemetry to cluster, so incident response, regression hunting, and update prioritization all run blind by construction. Detection without observability is how real failures become folklore. This wants a booked residual of its own, or a scoped construction that survives the channel audit (a one-time bring-up instrument fused out after characterization, an RoT-attested aggregate with a stated information budget); what it cannot survive is being left as an unnoticed consequence of two separately reasonable deletions.

### F4. The two-machine world

Compilation and proving live off-device by design (R-13-027), and the declined off-device-correspondence entry confirms the split is deliberate and load-bearing. The consequence is unremarked everywhere this review read: the platform is permanently a consumer of artifacts produced by infrastructure it does not contain. The reference form factor is a laptop; one of a laptop's canonical uses, developing software for the machine you are holding, is structurally excluded. This is defensible (the trust-base arithmetic demands it) and it has a resilience edge worth naming: the device's software supply depends on the off-device pipeline's continued existence, and there is no degraded mode in which the machine rebuilds itself from itself. One honest sentence in the goals or non-goals section would do; a reader can currently reach the realization chapter without ever learning that self-hosting is not a milestone but an exclusion.

### F5. The unpriced resource

The README prices trust as the scarce resource and engineering effort as free. The proof program's actual binding input is neither: it is Coq-literate prover and reviewer attention, one of the smallest labor pools in software. The single-prover rule (R-05-011) is grounded on composition across one linked development, and that same ground caps parallelization: a second team in a second prover joins only across a semantics bridge nobody has built, by the rule's own argument. The canonical critique already prices the *reviewer's* attention being spent on the document rather than the machine (its finding 2); this finding is the supply side of the same market. There is also an onboarding tax no estimate cell carries: an independent reviewer must learn the house machinery (conferral, seams, co-read blessings, the rule registry) before auditing anything, and that cost is paid per reviewer, forever. A reviewer-onramp artifact, one document that teaches the machinery to the person the release gate exists to convince, is plausibly the cheapest assurance the project can buy.

### Open questions, cheap to ask

- **Update cadence against counter endurance.** R-10-013 is deliberately a budget over a counter with a rated endurance. Incident response burns generations fastest exactly when headroom is most needed. Is there a rated floor of emergency generations per device lifetime, and what does the device do when the floor is in sight?
- **One human, one powerbox.** The uid/gid deletion is a feature. Its product-shape consequence had no named home: a shared family device, and the fleet case where an organization issues thousands of units and wants attestation-gated policy rather than thousands of consent ceremonies. **Answered by amendment**, at R-17-013g, which states the unit the design actually has and what each of the two cases costs against it.
- ~~**Secure time.** Attestation freshness and certificate validity want a wall clock the root of trust trusts. The always-on timer answers the watchdog. What answers time?~~ **Withdrawn: the question was already answered and this reviewer missed it.** R-09-014 boots the device into an explicit time-unknown state and re-acquires calendar time from the network, with Roughtime the bootstrap source, chosen over bare NTP for being unauthenticated and over NTS for needing a roughly-correct clock to bootstrap its own TLS, so it establishes coarse trusted time from cold with no prior clock; R-09-016 persists the monotonic floor Roughtime refines upward, and R-12-035 disciplines the wall clock from three scope-graded sources cross-checked against each other so a lying or stalled one is caught by disagreement.
- **Model-swap ceremony.** Is a new model a package admission, a recomposition, or a generation? The user-visible latency of "try a new model," the flagship feature's core interaction, is set by whichever it is.

## What this review does not repeat

[docs/critique.md](docs/critique.md) already holds, and this review verified rather than re-argues: the interim assurance gap (its dominant fact), the review gate's semantic residue and the weak-criterion class, the fail-closed EMI denial vector including the emergency-call case, the gain-cell evidence gap and its adoption ahead of measurement (work item 30), the refresh-readout and remanence residuals (R-17-058e/f), the store-buffer gate (work item 10), the degraded-subset rate with no figure, the duress dynamic, and the single-prover tier covering only hardware nobody has designed. Where a sentence above rhymes with one of those, the citation is the argument.

## Bottom line

1. The two-class memory hierarchy is ugly and forced, on two physical axes the brief names neither; the open wounds are the correlation bet (the pitch is conditional on the least-proven component) and the welded ratio (every corrective degree of freedom is spent before measurement), not the aesthetics.
2. The golden model not being the binary is the field's ceiling, and this corpus bounds it as well as anyone; the under-booked cost is F3, a machine that cannot be measured against its own model after tapeout.
3. The third-party objection is mis-aimed at code and correct at ratchets: the design deletes every degrade-to-boring path, which puts the entire certificate pipeline on the critical path to any demonstration, and that exposure is mostly unbooked.
4. Worth a register home: F1 (ceiling against growth), F2 (dynamic workload against static plan), F2a (the missing slush fund, with the Transputer as the uncited precedent), F5 (attention economics). Worth one honest sentence each: F4 and the instrument hole of F3.
